# Make Earth Memories Serve Aetheria

**Ascend should make modern-Earth background matter as a recurring lens for noticing, asking, and interpreting—not as a second progression system that competes with Aetherian learning.** Canon already sets that boundary: otherworlders remember their former lives and their death, the final day remains blurred, their jobs offer small practical perspectives and dialogue, and the new life in Aetheria carries the story. A strong design can make that past emotionally present and occasionally useful while leaving skills, class growth, reliable knowledge, and consequential choices to play in the fantasy world. The draft `races_and_origins.md` repeatedly exceeds that boundary with XP, combat, crafting, economy, trust, class, and unique-skill benefits; it also proposes origins and exclusive arcs that Canon has not approved. For optional community-adjacent features, keep the game wholly playable offline and let platform surfaces add convenience or player expression at the edges. The sensible order is local save safety, a small job-memory prototype within one origin, core controller and screenshot support, then optional cloud and achievements; defer mod hosting and all in-game social services.

## Canon makes Earth a lens, while Aetheria owns progression

Canon says the protagonist remembers their past life and that they died, but not the cause; old-world jobs provide “small practical perspectives and dialogue,” and “the new life in Aetheria carries the story.” It also locks a premium, PC, single-player game and excludes returning to Earth. These are settled project constraints, not research-based proposals. Canon’s field-journal proposal offers an especially good fit: observations and reports can support uncertain bestiary hypotheses and counter-suggestions, while brute force remains viable. Its world-response rules also require plausible knowledge paths and visible receipts for important outcomes. ([Project Ascend Canon](../Canon.md))

The useful distinction is between **memory, knowledge, and capability**. Memory is what the former job lets the character notice or tentatively associate with something. Knowledge is what they confirm through Aetherian evidence, people, books, training, Appraisal, and the field journal. Capability is what they can reliably perform, governed by the normal class, skill, tool, material, and facility systems. An engineer might recognize that a sagging bridge suggests a load or support problem, then propose a cautious test; local materials, craft skill, and a builder still determine whether it can be repaired. A nurse may notice triage priorities, but should not diagnose magical illness or replace healing. Earth experience creates a lead; it does not certify the answer or execute the solution.

The design precedent supports this separation without proving any particular Ascend implementation. BioWare’s pre-release announcement for *Dragon Age: Origins* described six lengthy origins whose choices would affect later dialogue, story, and plot branches; mark those scope claims as **pre-release**, not a post-launch audit. In a later developer interview, lead writer David Gaider said the fixed protagonist and background in *Dragon Age II* gave the team a continuing plot hook, while he felt the *Origins* introductions had limited relevance after their opening. This is a developer’s retrospective rationale, not a measured player verdict. ([EA/BioWare, pre-release announcement](https://news.ea.com/press-releases/press-releases-details/2009/BioWare-Reveals-All-Six-Dragon-Age-Origin-Stories/default.aspx); [Reactor, interview with David Gaider and Mike Laidlaw](https://reactormag.com/dragon-age-ii-an-interview-with-designers-david-gaider-and-mike-laidlaw/)) CD Projekt RED’s post-release patch notes show at least some *Cyberpunk 2077* lifepath recognition persisted in shipped quests, including a Corpo dialogue fix; Bethesda’s post-release *Starfield* guide describes backgrounds affecting dialogue and solutions while skills remain developable. Together, these support selective, recurring recognition—not a new branch for every combination. ([CD Projekt RED, Patch 2.01](https://www.cyberpunk.net/en/news/49231/patch-2-01); [Bethesda, *Starfield* guide](https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/build-your-ideal-starship-captain-in-starfield))

For the knowledge loop itself, CD Projekt RED’s official *Witcher 3* material describes learning monster habitats, attack patterns, and fears before preparing oils and elixirs; Nintendo’s *Fire Emblem: Three Houses* guides describe training students toward classes while allowing skills outside their goals to develop more slowly. Those shipped-game descriptions support investigation followed by preparation, and growth through practice rather than a permanent background lock. They do not document Ascend’s proposed memory/journal states or prove its costs. ([CD Projekt RED, monster knowledge](https://www.thewitcher.com/us/en/news/1018); [CD Projekt RED, *The Witcher 3*](https://www.thewitcher.com/us/en/witcher3); [Nintendo UK, *Fire Emblem: Three Houses* guide](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Fire-Emblem-Three-Houses-1175482.html); [Nintendo Australia, gameplay guide](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/fire-emblem-three-houses-101/))

For Ascend, choose **one primary old-world job** and optionally a few non-mechanical sensory memories, rather than three active jobs. Give each job a small, planned set of high-value recognition moments across the opening, a relevant guild or town scene, and perhaps a companion or side-story beat. At each moment, let the player select whether to voice or pursue the recognition. The line should say “this resembles…” or “you wonder whether…” until evidence confirms it. Other jobs, companions, local experts, exploration, or ordinary skills must provide alternate paths to essential clues and outcomes. This makes the old life legible in a long save while keeping Aetherian choices and growth central.

## The current draft turns jobs into build advantages

The current `06_Races_Origins/races_and_origins.md` is explicitly a v0.2 draft, but its mechanics conflict with Canon’s small-perspective rule or risk making a job the optimal build choice. **Student +XP, engineer crafting precision, cook +1 quality tier, nurse/medic +20% healing, athlete stamina and climbing speed, office-worker trade benefits, farmer faster crops, soldier/guard bonuses, and musician Bard skill +1 all alter durable progression, combat, or the economy.** “Gamer sees some hidden System values” pre-empts knowledge the player should acquire through Appraisal and the story; “otaku trope feats ×1.5” is a direct performance multiplier and pulls the tone toward player-facing trope mastery. These should be removed as old-job perks. If a small numerical nudge is ultimately desired, make it temporary, modest, nonexclusive, and recoverable through ordinary play—but the cleaner recommendation is no persistent job stats.

Other entries grant automatic social or content advantages: faster Academy bonds, farmers trusted instantly, contest wins, unique class skills or skill pools, and exclusive family, companion, faction, or “freedom” arcs. These are not all direct violations of an explicit prohibition, but they exceed the Canon’s small dialogue/practical perspective and are **unapproved scope proposals**. They risk making one background a relationship shortcut or an exclusive solution, and multiply testing and writing across race, origin, class, relationships, and quest state. Keep a job-specific line or alternate approach convergent on shared quest outcomes. Treat genuinely exclusive content as an origin decision, not a hidden reward for selecting a practical Earth occupation.

The draft also bundles together choices that Canon has not settled: three job memories, the final origin roster and prologues, “unique skill pools” and higher rarity odds, and the scale of origin-exclusive content. The creator’s mandatory “one thing you regret” and recurring Earth-memory side story are not explicitly forbidden by Canon, but should remain optional proposals. They risk implying a fixed biography, turning the deliberately blurred final day into a mystery to solve, and creating a new mandatory authoring branch. Likewise, an Earth appearance preview or flashback is compatible only if it supports memory and does not imply a return-to-Earth plot. Canon’s section 6 lists many features as proposed locks requiring the user’s OK; those and the draft itself must not silently become settled facts. ([Project Ascend Canon, §§4, 6](../Canon.md); [current origin draft](../06_Races_Origins/races_and_origins.md))

The systems draft further proposes an “Engineering (Otherworld)” crafting category and recipe discovery by recalling the old world. Canon’s proposed “Otherworld Knowledge economy” can make Earth knowledge relevant, but neither wording should authorize instant invention. Retain these as **research proposals** with a staged rule: memory identifies a possible principle or question; Aetherian research validates whether local physics, magic, organisms, and materials permit it; then skills, facilities, collaborators, and ingredients gate reliable construction. A remembered recipe can be an optional clue or starting hypothesis, not an automatic catalog of Earth products. ([Systems design notes](../12_Systems/systems.md); [Project Ascend Canon, §6](../Canon.md))

## A small knowledge loop gives jobs payoff without power creep

Use a compact, authored loop: **job memory → player-chosen hypothesis → local evidence or expert → confirmed journal entry → ordinary capability or optional approach**. The job determines the first lens, not the truth or reward. The journal distinguishes observed, reported, inferred, and confirmed information; Appraisal and Aetherian practice remain independent ways to learn. A background can make a question available earlier, frame a clue differently, or offer an alternate conversation, but cannot be the only route to a mandatory quest step, recipe, class, relationship, or ending. This complements Canon’s proposed field journal and its uncertainty/brute-force rules rather than duplicating Appraisal or skill progression. ([Project Ascend Canon, §§6, 10; HUD/UI notes](../12_Systems/hud_and_ui.md))

For example, an engineer notices that an old stone lift is binding unevenly and suggests testing its counterweight. The player can inspect wear marks, ask the local mason, use Appraisal, or try the mechanism. Any route can establish the same safe diagnosis. The job memory can offer a hint or reduce a search step, but the player still needs the local tool and relevant skill to repair it; the reward is access, trust earned through action, or a new journal fact—not a permanent crafting-quality bonus. Similarly, a cook may suspect a preservation method, but must identify unfamiliar ingredients and test their properties. These are **Ascend-specific design inferences**, not claims that an external RPG uses these exact rules.

Scope the first implementation as tagged content, not job-specific systemic simulation. Select a short roster of distinct jobs only after listing the scenes each will affect. Track each authored reaction as flavor/recognition, optional clue, or alternate route to a shared outcome. If two jobs produce the same moment, combine them under a practical memory tag; if a job cannot support several memorable scenes, do not promise it as a mechanical choice. Keep dialogue responses player-selectable so the background does not dictate personality. Never gate core progression behind a job. This follows Canon’s tiny-team setting and prior research’s evidence that branching combinations create maintenance and QA burden; public studio examples do not give comparable Ascend production-hour estimates. ([Project Ascend Canon, §§1, 2, 6](../Canon.md); [CD Projekt RED, QA account](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/184/answered-podcast-episode-28-more-than-testing-the-truth-about-game-qa-transcript-included))

## Optional platform features belong outside the offline game

Canon already proposes the correct boundary: local saves first; consider cloud, achievements, and screenshot/photo export as optional platform features; defer Workshop; exclude in-game community services and online-gated play. The rationale is especially strong for a premium RPG whose main story is planned at roughly 200–250 focused hours and whose post-game can continue: platform convenience cannot become the only safety net for a long save. Those playtime figures are project targets, not shipped product measurements. ([Project Ascend Canon, §§1–2, 6](../Canon.md))

Official Steamworks documentation establishes distinct platform behaviors. Steam Cloud synchronizes configured files across a player’s devices and can be disabled globally or for a game; Valve recommends avoiding machine-specific settings and notes that synchronization can affect bandwidth and relaunch/exit time. Steam’s offline achievement cache retains uncommitted stats for a later online session, but that cache is separate from the game’s save system. Remote Play streams a user’s own session to another device; Remote Play Together is a separate feature for inviting others into a local multiplayer game. Valve describes Workshop as an upload, discovery, download, installation, and game-consumption pipeline for user content. These are platform facts, not endorsements or cost estimates for Ascend. ([Steam Cloud documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/cloud); [Steam stats and achievements](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/achievements); [Steam Remote Play](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/remoteplay); [Steam Workshop implementation guide](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/workshop/implementation))

Steam Support warns that playing offline can leave local and cloud saves divergent; when both copies have changed, the client presents a conflict instead of safely choosing for the player. Steam also documents overlay screenshots and Community Hub discussions, guides, and moderation tools. These platform capabilities mean a developer can avoid building a social service, but do not remove the need to handle save recovery or decide how much community support the team can sustain. ([Steam Cloud conflict help](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/68D2-35AB-09A9-7678); [Steam screenshots](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/screenshots); [Steam Community](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/community); [Community moderation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/community_moderation))

The best fit is **offline-complete local saves and recovery first**, then optional Steam Cloud once save format and backup/restore behavior are stable. Preserve a local authoritative save, use versioned/atomic writes and rotating backups, and test offline-to-online conflicts; cloud is portability, not backup or permission to require a network. Add a modest set of achievements near release if their conditions are achievable offline and do not require uploads, account state, or community activity. Support ordinary user-triggered screenshots through the platform overlay; defer bespoke photo mode until its camera and QA cost clearly supports the core art experience. Native controller support and remapping are core usability, while Steam Input community layouts are optional player configurations. Steam Discussions and Guides can be left as platform surfaces without in-game integration; define a limited developer presence so their existence does not imply continuous moderation or support. If Remote Play is advertised, validate readability and controls on each claimed device. Do not enable or advertise Remote Play Together for a single-player-only product.

Defer Workshop and community translation until the team deliberately budgets for tooling, schema/version compatibility, save behavior, testing, permissions, moderation, and support. Larian’s official BG3 FAQ documents that its shipped mod pipeline has version and mod-compatibility concerns; Larian’s 2024 statement about limited long-term mod-support capacity is a dated company-specific constraint, not a general staffing benchmark. The lesson for Ascend is simply that a mod pipeline creates an ongoing product surface. Likewise, Steam’s community and localization tools can collect guides and feedback, but platform availability does not erase curation, spoiler, review, and localization QA work. Keep user uploads, accounts, chat, leaderboards, telemetry, and in-game forums out of the plan unless a clear product need and sustainable owner emerge. ([Larian, official mod support FAQ](https://larian.com/support/faqs/official-mod-support_113); [Larian, 2024 modding announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appids=1086940&enddate=1721124016&feed=steam_community_announcements); [Steam Community](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/community); [Steam localization guidance](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/localization))

## Sequence one contained prototype before expanding scope

Build in this order. First, settle the **old-life contract**: preserve blurred death-cause memory, keep Aetheria as the story, and decide whether the player chooses one job plus optional sensory memories. Second, prototype a single job-aware scene inside one existing origin with two non-job routes to the same clue and outcome; record how memory becomes hypothesis and confirmation. Third, extend that job tag to a few authored scenes and evaluate whether recognition feels meaningful without adding numerical power or creating a mandatory branch. Fourth, validate local save integrity, recovery, and offline launch as core behavior. Fifth, add platform screenshots and achievements only when their conditions are fully local; enable Cloud after conflict and backup flows are proven. Controller/remapping quality belongs in core development; remote streaming claims require device testing. Consider photo mode, Workshop, and community translation only after the offline game and release support capacity are stable.

This should be tested as one vertical slice, not adopted as new canon wholesale. The player should be able to explain what the old job let them notice, what evidence confirmed it, how they could have reached the same outcome another way, and what capability they earned through Aetherian progression. Separately, disconnect platform services and confirm that launch, saves, world state, endings, and post-story play remain complete. Keep the existing world-memory research’s principle of legible provenance and visible consequence receipts, but do not add another broad simulation layer just to make the job feel present. ([Project Ascend Canon, §§5–6](../Canon.md); [World memory and agency report](World%20memory%20and%20agency.md); [Open world RPG systems 2 report](Open%20world%20RPG%20systems%202.md))

## Conclusion

The old life will feel meaningful when it changes the protagonist’s first question, not the game’s answer key. A small set of authored recognitions can make work and memory emotionally present while Appraisal, evidence, teachers, practice, and class systems keep Aetheria’s knowledge and power earned. This also gives the team a clean content rule: jobs can open a thought or an alternate approach, never a permanent advantage or the only door.

The platform boundary follows the same principle. Keep authored play and save ownership local; use cloud, achievements, screenshots, guides, and perhaps remote streaming only as removable conveniences. A stable one-region prototype and dependable save recovery should decide whether any additional feature earns its place, rather than the draft’s current breadth setting the production commitment.

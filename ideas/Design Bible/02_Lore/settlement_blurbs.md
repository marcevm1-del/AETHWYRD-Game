# Settlement blurbs (DRAFT)

_Wave 4 of `13_Production/world_upgrade_plan.md` · narrative-writer draft · spoiler-free · **Canon.md wins on any conflict**._

What a traveller sees and feels on arriving at each of the 51 settlements in `Data/World/physical_map.json`. Names are the exact `name` strings from that file (several are lowercase because they name a group of places, not one town). Each blurb is 2-3 sentences: setting, livelihood, one sensory detail, one hook. Elevation and slope come from the physical map, and the blurb respects them.

Marking: lines ending **(new detail)** add something the gazetteer, `regions.json` or the lore files do not already say. They are modest and need review before anyone treats them as fact. Everything else restates or lightly extends existing region lore. Canon questions are listed at the end, not decided here.

## Aldmere (reg_aldmere)

**Aldmere** _(capital, 279 m)_
The royal city sits on its granite hill with the river bending round three sides, and from the wheat fields below you hear its bells long before the walls fill the sky. Banks, guild halls and every people on the continent crowd its streets, and the air smells of fresh bread and wet stone. Ask a guide about the Hall of Heroes and watch how quickly they look at the floor.

**Ashby** _(town, 284 m)_
A walled market town on the last gentle rise before the barrow-hills, with its gates shut by day and the wall-walk manned long before dusk. Farmers and carters still trade here, but they pack up early, and the market ends in a hush instead of a bell. (new detail) Every week the watch is the first to see who the barrows send out, and they will tell you if you buy them a drink.

**Greyhollow Village** _(village, 315 m)_
Low farmhouses stand among grassy mounds, and half the doors have been left to the nettles by families who moved away. Those who stayed keep their lanterns lit and their children in by dusk, out of stubbornness more than hope. (new detail) At harvest-end they burn a straw knight in the lane, and the smoke drifts toward the open barrow doors on the hill. (new detail)

**Royal Valley farms** _(farmland, 265 m)_
A patchwork of manors, mills and orchards runs along the slow river, flat and rich after the spring floods leave their silt. The tenants live by wheat, apples and wool, and every farmhouse has a sword buried under its doorstep and a story about why. Some of the outer farms are emptying now, and the ones that remain would pay well for a night guard. (new detail)

## Whisperwood (reg_whisperwood)

**Brindlewick** _(village, 289 m)_
A timber village of woodsmoke and creaking signboards, where a great many adventurers open their eyes for the first time in Aethwyrd. Woodcutters, charcoal burners and beekeepers make their living here, and honey on black bread is the smell of the morning. The Sleeping Griffon's loft beams are covered in rookies' carved names, and there is always room for one more.

**Millbrook** _(town, 328 m)_
The waterwheel never stops turning, and the whole town hums with it: flour dust, wet wood, the cellar-cold smell of cheese. Millers, weavers and a few retired adventurers run the place through a council that argues about everything and agrees on very little. A miller swears the wheel turns backwards on nights the goblins come, and he would like someone to check.

## Greymoor (reg_greymoor)

**Greymoor stilt-village** _(village, 275 m)_
Ironwood stilts lift the houses out of the fog, and the walkways between them are slick, narrow and always dripping. Fishers and eel-smokers live here, and the smoke is the only thing that carries any sound, because the fog swallows everything else. (new detail) The villagers still keep the sunk abbey's feast days, and they keep them quietly.

**the Walking Hut market** _(market, 257 m)_
A trader's hut on great bird legs strides through the reeds at a patient pace, with lanterns swinging from its eaves. Where it stops, the fen folk gather with eel, peat, salt and gossip, and the market is over when it stands up again. (new detail) Nobody will say who owns it, and the stall-keepers answer only "the hut decides." (new detail)

## Sylvaran (reg_sylvaran)

**Canopy City Lothaniel** _(city, 334 m)_
You climb into this city by living stair and stay on bridges grown from living wood, with a hundred feet of green light between you and the ground. (new detail) Elven court officials, weavers and singers live in halls shaped by patient gardeners instead of builders. (new detail) Everything creaks softly, and now and then a faint sweetness drifts up from below that nobody wants to talk about.

**Moonwell hamlets** _(hamlet, 320 m)_
Small wood-elf villages cluster around pools that glow pale at night, with lanterns hung low to match them. (new detail) The people tend the wells, gather glowcap and keep a quiet watch, and they are the first to feel the blight spreading from below. (new detail) Their water still tastes cold and clean; the hook is how long that will last. (new detail) A forest lake near the hamlets is mentioned in the lore but has no basin on the physical map yet (see Canon questions).

## Mossdeep (reg_mossdeep)

**Thimblefolk Mushroom Town** _(town, 307 m)_
Hollowed toadstools and grass bridges, all about knee-high to a human, with warm wet moss underfoot and the drone of bees loud as a mill. The Thimblefolk farm honey and mushrooms and are brisk and polite to the giants who visit. (new detail) Each evening someone sets a thimble of milk on the sill for a giant who has not been seen in generations.

## Lirian (reg_lirian)

**Lirian canal cities** _(city, 281 m)_
Island cities joined by bridges and canals, lit by lanterns at dusk, with the doge's palace at the centre and boats everywhere. Boatmen, glassmakers, bankers and bell-ringers keep the place alive, and the smell is salt, lamp oil and sugared almonds. (new detail) At dusk a song rises from the water that nobody can quite place, and the boatmen tell you not to follow it. (Note: the physical map places Lirian inland, so "salt" is a loose sense, see Canon questions.)

## Fanghold (reg_fanghold)

**Fanghold** _(fortress, 287 m)_
The only well for a week's ride sits under this stone fortress, and a market town has grown around its walls with the wind combing through every street. Herders, horse-traders and well-guards live here, and the air carries horse sweat and wild thyme. (new detail) The Howling Hearth keeps its fire all night for riders coming in off the steppe. (new detail)

**nomad yurt camps** _(camp, 285 m)_
Felt yurts stand in loose rings on the open grass, and the smoke of dung fires leans with the wind. The clans herd horses and goats, trade with Fanghold and keep the water-peace that their grandparents swore. (new detail) A camp that was here yesterday may be gone by tomorrow, so ask where the herds are going, not where the camp is. (new detail)

## Iron Hold (reg_iron_hold)

**Karak Dûn** _(fortress, 2,596 m)_
The dwarven hold is carved into the mountain's heart and entered through the Khazdûr Gate, and the climb to it is long, cold and loud with hammer-song. Smiths, miners and stone-wardens live and work here, and the air smells of hot iron and coal smoke. (new detail) The deepest shaft was sealed after something answered the hammers from below, and the seal is warm to the touch.

**Ashen Mines camps** _(camp, 1,819 m)_
Plank barracks and ore sheds cling to steep seams high on the eastern slopes, linked by cable-lines and narrow ledges. (new detail) Dwarves and hired labourers live here under the army that holds the seams now, and the dust tastes of cinder and old fear. Families still hide small things in the rock, tools and keepsakes mostly, waiting for the day the mines are theirs again. (new detail)

## Moraveil (reg_moraveil)

**Moraveil goblin village** _(village, 287 m)_
Caves and ladders climb a cliff above the river, with rope walkways and painted doors up and down the face. (new detail) The goblins here trade mushrooms, cave-salt and mended tools, and the smell is stew and wet stone. (new detail) A child's drawing on one doorframe shows a flooded cave and two figures holding hands, and the neighbours will tell you the story if you sit down. (new detail)

**Moraveil market town** _(town, 298 m)_
A busy human market town on the river, with two languages shouting over the stalls and bridges full of carts. Farmers, millers and traders live here, and every stall has a small goblin charm on a nail "for luck." (new detail) The Truce Bridge joins it to the cliff village, and everyone on both sides knows how fragile it is.

## Strife Marches (reg_strife_marches)

**border baronies** _(fort, 319 m)_
Small fortified towns and manors sit on every rise, each flying the banner of a baron who has a treaty that proves he is right. Farmers pay tolls to whichever baron's soldiers reach them first, and the smell is trampled wheat and old smoke. (new detail) Travellers are asked which treaty they carry, and it is wise to have an answer ready. (new detail)

**mercenary forts** _(fort, 339 m)_
Timber and stone forts where sellsword companies wait for the next contract, with practice yards, gambling tables and marching songs in three languages. Cooks, farriers and scribes make a better living here than most of the soldiers, and the crows watch from every wall. (new detail) Companies hire out to the highest bidder, but the better ones keep a quiet list of work they will refuse. (new detail)

## Velmark (reg_velmark)

**Velmark ducal towns** _(town, 282 m)_
White stone towns of markets, banks and courts, each with its own duke, its own bell tower and its own opinions about the neighbours' tolls. The clerks and masons are brisk and proud, the air smells of ledger ink and cut stone, and the bells strike on the hour. Under the foundation stones there is a slow grinding that the locals have learned to talk over.

## Hoshizora (reg_hoshizora)

**Hoshizora City** _(city, 798 m; on a lake cell)_
Red bridges arch over the river mouth, with the castle and temples above them and the water bright with lanterns at dusk. Priests, boatmen and festival-makers keep it going, and the air carries cedar incense and the sound of temple bells. (new detail) Spirits are given seats at the festivals, and at the Ryokan Moon-on-Water the empty cushion by the window is never taken. (new detail)

**Bamboo Sea villages** _(village, 1,723 m)_
Villages perch on steep slopes among bamboo that rustles like surf, with paths of stone steps and lashed bridges between the roofs. (new detail) The people cut bamboo, make paper and tend small shrines, and the green light is cool and moving. (new detail) Kurogane's masks reached these villages first, and the villagers watch for any stranger who offers a mask as a gift. (new detail)

## Kitsune Isles (reg_kitsune_isles)

**Fox Shrine Isle** _(shrine, 130 m)_
Steps climb from a small harbour to a great fox shrine with nine tails of red cloth fluttering on the roof, and mist and pine smoke hang over everything. (new detail) Shrine keepers, fishers and tea-sellers live here, and the Ninth Tail Teahouse serves tea that is just a little too sweet. (new detail) Each island holds a promise between a fox and a fisherman, and one of them was broken.

**spirit inns** _(inn, 161 m)_
Lantern-lit inns scattered across the isles, each run by a spirit host whose ears do not always match their face. Humans are guests, and the house rules are written on paper strips that nobody quite remembers agreeing to. (new detail) Eat what you are offered, pay what you owe, and never ask what the soup is made of. (new detail)

## Frostveil (reg_frostveil)

**Skjaldheim** _(fortress, 518 m)_
The great hold stands at the head of the longest fjord, black stone against white water, with smoke streaming sideways in the wind. (new detail) Skalds, smiths and boatwrights live here, and in the long dark winter the singing carries from hall to hall. The Last Fire is never allowed to go out, and tending it is an honour that families wait years for. (new detail)

**longhouse steads** _(farmstead, 953 m)_
Long turf-roofed houses hug the steep fjord walls, with fish racks, boats hauled up the shingle and goats on the grass roofs. (new detail) Clan farmers fish, herd and cure meat, and every stead smells of woodsmoke and salted fish. The cold comes a little farther down the slope each winter, and the old people mark it with notches on the doorposts. (new detail)

**sect foothill towns** _(town, 1,243 m)_
Towns sit at the feet of the sect mountains, where the stairs begin, with disciples buying tea and rope and villagers selling them at fair prices. The air carries pine, mist and incense, and training swords clash somewhere above in the dawn. The Wandering Dragon Inn at the Jade Crossroads is neutral ground, and every sect sends someone to listen at its tables. (new detail)

**Haneul gate-towns** _(town, 1,249 m)_
Terraced towns climb the pass in steps, each behind its own gate, with the capital at the top and the Cloud Gate Guesthouse facing the sky. Students, clerks and soldiers fill the streets, and the day is full of drums on the wall and the clack of examination brushes. ## Whitewater Reach (reg_whitewater_reach)

**ice-fishing villages** _(village, 490 m)_
Huts on runners move between the rocks and the ice as the seasons turn, and the sea booms when the floes crack. Fishers drag sleds, tend seal-oil lamps and mend nets in mittens, and the smell is salt, fish oil and burning blubber. (new detail) A fisher is married to the sea before anyone else, and the vow is spoken at a rock at the edge of the shore.

## Sunscar (reg_sunscar)

**Oasis of Qadira** _(town, 342 m)_
The green of seven wells and palm groves is visible long before you reach it, and when you do the heat gives way to cool water and shade. Water-keepers, date farmers and caravaneers live here, and the Oasis Caravanserai is crowded at dusk. A keeper can recite the shoreline of every year back to the first well, and the shoreline is shrinking.

## Goldmere Coast (reg_goldmere_coast)

**Port Goldmere** _(port, 308 m)_
The deep harbour is ringed by houses built of old ship-timber, and half the beams still carry the shape of a hull or a name. (new detail) Salvagers, ropers and tavern-keepers live off what the wreck-tide brings in, and the harbour smells of salt, tar and rum. The Drowned Mermaid sells the best stories on the coast, and some of them are even true. (new detail)

**pirate stilt-towns** _(town, 273 m)_
Towns on stilts stand out on the reefs, joined by rope bridges that sway underfoot, with captains elected by a show of hands. (new detail) The people live by what Port Goldmere misses, and warm water laps at the planks while the timber creaks. Votes are loud and cheerful, and a captain who has lost one will often sail on the same ship under a different flag. (new detail)

## Zarvan (reg_zarvan)

**Zarvan garden cities** _(city, 2,583 m)_
Terraces of roses and pomegranates climb the mountain in stacked gardens, with fountains in every court and aqueducts running overhead. Scholars, gardeners and water-masters live here, poetry is recited at the springs, and the dry mountain wind smells of saffron. Everything depends on the water, and the garden treaties were signed in it.

## Zaruma (reg_zaruma)

**Zaruma gold-road towns** _(town, 277 m)_
Mud-brick towers rise beside deep wells along the gold road, with libraries on upper floors and markets in the shade below. Traders, griots and caravan guards live here, and the air is red dust, spice and the jingle of harness bells. A baobab in the square holds the town's stories, and griots are paid to say which one is true. (new detail)

## Dunewhale Drift (reg_dunewhale_drift)

**the town on Thalassa's back** _(town, 55 m)_
Rope, timber and canvas are lashed to the hide of a sand-whale as big as a hill, and the streets sway slowly as she swims. Riders, rope-menders and cooks live aboard, and the whale's song comes up through the floorboards. The Whale's Belly serves the hot meals, and the riders' calendar of her songs has been getting shorter.

## Xochitl Mire (reg_xochitl_mire)

**Xochitl river-cities** _(city, 235 m)_
Reed and carved stone rise from islands in the lagoons, linked by canoe-roads and bright with flower markets. Canoe-makers, flower-growers and ball-court players keep the cities busy, and the smell is chocolate, chilli and wet green. The old river-kings fed the swamp so it would not feed on them, and a few families still leave offerings on the steps. (new detail)

**Lumbaya temple villages** _(village, 182 m)_
Villages are built in the shade of old temples, with ruined steps climbing into the leaves and roofs of thatch over stone. Fruit-growers, drummers and guides live here, and the air is hot, wet and sweet with overripe fruit. At dusk you hear footsteps behind you that stop when you do, and the locals have a polite way of ignoring them. (new detail)

**Stormreach cliff-towns** _(town, 2,712 m)_
Towns are bolted to the cliffs with iron, and crowned with lightning rods that hum in the weather. Rod-keepers, rope-makers and storm-watchers live in them, and the air is spray, ozone and the cry of seabirds. Every house has a rope to the next one, and the children learn to walk the ropes before they learn to read. ## Ashira Vale (reg_ashira_vale)

**Ashira** _(town, 339 m)_
A warm, welcoming town of bridges and gardens on the river, with laughter from every doorway and fresh bread on every sill. Gardeners, bakers and bridge-wardens live here, the birdsong goes on all day, and everyone greets you by name before you have given it. It is the kindest place in the world, and it is hard to say why nobody ever seems to leave.

## Dragon Spine (reg_dragon_spine)

**dragon-bone monasteries** _(monastery, 1,403 m)_
Monasteries are built into the bones of an old dragon along the Bone Road, with the Skull at the head and warm rock underfoot. Monks keep lamps, chant lullabies in deep voices and tend gardens on the warm slopes, and the air is sulphur and incense. They pray that the mountain stays asleep, and they take turns keeping watch. ## Heavenreach (reg_heavenreach)

**Heavenfolk eyries** _(eyrie, 1,086 m)_
Homes are carved into south-facing cliffs, and the Long Stair climbs through thin cold wind past ledges where wings can open. The winged Heavenfolk fly messages, mend rope-stairs and keep roc-watch, and ice crackles on the stone in the morning. (new detail) Walkers are welcome, but visitors who cannot fly are asked to leave their lantern at the lowest ledge, which is where the stair starts. (new detail)

## Vharlen (reg_vharlen)

**Vharlen frontier legions** _(fort, 322 m)_
Fortress plains of garrison towns, legion camps and the imperial capital behind them, all facing the demon frontier. Every citizen drills, every child knows the alarm horn, and the air smells of polished steel, horse sweat and wax. The pledge that held the line the last time is carved under the barracks, and the old soldiers read it aloud on certain nights. (new detail)

## Gearholm (reg_gearholm)

**Gearholm Undercity** _(city, 1,152 m)_
Tunnels, boilers and slums sit beneath the clockwork mountain, and the steam and the poor collect there together. Stokers, mechanics and scrap-pickers live here and keep the Great Engines running, and the clank and whir never stops. The founders lost the engines' instructions within a generation, and the labourers keep the machines going by feel. (new detail)

## Cinderhole (reg_cinderhole)

**Cinderhole** _(city, 1,250 m)_
The city is built into the walls of a smoking crater and the caves behind them, and it is never dark and never clean. Miners, mask-makers and traders work in the smoke, which stings the eyes and hangs in the streets. Everyone wears a mask, and the best of them are family heirlooms. (new detail)

## Bastion (reg_bastion)

**Bastion outer ring** _(village, 415 m)_
The outermost ring of walls, with villages and fields on the open steppe in front of the rest of the city. Masons, farmers and wall-wardens live there, the hammers ring from dawn, and war drums sound in the distance. When the horde breaks this ring the city builds another, and the people of the outer ring know that they are the ones standing in front of it.

The last glades of the Summer Court are warm and golden, with music that never quite stops and light that is always about to change. ## Apu Highlands (reg_apu_highlands)

**terrace cities** _(city, 2,977 m)_
Cities of fitted stone climb the terraces, linked by stone roads and relay huts, with llama bells ringing along every stair. Cord-keepers, farmers and stone-masons live here, the air is thin and cold, and the smell is woodsmoke, maize beer and roasted potatoes. ## Lustrine Shores (reg_lustrine_shores)

**Lustrine glass beaches** _(village, 279 m)_
Villages sit on the dunes above beaches of moon-glass, where the tide leaves light behind it and a red glow lies on the horizon. Fishers sing slow sad songs, glass-gatherers work the flats, and the lamp market glitters at dusk. The glass began washing in after the red moon rose, and the songs changed key that same year.

## Quaking Lands (reg_quaking_lands)

**Stiller spring-towns** _(town, 295 m)_
Wood and rope houses are built around the still springs, the only places where the ground does not tremble, and they creak softly even in calm weather. The Stillers read tremors the way sailors read weather, and they know when to pull the ropes tight. (new detail) Their oldest maps are all wrong now, and the map-keepers spend their evenings redrawing them. (new detail)

## Canon and lore questions (for the user; none decided here)

1. **Brindlewick's region.** Canon §6 already asks whether Brindlewick belongs to Whisperwood (regions.json, atlas, this file) or to Aldmere (world.md, most Ring 0 quests). Blurbs here follow regions.json.
2. **Moraveil inn.** `Data/World/inns.json` puts The Friendly Goblin in the market town; the gazetteer and `physical_map.json` put it in the goblin village. Which settlement owns the inn?
3. **Lirian and Stormreach coasts.** Both are tagged coast but landlocked in the atlas (Canon §6, "Region data vs the relief"). The Lirian and Stormreach blurbs keep the lore wording; they need rewording or a moved anchor once decided.
4. **Moonwell Lake.** The Sylvaran lore names it but the relief has no basin. Is it hand-placed?
5. **Settlements in lakes.** Hoshizora City and the Summer Court glades fall on lake cells. Are they lakeside towns (move the pad) or lake-island towns (keep the water)?
6. **Steep settlements.** Haneul gate-towns (123%), Bamboo Sea villages (199%), Gearholm Undercity (99%), Cinderhole (73%) and Bastion outer ring (65%) are on steep ground. The blurbs treat them as stepped or terraced; for Bastion, which lore calls an open steppe, that is a mismatch. Should the lore or the placement change?
7. **Elevation of the Kitsune Isles, Skjaldheim and Dunewhale.** Fox Shrine Isle (130 m, 17%) and the spirit inns are modest; Skjaldheim is 518 m on a 32% slope; the town on Thalassa's back is 55 m. Confirm each fits the shoreline or dune the lore wants.
8. **Vharlen frontier legions kind.** Listed as `fort`, but the entry also covers the imperial capital. Should the capital be its own entry?
9. **Heavenfolk eyries and "visitors who cannot fly."** The blurb assumes a staircase for walkers (the Long Stair). Canon bars the player from flying mounts, but not from visiting. Confirm the stair is the intended route.
10. **Spoiler boundary.** Ashira and Brindlewick blurbs avoid the secrets in Canon §3 and the Gentle Warden's nature; the "nobody ever seems to leave" line is a hint only. Confirm that is within the spoiler policy.

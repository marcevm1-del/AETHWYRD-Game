# 04c — Monster Evolution (Reborn Monster Origin) (v0.2)

> *"You died. You woke up as something small, wet, and very hungry. The System said: **Congratulations.**"*

## 1. How It Works
- You start as one of **6 starter monsters**, deep inside a dungeon (the **Hollow Nursery**, under Whisperwood).
- **No classes.** You **evolve** when you hit the level cap of your current form (cap 10/20/30/... by stage).
- **Evolution choice** depends on **Evolution Factors**:
  - **Diet:** what you eat (*Predation*). Every devoured monster gives "essence" of its element/family.
  - **Deeds:** kills, stealth, mercy, alliances.
  - **Skills:** which skills you trained highest.
  - **Environment:** where you evolve (volcano = fire branch, moonlit shrine = holy branch).
- **Humanoid Form** unlocks at stage 3 (C-equivalent), so you can enter towns. The guild registers you as F-Rank and you climb the ladder too.
- **Hunted status:** before humanoid form, adventurers attack on sight. NPC reactions change once you have a name and reputation.
- **Monster Rank = Adventurer Rank** for unlocks (evolution stage 1 = F/E, 2 = D/C, 3 = B/A, 4 = S/SS, 5 = SSS).

## 4. Monster-Origin Exclusive Content
- **The Hollow Nursery** prologue dungeon (survival, hunting, hiding from adventurers).
- **The Monster Council**: an underground parliament of intelligent monsters; a faction only you can join.
- **"Name-Giving"** trope: a strong being (e.g. Nyx the dragon) gives you a name, which triggers a mega-evolution.
- **Rival:** an adventurer party that "keeps almost killing you." They become your first human friends, or your first human meal.

## 5. Origin Promise and Play Rhythm (expanded proposal)

This origin is a different **way into the same world**, not a second game that ignores its geography, factions or consequences. Its opening promise is intimate and physical: wake small, learn what the body can do, find food and shelter, then decide what kind of creature you want to become. Curiosity and restraint matter as much as appetite.

### First-hour progression

1. **Wake:** learn movement, camera, a basic attack and one defensive response in a safe nursery pocket. The System gives concise prompts, then gets out of the way.
2. **Read the nursery:** tracks, shed materials, sounds and nearby creatures reveal where food and danger are. Watch, sneak, forage, fight or retreat.
3. **Choose a first meal:** eating is intentional, with a visible essence preview. The player can take a small, safe benefit or risk stronger prey for a better chance at a skill trace.
4. **Meet a pressure:** an adventurer patrol or territorial creature demonstrates that the outside world has its own rules. Multiple exits exist; a new player is never forced to win a boss-level fight to continue.
5. **Earn or claim a name:** identity becomes a relationship and social mechanic. Naming does not instantly erase fear or suspicion; it gives characters a way to address the player and creates a record of trust.
6. **Evolve:** a short scene shows the body changing and the new capabilities in play. Preview the choice; any reversibility must be clearly described and deliberately approved.

The first hour should end with one personal victory, one unanswered question and a clear next destination. It should not require grinding to a level cap before the player sees the origin’s defining fantasy.

### Evolution as a build decision

Evolution should be a moment of authorship, not a hidden quiz. Before the choice, show available forms and silhouettes; skills and resistances likely to carry forward; strengths, vulnerabilities and social consequences; changes to gear, party access and town entry; and the requirements already met. Preserve discovery through clues before the choice, then let the journal explain which deeds, diet or environment influenced the result. An imperfect threshold should still produce a viable form.

Each branch needs a unique gameplay verb, a meaningful tradeoff and a way to contribute outside combat. Examples already implied by the roster include absorbing a skill, binding with thread, recovering through bone, coordinating a tribe, changing range through breath, or restoring health in sunlight. Keep the starter’s silhouette and movement identity readable across forms.

### Predation and skill traces

Predation should feel like an uncanny survival skill with limits, not a free ability vending machine. Devouring asks the player to choose between recovery, gathering a material, taking an essence trace or leaving the creature alone. The journal records what was learned and why a trace was compatible.

- Common prey can provide modest, predictable essence or food.
- Rare skills need a legible source, a meaningful chance or a story condition, and an alternate path so bad luck does not block progression.
- A creature’s intelligence and community affect the moral framing. A hostile animal hunt, self-defense and consuming a sentient person are not treated as the same interaction.
- Predation cannot copy unique story powers, erase another character’s identity or bypass rank gates without an authored consequence.
- The default route should not force graphic consumption; optional settings may soften the presentation without deleting core progression.

### Body, equipment and control

Each evolution changes readable body affordances such as reach, turning speed, jump, climbing, burrowing or gliding. Keep core controls consistent. A large form may gain reach and commitment; a small form may trade damage for evasion and access to narrow routes. Never remove a basic action without a clear replacement.

Gear must account for non-humanoid anatomy. Form-specific equipment categories can map onto familiar item power budgets. Visual growth should not make equipment invisible: show it on the body, as a clear aura or in the Status display.

### Town acceptance and social trust

Humanoid appearance is not a universal “all forgiven” switch. Model recognition in a few authored states: **unknown threat**, **observed but tolerated**, **known individual**, **trusted ally**. Settlements and factions can begin differently, and a companion or guild sponsor can help the player cross a boundary through demonstrated behavior.

Tell the player why a gate is closed: visible form, witnessed harm, missing papers, siege or local law. Offer alternatives such as a companion conversation, a supported disguise, a side route or earning a sponsor. This keeps the origin about choices instead of invisible walls.

### Convergence with the main world

The origin joins the common story when the player understands a shared objective and the world has a plausible way to recognize their agency. Geography, calendar, guild rank rules, companion mortality settings, quest outcomes, Tyrant states and ending eligibility should converge. Origin-exclusive dialogue can acknowledge the different start without multiplying every later quest into a separate campaign.

The same region map and persistent state rules apply. If the player liberates a region, its defense cycle ends and residents respond. The transformation origin does not silently waive the no-flying-mount rule: personal flight is only available when the form’s ability and progression explicitly unlock it.

### Accessibility, difficulty and recovery

Small-body vulnerability creates tension without forcing repeated reloads. Early threats telegraph clearly, escape routes remain available, and recovery items are understandable. A lost fight may cost time or a recoverable resource; it should not erase evolution progress on default settings. Optional challenge settings can raise the stakes after the player understands them.

Evolution previews, color-independent telegraphs, remappable controls, adjustable timing assists and non-parry routes apply here as in other origins. Body size must not make critical targets or interactions hard to see; camera distance and aim assistance can adapt to the form.

### Production slice

Do not build six full evolution trees before validating the experience. Prototype one small-bodied starter:

1. One safe nursery pocket, one optional branch and one readable exit.
2. Three behaviors: foraging, an ordinary creature encounter and an avoidable elite.
3. One essence choice with a clear preview and a guaranteed first evolution.
4. One evolution that changes both movement and combat.
5. One social encounter with two outcomes based on observed behavior.
6. A save/reload check proving form, skills, choices and world flags persist.

The test is not “does this contain enough content?” It is: **does surviving as a creature make the player curious about the wider world, and do their choices feel like the reason this monster has become a person?**

## 6. Open Decisions (proposals; do not silently lock)

- Whether Reborn Monster is a full alternate origin at launch or a later content ring.
- Whether the player consumes monster essence only or other remains too; the default should avoid forced graphic consumption.
- Whether branch evolution is permanent, respec-able at a cost or allows a limited number of earned re-evolutions.
- Whether humanoid form is required for guild registration or intelligent non-humanoid adventurers have a separate charter.
- Which origins converge on the main story earliest and which retain exclusive quest chains.

These questions affect scope, rating, progression and story branching. Record a decision in Canon §6 before implementation.

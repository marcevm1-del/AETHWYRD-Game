# Progression Without Repetition Debt

> **Proposal, not canon.** The game retains the level cap, long SSS path, rank trials and other locked facts in Canon. This document explores how to make advancement feel purposeful across that long horizon without requiring repetitive farming.

## Player promise

Every hour should change at least one of these: what the player can do, what they understand, who trusts them, where they can go, what they can express, or what the world remembers. A level number alone is not a satisfying reward. The player should feel stronger because they learned a new rhythm, earned a new option, opened a path, completed a promise or saw a long goal move closer.

## Research-informed direction

Obsidian's *Avowed* developers describe flexible respecialization and upgrades that let players lean into a chosen playstyle, while keeping attributes available across builds rather than imposing narrow weapon gates. Their interview also describes curiosity finding resources and unique equipment that support experimentation. ([Xbox Wire, Nov 21 2024](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/11/21/player-choice-avowed-approach-combat/))

Obsidian's *Fable* interview describes supporting both players who follow the main story and players who explore systems, without requiring the latter path for main-story progression. ([Xbox Wire, Jan 22 2026](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/01/22/fable-interview-overview-details-developer-direct-2026/))

**Ascend inference:** long progression should be a portfolio of parallel, mostly optional goals. Main-story access should not require finishing every system. Build experimentation should be possible without fear of permanently ruining a 1,000-hour save, while mastery, collection and rank prestige can provide a much longer tail.

## Five kinds of advancement

### 1. Character growth

Levels and primary stats give a steady, understandable improvement. Each level should provide a small but tangible gain plus periodic choice points. Avoid sudden intervals where the XP bar moves for hours and delivers nothing. The level curve should be tuned against combat time and real-hour session rewards, not only monster kill counts.

### 2. Technique mastery

Skills improve through use, training and quest milestones. Use broad mastery bands—learned, practiced, mastered—rather than per-skill counters that demand hundreds of identical repetitions. A technique can unlock a cancel, alternate follow-up, resource discount or visual flourish after the player demonstrates its intended use in varied combat.

Mastery should not require grinding weak enemies. Accept several evidence sources: successful use against elites, training, skill challenges, story encounters or class mentors. Cap the benefit so one favorite move remains expressive without becoming the only correct move.

### 3. Professional rank

Guild rank measures demonstrated reliability, threat handling, professional trust and access. Rank advancement is an authored review with evidence from several kinds of play: contracts, rescues, investigation, defense, hunts, conduct and exceptional feats. A player who solves problems socially or through preparation should have an alternate path to demonstrate competence where it fits the trial.

Rank is not a level gate. A high-level player may still need to earn a guild's trust; a low-rank exceptional feat can trigger a trial as canon permits. The review should explain the requirements and let the player prepare.

### 4. World knowledge

The field journal, maps, rumors, clan relationships, monster research and local customs increase capability without adding raw stats. Knowledge can reveal better routes, likely monster behavior, available services, negotiation options or safer preparation. It should not silently make basic combat mandatory knowledge-check content.

### 5. Identity and legacy

Titles, outfits, weapon signatures, companion bonds, settlement memories and the Memory Reel express who the player has become. Prefer rewards that show up in the world or change an interaction. Add numerical power only when a specific combat gap needs it.

## The long goal ladder

Break the road to SSS into visible arcs, each with a different kind of payoff:

- **F–E: Survival and belonging.** Learn movement, defense, guild practice, first contract chain and first companion trust.
- **D–C: Craft and competence.** Choose class expression, meet regional cultures, investigate signature ecology, complete first consequential promise.
- **B–A: Influence and responsibility.** Handle multi-party contracts, faction decisions, difficult expeditions and visible regional recovery.
- **S–SS: Expertise and sacrifice.** Face complex encounter rules, late-story dilemmas and the cost of advanced power; preserve optional routes for different builds.
- **SSS: Mastery and legacy.** Complete high-skill trials, deep world research, companion and region milestones, then pursue Transcendence or the hidden long-term secret as canon permits.

The hours are a pacing aspiration, not a requirement to fill the calendar. If an arc takes longer, it should be because the player chose more stories, not because the same contract was repeated until a hidden counter filled.

## Multiple progress tracks without a checklist explosion

The status interface should group progress into four readable panes:

- **Adventurer:** level, class, skills, rank trial and equipment.
- **Field Scholar:** species, regions, clues and ecology understanding.
- **Companion:** bonds, promises and personal quests.
- **World:** siege/liberation, restoration and faction consequences.

Only one or two goals are highlighted by default. The player can pin an objective and choose whether the System gives strong guidance, subtle reminders or silence. The interface celebrates an unexpected completion rather than forcing the player to pre-plan every activity.

## XP and reward quality principles

- Reward challenge completion and exploration discovery above repetitive low-threat kills.
- Diminish XP from trivial enemies when the player substantially outlevels them; keep materials and fun encounters if desired.
- Give partial progress for successful investigation, rescue, defense and nonlethal resolutions where the contract supports them.
- Pay the reward when a quest is completed, not only after returning to a specific desk if the story context does not require it.
- Avoid daily login rewards, expiring battle passes, stamina timers and offline decay; those conflict with a premium single-player game and the user's preference for relaxed session play.
- Make XP gains, rank proof and item rewards legible in a compact debrief that can be skipped.
- Let optional rare drops be exciting, but never make a core skill, class switch or required story item depend on a tiny random chance.

## Skill development alternatives

### Option A: milestone mastery (recommended to prototype)

Skills unlock ranks from authored class milestones, training and quest events. Low grind and easy to communicate; requires writing/implementation of milestones and careful content mapping.

### Option B: use-based mastery with safeguards

Using a technique builds familiarity, but only up to a small threshold and across varied enemy ranks. Grants style variants or slight quality-of-life benefits, never large hidden damage multipliers. More organic, but can favor repetitive play if poorly tuned.

### Option C: mentor challenges

A class mentor gives a short practical test: land a launcher, survive a pressure pattern, use a counter, protect a target or demonstrate control. High character and anime potential; risks becoming repetitive if every skill has a bespoke challenge.

### Option D: hybrid

Base skill levels come from class milestones; one signature mastery challenge unlocks an expressive variant. This limits content cost and gives players a story-worthy mastery moment.

## Respec and class switching

Canon already establishes that respec is available with costs after the early period and that class change does not erase level or learned skills. Build experimentation should still be safe:

- preview new allocation and resulting derived stats before spending;
- show what is refunded and what is retained;
- preserve named builds/loadouts;
- grant a token at class-tier changes as already proposed;
- do not charge for correcting a misread description or broken implementation;
- class switching should require an in-world mentor/trial and cost, but not months of replay;
- communicate that switching changes active skill access while preserving durable investment.

## Catch-up and returner support

A long game needs a gentle way back after a break:

- journal recap lists current leads, recent promises and last major story beat;
- training yard can replay control lessons on demand;
- loadout screen shows recent setup and a safe baseline build;
- quest board labels urgent, time-sensitive and indefinite work honestly;
- no companions become weaker, poorer or angry because real days passed outside the game;
- recap avoids spoilers for undiscovered twists and secret endings.

## Reward cadence hypotheses for playtesting

Do not treat these as locked times. Test with new players and adjust:

- short session: one encounter or discovery plus a visible reward;
- 45–90 minute session: one contract/story beat resolved and one longer goal advanced;
- several-session arc: new region access, skill expression, bond threshold or rank review;
- major act: a meaningful world-state shift and new questions;
- long tail: optional mastery and collectible depth with no mandatory repetition.

The central metric is not XP per minute. Ask the player: what changed for your character, what did you learn, and what do you want to do next?

## Ring 0 prototype

Use only Swordsman, F-rank monsters and the Brindlewick guild path:

1. Level from 1 toward 5 using a mix of kills, contracts, training and discovery.
2. Unlock Slash Chain, Rising Cut and one class technique through readable milestones.
3. Compare a direct monster hunt, evidence-led hunt and optional training challenge.
4. Show rank-proof summary separately from XP and loot.
5. Let the player try a safe alternate allocation through preview and a starter respec token.
6. Return after a simulated long break and receive a non-spoiling recap.

Record how often players repeat an activity only for XP, when they lose sight of the next useful goal, and whether the reward changes their combat options or world understanding.

## Open decisions

- How much power should raw levels contribute relative to equipment and skill?
- Should technique variants be earned by use, mentor challenges or both?
- How many goals should the System surface at once?
- How long should an F-to-E review take in a new player's first hours?
- Which rare rewards are exciting without becoming completion blockers?
- Should Transcendence be mostly combat mastery, world knowledge, narrative choice or a mixture?

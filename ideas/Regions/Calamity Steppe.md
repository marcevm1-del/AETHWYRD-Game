## Overview

The Calamity Steppe is an immense grassland in the far west, the size of a kingdom, with no towns, no roads and no people. It belongs to the monsters, and the monsters here are the size of hills. They graze its grass and hunt one another in herds, and now and then one of them wanders east into the inhabited world and becomes a Calamity. The guild charts their trails like rivers. No Tyrant holds the steppe and no one lives there to be besieged; there is nothing to defend but your own life.

## Geography

The steppe is a vast, rolling plain of tall grass that ripples like the sea, broken by lone hills, dry riverbeds, salt pans and craters left by battles between monsters. The grass grows so fast that trails close behind the herds within days. There are no trees for a hundred miles. In the middle of the steppe stand the Tusks, a ring of colossal bones, the remains of something that died here long ago and that every Calamity seems to visit at least once.

## Ecology

The Calamity Steppe is an ecosystem of giants. Behemoths graze, titanic beasts hunt them and swarms of smaller monsters follow both to feed on the leftovers. The grass is rich in mana, which may be why the monsters grow so large. Birds as big as ships ride the thermals, and burrowing monsters tunnel beneath the plain. When a Calamity is born, a creature grown beyond the steppe's capacity to feed it, it walks east toward the lands of people.

## Peoples

No one lives on the steppe. Expeditions of S-rank adventurers, scholars and guild cartographers cross it in armoured caravans, and a few hermits and madmen have tried to live there. The beastkin clans of Fanghold tell stories of their ancestors who walked the Calamity Steppe and came back changed.

## Power & trade

No power holds the steppe. The guild maintains watchtowers along its eastern edge to track Calamities as they leave it, and issues expedition licences only to the highest ranks.

## Faith & customs

The clans of the eastern steppes believe the Calamity Steppe is where the first monsters were made, and they leave offerings at its edge to keep them there. Guild cartographers have their own superstition: never name a monster on the steppe until you are off it.

## The Tyrant

The Calamity Steppe has no Tyrant and no siege, because there are no settlements to defend. Its monsters are the greatest wild creatures in the world, and some of them are greater than many Tyrants.

## Senses

Endless grass hissing in the wind; the ground trembling under distant footsteps; the smell of crushed herbs and old bone; a sky that feels too big.

## History

- **Now:** The guild's watchtowers report more Calamities leaving the steppe this year than in any year on record, and all of them walking in the same direction.
- **Parents:** Within living memory a Calamity walked out of the steppe and flattened a city before it was killed, and the guild built the watchtowers in response.
- **Grandparents:** In their grandparents' time the steppe's edge was a hundred miles further west.
- **Ancient:** Calamities are born here and wander out. The guild charts their trails like rivers.

## Landmarks

- **The Tusks:** A ring of colossal bones in the middle of the steppe that every Calamity visits.
- **The Watchtowers:** The guild's towers along the eastern edge, tracking Calamities as they leave.
- **The Salt Pans:** White flats where the giants come to lick salt and fight.

## Story hooks

- A watchtower has gone silent, and the last report mentioned a Calamity walking toward it.
- A scholar wants to know why every Calamity visits the Tusks.
- A guild cartographer has named a monster on the steppe, and now it is following her.

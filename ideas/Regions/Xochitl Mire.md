## Overview

Xochitl Mire is a jungle swamp of rivers, lagoons and floating gardens, where river-cities of reed and carved stone rise above the water and are linked by canoe-roads and flower markets. Its people are farmers, traders, astronomers and priests, and they fill their canals with flowers every morning. Xibalbar, the hydra of the mire, has begun hunting along the canoe-roads, and it grows back every head it loses.

## Geography

The mire is a great basin where several rivers spread out into a maze of channels, lagoons and swamp forest. The river-cities stand on islands of rock in the lagoons, and the people have built floating gardens of reed and mud, called chinampas, around them. Canoe-roads, marked by carved posts, link the cities. Deep lagoons in the middle of the mire are said to have no bottom.

## Ecology

The jungle is full of jaguars, caimans, monkeys, parrots, frogs and snakes. The lagoons are full of fish, turtles and water-birds. The floating gardens grow maize, beans, squash, flowers and chillies. Xibalbar lives in the deepest lagoon and hunts everything that swims or floats.

## Peoples

The people of the mire are farmers, fishers, traders, artisans, priests and astronomers. They are hard-working and ceremonial, and every part of life has a ritual. Their markets are among the busiest in the world. Ball-games are a passion, and great matches are played between the cities.

## Power & trade

Each river-city is ruled by a king advised by priests, and the cities form a league that meets to settle disputes. Maize, cacao, feathers, cotton, jade and flowers are traded between the cities and abroad. Cacao beans are used as money.

## Faith & customs

The people honour the day-gods and the river, which they feed with flowers every morning. Their astronomers keep a calendar of the stars as precise as any in the world. In the old days the river-kings fed the swamp with greater offerings at the Feeding Steps.

## The Tyrant

Xibalbar, the Hydra of the Mire, is a vast serpent with many heads that grows two back for every one it loses. It slept for generations, fed by the offerings of the river-kings. Since the rites were abandoned it has woken, and now it hunts.

## Senses

Flowers floating on dark water; drums from the ball-courts; chocolate and chilli; the slow ripple of something very large.

## History

- **Now:** Xibalbar has begun hunting along the canoe-roads, and the cities are cut off from each other. The league is meeting for the first time in a generation.
- **Parents:** Within living memory the old feeding rites at the Feeding Steps were abandoned as barbaric, and the priests who kept them were driven out.
- **Grandparents:** In their grandparents' time the river-kings fed the swamp, and Xibalbar slept.
- **Ancient:** The old river-kings fed the swamp so that it would not feed on them. The tradition lapsed.

## Settlements

- **Xochitl river-cities:** River-cities of reed and carved stone on islands in the lagoons, linked by canoe-roads and flower markets.

## Landmarks

- **The Floating Gardens:** Gardens on rafts of reed and mud around the cities.
- **The Feeding Steps:** A stone stair into the deep lagoon, where the river-kings once made their offerings.
- **The Star Tower:** The astronomers' observatory, the tallest building in the mire.

## Story hooks

- One of the old priests has come back, and he wants to restore the feeding rites; the city wants someone to find out what the rites really were.
- A great ball-game between two cities is threatened by the hydra, and the players want an escort.
- The astronomers have seen something in the stars that they say explains why Xibalbar woke.

> **The Silent Fen.** Stilt-houses and a market hut that walks on its own legs stand in a fog so thick that sound dies in it. The Hollow Abbot leads a choir of silence through the reeds, and wherever it passes the bells stop ringing.
>
> *The abbey sank into the bog a lifetime ago, monks and all. The villagers still keep its feast days, out of habit and out of fear.*

## Overview

Greymoor is a fen country west of Aldmere, a sprawl of black water, reed beds and drowned woodland under a fog that rarely lifts before noon. Its people live on stilts, trade from flat-bottomed punts and buy their medicines from a market hut that walks. It is a strange, close-knit land that keeps its secrets, and the fog keeps them better. Lately the fog has become quieter than it should be, because wherever the Hollow Abbot's choir passes, every sound dies.

## Geography

The moor is the flood basin of three rivers that fail to agree on where they meet. The ground is peat floating on water, and the safe paths shift with the seasons; only the punt-guides know them. Drowned forests of black alder stand in the deeper meres, and the remains of a great abbey lie half sunk in the centre, its bell tower leaning out of the bog like a broken mast. The western edge rises into the Witchwood, a tangle of thorn and yew where the swamp witches live.

## Ecology

The fen is rich in food for anyone who knows where to look: eels, frogs, wildfowl, cranberries and bog-iron. It is also rich in things that eat travellers. Bog hags, leech swarms, giant herons and drowned dead rise from the mires, and the peat preserves anything that falls in, which means the dead of Greymoor are unusually well kept. Will-o-wisps are not monsters here but a kind of lantern-moth that the locals farm in jars.

## Peoples

Greymoorers are small, wiry, patient people who speak softly out of long habit; voices carry over water. They are eel-fishers, peat-cutters, reed-weavers and herbalists, and the witches of the Witchwood are both their healers and their judges. Outsiders find them rude; they find outsiders loud. They are fiercely loyal to each other and to the Walking Hut, whose trader has sold them medicine and news for longer than anyone has been alive.

## Power & trade

Greymoor has no lord. Each stilt-village is run by a moot of elders, and disputes go to the witches, whose word is law because nobody wants to find out what happens if it is not. Aldmere claims the fen on paper and has never collected a tax from it. Trade flows out as eels, reeds, peat and witch-medicine, and flows in as salt, iron and cloth. The Walking Hut is the closest thing the fen has to a capital.

## Faith & customs

Greymoorers keep the feast days of the sunken abbey, though no monk has lived there in a lifetime. They ring hand-bells at dusk to tell the dead which way is home, and they never whistle on the water. Every house keeps a lantern-moth jar burning through the night. On Morrowsday no one cuts peat, because the bog is said to give back what it takes on that day.

## The Tyrant

The Hollow Abbot was the last abbot of Greymoor, and his Choir of Silence is made of his monks. They walk the reeds in a slow procession, singing a hymn that cannot be heard, and every sound within reach of it dies: bells, birds, voices and the heartbeats of anyone who stays too long. The abbot wants silence because something in the bog stirs at noise, and he has decided that the only way to keep it asleep is to silence the whole fen. He is right about the thing in the bog and wrong about everything else.

## Senses

Fog that tastes of iron; eel smoke; the drip of water from reeds; a silence so complete it hums.

## History

- **Now:** The Hollow Abbot's choir walks the reeds and the bells have stopped. Villages that once rang at dusk now sit silent, and those who go out into the fog after a lost sound do not return.
- **Parents:** Within living memory the last monks of the abbey were seen rowing out to the bell tower one night with every bell they owned. The bells were never heard again, and neither were the monks.
- **Grandparents:** In their grandparents' time the abbey stood on dry ground, at the end of a causeway. One wet spring the causeway sank, then the cloister, then the church, over a single month.
- **Ancient:** The witches say the fen was once a lake of clear water, and the abbey was built to hold down something that lived in it.

## Settlements

- **Greymoor stilt-village:** The largest of the fen's villages, built on ironwood stilts around the Crooked Lantern inn.
- **The Walking Hut market:** A trader's hut on great bird legs that walks a slow circuit of the fen; where it stops, there is a market.

## Landmarks

- **The Leaning Tower:** The abbey bell tower, sinking a hand's width each year.
- **The Witchwood:** A thorn forest on the fen's western rim, where the witches meet on the darkest night of each month.
- **The Peat Library:** A cave where the bog has preserved a monastery's books, readable if you dry them carefully.

## Story hooks

- A girl wants her brother's voice back; the choir passed his punt and took it.
- The witches want a bell, any bell, carried to the Leaning Tower and rung at noon.
- The Walking Hut has stopped walking, and no one knows how to ask it why.

## Clans

- **The Bogkin** (Amphibian Clan, led by Choir-Mother Gullagrum): Greymoor's frogfolk live in the deeper meres and trade with the stilt-villages by night. They croak in harmonies that carry under the fog, and the Hollow Abbot's silence is killing their songs.
- **The Shellmoot** (Snail Clan, led by Grandfather Spiraleth): Greymoor's oldest folk, who remember the abbey on dry ground.

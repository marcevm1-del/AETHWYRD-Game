## Overview

Heavenreach is the roof of the world, a range of peaks so tall that their summits stand above the weather. The winged Heavenfolk live there in eyries carved into cliffs that no one without wings can reach. They once flew messages for every king below and were honoured for it; now they keep to themselves and look down on the world with long memories. Above them all rules Aquilon, the Roc Sovereign, who has declared the sky his kingdom and every wing in it his subject.

## Geography

The range rises in a wall of ice and grey granite from green foothill valleys to summits wrapped in permanent snow. The eyries are carved into the south-facing cliffs, where the sun warms the rock, and they are linked by flight paths marked with painted stones. A single stair, the Long Stair, climbs from the valley to the lowest eyrie for the use of wingless visitors; it has eleven thousand steps and no railing. Above the eyries the air is so thin that even the Heavenfolk struggle, and only the rocs fly there easily.

## Ecology

Golden eagles, lammergeiers, mountain goats, snow leopards and pikas live on the slopes, and the valleys below are full of rivers, meadows and herds of yak. Rocs nest on the highest peaks and hunt goats, yak and, when hungry, anything with wings. Since Aquilon's rise the rocs have multiplied, and the Heavenfolk have had to fly in groups for safety.

## Peoples

The Heavenfolk are a people with broad feathered wings and light, hollow-boned bodies. They are proud, formal and slow to trust, with a culture built around flight: their children's first flight is the most important day of their lives, and a Heavenfolk who loses the ability to fly is mourned as if dead. They speak the old courier-cant of the kingdoms below, a language of short, exact sentences. Younger Heavenfolk are restless, curious about the ground and tired of their elders' grudges.

## Power & trade

The Heavenfolk are governed by a council of the eldest flyers, who meet in the highest eyrie that the old can still reach. They trade little with the ground: feathers, falcons, herbs and, rarely, their services as scouts. The valley folk below trade food and cloth up the Long Stair for those goods. Aquilon now demands a tithe of everything that flies, and the council has not yet decided whether to pay.

## Faith & customs

The Heavenfolk honour the day-gods and the open sky, and they believe the Titans of the Sky Archipelago were their ancestors' teachers who gave them the gift of flight. Their dead are laid on high ledges for the birds, so they can fly one last time. Breaking a sworn message, failing to deliver what was entrusted, is their gravest sin.

## The Tyrant

Aquilon, the Roc Sovereign, is a roc of enormous size with feathers like beaten bronze. He has gathered the rocs of the high peaks into a flock that obeys him, and he has declared that everything that flies owes him tribute. He considers the Heavenfolk upstarts who stole the sky from the rocs, and he means to make them his servants. He can be fought only in the air, or by anyone patient enough to climb to his tribute peak.

## Senses

Thin cold wind; the beat of great wings overhead; ice crackling on stone; the cry of a roc echoing between peaks.

## History

- **Now:** Aquilon has declared the sky above Heavenreach his kingdom and demands a tithe of every flight: food, feathers and, lately, children. The council is divided, and young flyers have begun disappearing on solo flights.
- **Parents:** Within living memory the Heavenfolk withdrew from the world below after a king had a messenger executed for the contents of a letter she carried. They have not flown a message for a crown since.
- **Grandparents:** In their grandparents' time the Heavenfolk carried messages for every kingdom, and a Heavenfolk messenger's seal was respected in every court.
- **Ancient:** The Heavenfolk once flew messages for every king below. Their oaths were broken first.

## Settlements

- **Heavenfolk eyries:** Homes carved into the south-facing cliffs, linked by flight paths and reached by the Long Stair.

## Landmarks

- **The Long Stair:** Eleven thousand steps with no railing, the only path for those without wings.
- **The Tribute Peak:** A summit above the eyries where Aquilon collects his tithe.
- **The Ledges of Last Flight:** High ledges where the Heavenfolk lay their dead.

## Story hooks

- A young Heavenfolk wants to fly messages again, and she has chosen a ground-dweller to carry her first one with her.
- Aquilon has taken a child as tribute, and the child's mother wants a climber, not a flyer, to bring him back.
- A merchant in the valley wants a crate carried up the Long Stair, and will not say what is in it.

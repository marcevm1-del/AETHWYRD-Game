## Overview

The Apu Highlands are a range of high green mountains carved by hand, from valley floor to cloud line, into tens of thousands of terraces. Their people farm those terraces, herd llamas along stone roads and keep their history on knotted cords that only a few can still read. Nobody knows who carved the mountains; the highlanders say the mountains carved themselves for their children. The oldest cords record a giant's name, and the giant has woken up.

## Geography

The highlands are steep and immense, their slopes cut into stepped terraces held up by walls of fitted stone so precise that a knife blade cannot slip between the blocks. Rivers fall through deep gorges, crossed by rope bridges that are rewoven every year. Stone roads with thousands of steps link the terrace cities, and relay huts stand along them a day's run apart. Above the terraces rise bare grey peaks capped with snow, and on the tallest stands the Giant's Bed, a flat summit shaped like a sleeping body.

## Ecology

Llamas and alpacas graze the upper terraces and carry loads along the roads, condors ride the thermals, pumas hunt vicuña in the high grass and hummingbirds feed on the terrace flowers. Each terrace makes its own little climate, and the highlanders grow hundreds of kinds of potato, maize, quinoa and beans at different heights. Stone giants sleep in the mountains, and the earth tremors that the highlanders call the mountains turning in their sleep are, the cord-keepers say, exactly that.

## Peoples

The highlanders are farmers, herders, masons, weavers, runners and cord-keepers. They are communal, hard-working and quietly proud, and they measure a person's worth by the walls they have helped mend. Every family owes a share of labour to the community, and work days are feasts. The cord-keepers are a respected, dwindling order, and young highlanders who leave for the coast rarely come back to learn the knots.

## Power & trade

The terrace cities are ruled by a lord in the highest city, advised by the heads of the great families. Labour, not coin, is the main tax, and the roads, bridges and walls are maintained by everyone in turn. Wool, potatoes, dried meat, medicines and fine stonework are traded down the mountains for salt, fish, cotton and metal. The relay runners carry messages across the highlands in a few days.

## Faith & customs

The highlanders honour the day-gods and the mountains themselves, which they call the apus, the lords. Every peak has a shrine where offerings of coca leaf, maize beer and woven cloth are left. A wall that falls is mourned like a person, and rebuilt by the whole community.

## The Tyrant

Ukhu-Tal, Stone Giant of the First Making, is the first stone giant ever made and the mason of the highlands. He carved the first terraces, taught their making to the first people and lay down on the summit to rest when the work was done. He has woken to find the terraces covered in people, his work patched with other stones and his name forgotten. He walks the terraces looking for the walls he made, and he breaks the ones he did not. He does not know whether he is angry or proud, and someone who can read the oldest cords might be able to tell him.

## Senses

Thin cold air and woodsmoke; llama bells on the stone roads; maize beer and roasted potatoes; the deep grinding of stone on stone.

## History

- **Now:** Ukhu-Tal has woken on the Giant's Bed and walks the terraces, and the walls break where he steps. He has not yet harmed anyone on purpose, but the terraces are falling, and the harvest with them.
- **Parents:** Within living memory a great earthquake shook the highlands for three days, and the terraces held; the lowland cities fell.
- **Grandparents:** In their grandparents' time the cord-keepers could still read every cord in the Cord House, including the oldest ones.
- **Ancient:** Highland families keep knotted cords recording every terrace. The oldest cords record a giant's name.

## Settlements

- **Terrace cities:** Cities of fitted stone built on the terraces, linked by stone roads and relay huts.

## Landmarks

- **The Great Terraces:** Tens of thousands of stepped fields climbing from the valley floor to the clouds.
- **The Cord House:** The hall where the oldest knotted records are kept, many of them unread for generations.
- **The Giant's Bed:** The flat summit where Ukhu-Tal slept, still bearing the shape of his body.

## Story hooks

- The last cord-keeper who might read the oldest cord is dying in a lowland city, and someone has to bring her home.
- Ukhu-Tal has spoken to a child herding llamas, and she is the only person he will talk to.
- A terrace wall has collapsed onto a village, and a family is trapped under the stones.

## Fauna

- **Shulon** (Herbivore): A long-necked, llama-like climber used on the terrace roads; a ground mount.

## Overview

Fanghold is the open steppe north-west of Aldmere, a sea of grass under an enormous sky. Its people are herders, horse-riders and wolfkin clans who follow the herds from pasture to pasture. One stone fortress guards the only reliable well for a week's ride in any direction, and whoever holds it holds the steppe. Right now a slaver holds it, and his ledger is longer than his whip.

## Geography

The steppe is a high, dry plateau of grass and wildflowers, cut by shallow river valleys that flood in spring and dry to cracked mud in summer. The wind never stops. The fortress of Fanghold sits on a rock outcrop above the Deep Well, a spring that has never failed. Ancient stone markers carved with animal faces stand at the old meeting places of the clans.

## Ecology

The steppe feeds vast herds of wild horses, saiga and woolly cattle, and the predators that follow them: wolves, steppe lions, giant eagles and dire boars. Stampedes are a natural disaster as dangerous as any storm. The clans move with the herds and take only what they need, and the ground repays them with grass that comes back every spring.

## Peoples

The clans of Fanghold are wolfkin, humans and half-orcs, organised into families that trace their descent through their grandmothers. They are hospitable to strangers, ferocious to enemies and suspicious of anyone who lives in one place. Their yurts are warm, crowded and full of children, and every guest is fed before they are asked their name.

## Power & trade

The clans form a loose federation that meets once a year at the stone markers. Each clan is led by its eldest woman, and disputes are settled by contests of horsemanship or arm-wrestling. Wool, horses, felt and leather flow out to the cities; salt, iron and tea flow in. The water-peace, a sworn oath that no clan may deny water to another, has held for three generations.

## Faith & customs

The clans honour the day-gods and the wind, which they believe carries the voices of their ancestors. They leave the first cup of milk of each day to the wind, and they never build a wall that blocks it. Eagles are sacred and may not be hunted.

## The Tyrant

Varrick Kade, the Slaver Lord, is a human mercenary captain who came to the steppe to sell weapons and stayed to sell people. He took Fanghold's fortress by bribing its guards and now holds the Deep Well hostage. He pays his mercenaries well, keeps meticulous accounts and believes that everything, including a person, has a price. He is the first man to break the water-peace and live.

## Senses

Endless wind; horse sweat and wild thyme; drumming hooves; fermented milk around a dung fire.

## History

- **Now:** Varrick Kade has seized the fortress and the Deep Well, and he trades water for people. The clans that cannot pay lose their children to his chains.
- **Parents:** Within living memory a great drought killed half the herds, and the clans survived only because the water-peace held.
- **Grandparents:** In their grandparents' time the clans swore the water-peace at the stone markers after a feud over the Deep Well that killed hundreds.
- **Ancient:** The stone markers were raised by the first clans, who are said to have been wolves who learned to stand.

## Settlements

- **Fanghold:** The stone fortress above the Deep Well, and the market town around it, home of the Howling Hearth inn.
- **Nomad yurt camps:** Moving camps of felt yurts that follow the herds across the steppe.

## Landmarks

- **The Deep Well:** A spring in the rock beneath the fortress that has never run dry.
- **The Wolf Stones:** Carved stone markers where the clans meet each year.
- **The Long Run:** A valley where the wild horses stampede every spring, and young riders prove themselves.

## Story hooks

- A clan grandmother wants her grandchildren back from Kade's ledger, and she has a plan.
- A wild horse herd has started stampeding toward the fortress every night, and no one knows what drives them.
- Kade's bookkeeper wants out, and she is willing to trade the ledger for safe passage.

# Three Songs of the Fen
*Collected by Tove Rushmere, who sang them badly until someone taught her how*

**Shelf:** Ballad sheet  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1012 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Three songs, set down as they are sung at the Crooked Lantern. The verses marked with a star are the ones people argue about.

## The Eel-Wife's Reckoning

*Slow, with a hum between the lines.*

> The eel is in the reed, my dear,
> the reed is in the mist,
> and the one who counts the both of them
> has never been dismissed.
>
> I weigh it on the walkway, dear,
> I weigh it with my hand,
> and what the scale will not forgive
> the fen will understand.

\* *Some sing "dismissed"; some sing "been kissed." The second is thought to be older, and is sung when the singer is tired.*

## The Lamp in the Window

*Soft, and the last line is a whisper.*

> A moth in a jar, a light in the glass,
> the dead come home as the long hours pass.
> Ring the bell once, and ring the bell twice,
> and leave them the stew with a little of spice.
>
> Ring the bell thrice, and let it be still:
> there is nobody out there who wishes us ill.

\* *The last line is disputed. Elders say it is a promise; the young say it is a question. Both agree on the tune.*

## Do Not Whistle

*Fast, with the foot on the planks.*

> Hold your tongue on the water, my love,
> hold your tongue, the fog is a glove,
> it takes what you give it and gives nothing back,
> so give it your silence and walk the black.

*Sung only on land, only in company, and never at the end of a long day.*

## A note from the collector

I did not write these. I wrote them down, and I changed the order of two lines where I thought the singer had lost the thread. I regret it, and I have marked the places. A song that cannot survive a correction is a poster, and a song that cannot survive a collector is a ghost.

Morrowyn, I am told, likes the second. I have never asked.

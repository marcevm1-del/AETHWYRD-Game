# Goods Through Brindlewick
*Ysolde Furrow, scrap and ore buyer on Forge Lane*

**Shelf:** Trade notes  
**Region:** Whisperwood  
**In-world date:** 1011 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

WHAT I PAY FOR, AND WHAT I DON'T

(Pinned inside my shutter, copied here so the next buyer has it. If you are standing at my counter reading this over my shoulder, please wipe your boots.)

## I pay for

- **Scrap Metal.** Any amount, any state, provided it has no flesh on it. Forge Lane builds on it. A bent blade is ore with an opinion.
- **Kobold Fang.** The little ones from Whisperwood are fine; I will not haggle on a fang that is clean. Kobold Miners carry the best, because they spend their lives biting rock.
- **Crude Blade.** Goblins forge them badly, but the metal is honest. I melt them down and sell the bar to the smiths.
- **Candle Stub.** Yes, really. Wax is wax, and every kobold in the wood hoards stubs. A fistful will buy a rookie a hot supper.
- **Gel Core.** Only if it is whole. A cracked core is jelly with a bad reputation.

## I don't pay for

- **Anything still wet.** I am not a butcher and neither is the forge.
- **Goblin Ear in any quantity.** The Guild clerk pays for those at the board; bring them there. I will take your gloves instead if you ask.
- **Stolen Trinket.** If it has a name scratched on the inside, it goes back to the Guild desk, and you go with it. I say this kindly.

## What the rookies get wrong

They think the scrap is the prize. It is not. The prize is the second trip: a rookie who sells me a bent sword on Solenday and buys a better one from the smith next door on Vexday has done what the whole lane is built to make people do. I earn a little on the scrap, the smith earns a little more, and a young person goes back into Whisperwood with a blade that does not fold on the first Goblin.

Thorgrim keeps the lane, they say, and I believe it, because I have never seen a promise kept so often by people who swear so little.

## Rules for the counter

1. Put it on the scale; do not tell me what it weighs.
2. If you dispute the weight, we weigh it again together. Nobody has ever won an argument with the scale.
3. Come back. A good buyer is paid by the second visit.

## A habit I recommend

When you have sold your first haul and you are feeling rich, walk the length of Forge Lane once without buying anything. Listen. The ring of the hammers tells you how many people are about to need the thing you are carrying. On a loud morning, price high. On a quiet one, price fair and be patient.

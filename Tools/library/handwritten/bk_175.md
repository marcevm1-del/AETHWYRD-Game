# The House Book of the Ninth Bough
*Mirelle Auvaen, housekeeper of a court house in Lothaniel, in the old formal manner*

**Shelf:** Household book  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 986 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

*It is proper that the house book begin with a courtesy.*

Good morning to whoever reads this. I hope the branch has treated you well.

## The shape of the house

The house of the Ninth Bough is grown, not built. Its rooms are hollows in the living wood; its stairs are roots coaxed upward over three generations; its windows are gaps in the leaves that the house has agreed to keep. There is no nail in it. A nail is a small wound, and the branch remembers.

## Mornings

We rise with the light, which comes late through the canopy. We greet the house before we greet each other. This is done by laying a palm on the wall, once, gently. The house does not answer. It is, I believe, grateful.

## Meals

We eat what the court's gardens give: honey, nuts, leaf-bread, a thin green soup. We do not eat meat in the house itself. A guest who wishes to may eat below, on the stair, where the roots are thick and do not mind.

## Guests

A guest is received at the door, not the window. A guest is offered water from the house's own cup, which has never been used by anyone else. A guest is given the quiet room, which is warm, and is not asked how long they stay.

If the guest is of the human kind, they are told, gently, the price of the quiet room. It is twice what an elf would pay. It is the custom. It is not an insult, and I have never found a way to explain that which did not sound like one.

## What I have learned in forty years

A house is a patient thing. It learns your steps. It learns your humour. After a time it learns your grief, and a house that has learned your grief will be quieter than it used to be.

Sylvasday is our holy day. We keep it by doing nothing at all, in the house, in the company of the house. It is the best day of the week and I wish it came more often.

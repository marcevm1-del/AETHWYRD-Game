# Four Generations in the Outer Mines
*Mara Tallowby, a human labourer's daughter, who keeps the house book for a family that has worked the outer seams since her great-grandfather's time*

**Shelf:** Household book  
**Region:** Iron Hold  
**In-world date:** 1016 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

We are not dwarves, and we have never pretended to be. We are the people who carry the ore. In Karak Dûn they call us the outer folk, and they do not mean it unkindly. They mean it accurately.

## The house

Two rooms cut into the rock at the edge of the working. It is warm in winter, because the mountain breathes out, and it is warm in summer, because the mountain does not stop. We keep the door open for the air and the shutter closed for the dust.

There is a table, a bench, a stove and a wall of hooks for the lamps. Everyone has a lamp. Everyone's lamp has a name. Mine is called Peg, and she has been to places I would not take a person.

## The shift

My father went down at dawn and came up at dusk. My brother went down at dusk and came up at dawn. My mother held the house between them, and we children learned to tell time by the sound of boots on the stair.

## What we eat

Bread, always. Soup, mostly. Beer on Thorsday, which is the day of the great forging, when the whole hold is in a good mood and even the outer folk are given a cup at the long table. Meat is a feast. A cheese is a treasure.

## What we owe

We owe the mine our backs. We owe the clans a fair day's work. We owe each other the sort of care that cannot be put in a ledger. I would not say the dwarves have made us welcome. I would say that they have made us useful, and in a hold, that is a form of belonging.

## What my grandmother said

"A mountain does not love you. But it keeps you warm."

I have never decided whether this is cynicism or tenderness. I suspect that in the outer mines they are the same thing.

## For whoever comes next

Keep your lamp trimmed. Keep your boots dry. Say good morning to the stair.

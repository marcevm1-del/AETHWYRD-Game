# A Peat-Cutter's House Book
*Garrick Thorne, peat-cutter, written on the backs of tally-slips*

**Shelf:** Household book  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1001 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

My house is a drying-rack with a roof on it. Everything in it is either peat or has been near it.

## The stack

Peat is cut in spring, stacked in summer and burnt in winter. Between those three, it is dried, and the drying is the whole of the art. A turf that has not dried smokes and does not warm. A turf that has dried too fast cracks and burns like paper. The right turf is the colour of strong tea and rings a little when two are knocked together.

Stack them in a hollow square, three high, with a gap between so the wind goes through. Roof them with reed. Do not stack against the wall of the house. A wall that has a stack against it will be damp by midwinter.

## What the day looks like

I rise before the light, cut until the fog lifts and eat standing up. I stack at noon. In the afternoon I mend: spade handles, a boot, the roof. I eat at dusk, ring the bell and go to bed.

## Morrowsday

On Morrowsday nobody cuts peat. The bog gives back what it takes on that day, and I have no wish to see what that is. I sit indoors and sharpen things, and I am generally in a better temper at the end of it than at the beginning of the week.

## The fire

A peat fire does not blaze. It glows. It is the only fire I know that is companionable, in that it asks nothing and will go on all night if left alone. Keep the ash. A good bed of ash makes the fire last and keeps the heat in.

## For the apprentice

A cutter learns three things: where the peat is good, where the ground is not and which of those two looks like the other. The third is the lesson. I have lost a spade and a boot to that lesson, and a cousin to a worse one. Cut where the old cutters cut, and follow the poles they left.

Thorgrim gave us the spade. I have never found out who gave us the patience.

# Pole Songs, With the Rests Marked
*Neddy Sallow, punt-pole singer, who says the rests are the song*

**Shelf:** Ballad sheet  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1019 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

We do not sing on the water. That is the first law of the fen, and it is the reason the pole songs are the strangest in Aethwyrd: they are sung with the mouth shut.

## How it works

A pole song is a rhythm, not a tune. The punt-guide taps it on the pole with the flat of the hand, once for a push, twice for a glide, and the passengers know where the boat is by the beat. A song is a way to say, "I am here, I am steady, you are safe."

## The one you will hear first

**Push-push-glide.** *(tap-tap, rest)*  
That is *Going Home*. It means the guide is sure of the path. It is the slowest song and the most welcome.

**Push, glide-glide, push.** *(tap, rest, rest, tap)*  
That is *Slow Water*. It means the channel is shallow, and the passengers should sit still.

**Push-push-push, rest, rest, rest.**  
That is *Hold On*. You will know it when you hear it. Hold on.

## The rests

The rests are the song. In a pole song, the silence between the taps is where the fog lives, and a good guide leaves it alone. A guide who fills the rests with sound is a guide who has forgotten where they are.

## What I teach

I teach young guides to sing with their wrists. The notes are taps; the words are the pauses; and the tune is the reed-edge you cannot see. A guide who has the rhythm in their wrists will never be lost.

I have sung *Going Home* for forty years and have not once sung it aloud. Sylvara knows it. The reeds know it. I think the fog does, too, and has agreed to forget.

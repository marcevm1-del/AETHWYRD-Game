# What the Goblins Keep in the Flooded Cave
*Dorrit Quillon, a visiting scholar of the Mages' Academy, on a fragment shown to her with some ceremony*

**Shelf:** Valdrisian fragment  
**Region:** Moraveil  
**In-world date:** 1011 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I came to Moraveil to look at a wall. I was shown a shrine.

## The Flooded Cave

It is where the truce began. The goblins keep it as a shrine, and I was asked to leave my boots at the entrance and to wade in barefoot. The water is cold, clear and ankle-deep. It reflects the roof, which is hung with small white stalactites, and the lantern-light makes them look like a ceiling of teeth.

At the back, on a flat rock above the waterline, there is a clay pot.

## The pot

Broken, and mended with a resin that has gone amber. It is old. It is, I believe, very old, and not goblin work: the clay is finer than theirs, the glaze is a different green and the shape is a style I have seen in the Academy's cases.

Around the shoulder, there is a band of writing. I cannot read it with certainty. I read what I could.

*"Keep the water. Keep the light. Keep faith with the one who keeps them."*

It could be a blessing. It could be a rule for a cistern. I will say it reads in the register of old Weave-tongue, which is a language that does not distinguish well between the two.

## The goblins' account

They say it was left by a *person who came before*. They do not say who. They say it was kept by the first goblins who found the cave, and that they were hunted, and hid in it, and were not found. They say the pot is why.

I do not know whether they are right. I know that a pot is a modest thing to be saved by.

## What I did

I copied the writing and made a tracing of the shape. I left a small gift: a packet of tea. I was assured it would be appreciated.

I recommend that no scholar try to move the pot. I recommend that no scholar be offered the choice. I put my boots back on, and I thanked the cave, in a whisper, in both languages.

# Carved in Stone, Changed with Difficulty
*Orm Chisel-Hand, stone-cutter of the Law Wall, on why nothing is ever quickly amended*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Iron Hold  
**In-world date:** 1005 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

The laws of Karak Dûn are carved in stone, and they are changed only with great difficulty. This is not an accident of temperament. It is the point.

## The wall

A long granite face in the lowest hall, three men high. Every law the hold has ever agreed is carved on it, in letters the length of a hand. The oldest are at the bottom, worn nearly smooth. The newest are at the top, where a man must stand on a scaffold to read them.

## How a law is made

A clan brings a petition. The clans debate it for a season. The Thane calls a vote, in pebbles. If the vote carries, the stone-cutter, who is I, goes to the wall and cuts it in.

That sounds simple. It is not. A law that is carved cannot be rubbed out. If a law is mistaken, we do not erase it. We carve another beneath it, saying that the first is withdrawn, and the two stand together, so that anyone who reads the wall can see both the mistake and the correction.

## The consequence

There are laws on that wall that contradict each other. There are laws that have been withdrawn four times and carved back three. There is a law about the proper length of a smith's apron that takes up a whole panel and has been the subject of a hundred and forty years of argument. It is the best-loved passage on the wall.

## The reason

A law that is easy to change is a law that is changed by the powerful. A law that is hard to change is a law that must be argued, patiently, in front of everyone, with everyone's grandfather listening. I do not say the method is quick. I say that nobody has ever been cheated by it.

I have carved eleven laws in forty years. I am proud of seven, content with three and sorry for one. It is still there. It will always be there. That is the discipline.

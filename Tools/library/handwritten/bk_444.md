# The Punt-Guide's Daughter and the Quiet Hour
*A tale for reading aloud, told by the fire in a stilt-house of the Silent Fen*

**Shelf:** Stories for reading aloud  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1014 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

*(Read this one softly. It has a hush in it, and the hush is the story.)*

In the Silent Fen there lived a punt-guide named Marrow, and a daughter named Wren. Marrow poled the black water from dawn until the fog came down, and Wren sat in the bow and learned the way.

"Which way is the Walking Hut?" asked Marrow.

"Left of the heron," said Wren.

"Which way is home?"

"Toward the bell."

"Which way is the Quiet Hour?"

Wren did not answer. She did not know.

"There is an hour in every day," said Marrow, "when the fog gives way and the fen goes still. The reeds do not rustle. The water does not lap. Not even the herons speak. It lasts as long as it takes to say a prayer, and then it is gone."

"What is it for?"

"It is for listening."

---

The next day, when the Quiet Hour came, Wren put down her pole. She sat in the bow with her hands in her lap and listened.

She heard nothing. Then she heard the nothing: a wide, round, soft nothing, the kind a bowl makes when it is empty. And inside the nothing, very far away, she heard the sound of a small bell, ringing three times.

"What is that?" she whispered.

"That," said Marrow, "is the village, saying that it is there."

"Why can I only hear it now?"

"Because for the rest of the day, you are too busy being the fen."

Wren thought about that for a long time. When the fog came back, she took up the pole.

"I'd like to be quiet for an hour every day," she said.

"Good," said Marrow. "So would I. We will start tomorrow."

And they did.

*(Say the last line together:)* **"The fen is only quiet for those who stop."**

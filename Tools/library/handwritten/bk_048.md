# Eel Three Ways, and Why Not Four
*Sedge Alder, eel-wife of Greymoor stilt-village*

**Shelf:** Cookery  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 987 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

My mother said there were three ways to cook an eel and a fourth that was an insult. I have never found out what the fourth was, and I have stopped asking.

## Smoked

Gut it by the water, where the fog will take the smell. Rub it with coarse salt and a handful of crushed alder bark, and hang it in the rafters over the fire for three days. Do not look at it. A watched eel goes tough. When it has turned the colour of a good boot, it is ready, and it keeps for a month if the roof does not leak.

## Stewed with bog-berries

Cut the eel in rings as thick as a thumb. Put the rings in a pot with cranberries, a little black bread torn to crumbs and enough water to cover. Cook it slowly. The berries give it a sourness that cuts the fat, and the bread thickens it into a gravy. This is what we eat on feast days and what we eat the day after.

If you have a Hearth Stew recipe from a southern kitchen, you can use its stock for the base. It will not taste of the fen, but it will taste of somebody's care.

## Fried, on a griddle, with a cold hand

Cut the eel in fillets. Roll them in black-bread crumbs and fry them quickly in a hot pan. The cold hand is the cook's: wet your fingers in water before you touch the fish, so that you do not leave your warmth on it.

## Why not four

I think the fourth way was raw. I think my mother said it was an insult because it was dangerous, and she did not want to say so in front of the children.

## For the guest

If you are asked to eat in a stilt-house, you will be given eel. Eat it. If you cannot, say so before you sit down, and the house will find you something else. Do not leave it half-finished. The fen does not waste, and neither do we.

Morrowyn keeps the dead, and the dead like eel stew. We leave a bowl out on feast days. It is always empty in the morning, and I have given up asking by whom.

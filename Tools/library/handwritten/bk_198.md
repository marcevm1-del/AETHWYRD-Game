# The Reed-Weaver's Book of the House
*Linnet Ashworth, reed-weaver, in the hours between light and sleep*

**Shelf:** Household book  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1015 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

A reed-weaver's house is a house that has been woven. The walls are mats, the roof is thatch, the floor is a layer of green stalks that are changed when they stop being green. If it were stone, it would not move with the water. Reeds give. That is why they last.

## What a reed does

It bends in the wind and does not break. It drinks from the water and does not rot, if it is cut in the right month and dried on the right side. It smells of the fen: green and wet and a little sweet. After a year in the house, you stop noticing, and after two, you stop being able to sleep anywhere else.

## The weave

There are four weaves, and a house uses all of them.

- **Plain**, for floors. Over one, under one.
- **Twill**, for walls. Over two, under two, shifted each row. It sheds rain.
- **Basket**, for storage and for the cradles.
- **Close**, for doors and shutters. It is so tight that a mouse takes the hint.

## Mending

A house is a thing you mend every day. When you rise, look at the roof. When you eat, look at the walls. When you go to bed, listen to the floor. Anything that sounds wrong is a thing that is about to be wrong.

## Guests

A guest is given a mat. If they are tired, it is a thick mat. If they are staying a season, it is their own mat, with their name woven into the corner. We do not take it back when they leave. It goes with them, and sometimes a mat comes back years later, carried by a child who has never met us.

## A thing I was taught

When a house is woven and the roof is on, the first thing hung is a jar of lantern-moths. It lights the dead home, and it tells the living that the house has been finished. Both are true, and both matter.

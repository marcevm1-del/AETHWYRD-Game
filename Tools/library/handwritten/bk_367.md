# The Street Law of the Proving Halls
*Cassian Wherry, arbiter's clerk, who writes down what the gate-town says*

**Shelf:** Law notes  
**Region:** Great Labyrinth (Floors 1-40)  
**In-world date:** 1012 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

There is a written law and there is a street law. The written law is in the arbiter's book at Aldmere and I have read it twice. The street law is what the gate-town says when two parties disagree at the bottom of the stair at the end of a long day. I write down the second kind, because the first never has the answer.

## Whose sword is it

A sword dropped on the floor of a hall is the sword of whoever dropped it, for as long as they can show a loss slip. A sword picked up from the floor of a hall is the sword of the one who picked it up, until a loss slip says otherwise. When both are true, the finder keeps the sword and pays the loser a fair share of what it is worth. Most disputes end there.

## Who pays for a cleared hall

Nobody. A hall that is cleared is a hall that resets, and the Guild says so in plain words: the halls reset every week. No one owns a hall and no one owes a toll. If a party claims a hall as theirs because they cleared it on Thorsday, I will remind them, kindly, that on Solenday it is somebody else's turn.

## When you fall

If you fall, you go back to the last checkpoint with your keys and the puzzles you solved. That is the law of the halls, and it is kinder than it sounds. It means that nobody can be punished by the Labyrinth for being brave. It also means nobody can use a fall as an excuse to leave a friend.

## Claiming a find

If you find a thing in a hall, say so aloud. The street law is that a find declared aloud belongs to whoever heard you say it, which is to say, to you and your party. A find kept silent is a find that may be lost to a quarrel.

## Disputes between parties

1. Ask who was there first. The Guild desk keeps the slips.
2. Ask who needs it more. People answer this honestly more often than you would expect.
3. If neither answers, flip a coin for the first choice and let the loser pick second. Vex keeps the coin, and she is fair in the long run.

## What I have learned

The law in a hall is not about who is right. It is about whether people can go in again tomorrow and meet each other at the stair. I have not seen a quarrel settled that was not also a quarrel mended.

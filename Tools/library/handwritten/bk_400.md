# What the Bridges Sing
*Collected by Fenwick Dale, who has crossed every bridge in Lothaniel and been asked to leave several*

**Shelf:** Ballad sheet  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 1009 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

The living bridges of Lothaniel creak. They are not broken; they are talking. The court says it is a language and the hamlets say it is a lullaby, and I say that after thirty years of crossing them I have learned to sing along.

## The Long Crossing

*Slow. Sing it on the first step and on the last.*

> One foot on the bough, one foot on the air,
> I trust you, old timber, to know that I'm there.
> A hundred feet under, the green rises up,
> and the hand of the tree is a hand that I trust.

## The Court Walk

*Formal, with the second line spoken, not sung.*

> Ninth bough, tenth bough, step in time,
> *(do not look down; it is not polite)*
> Eleventh bough, twelfth bough, rank and rhyme,
> *(and mind the gnome with the clock)*

## The Hamlet Walk

*Quick, with a lilt. The wood elves sing this one going up, and the court elves never do.*

> We came from the well and we go to the sky,
> we carried the water and we asked not why,
> the court may be high and the court may be wise,
> but the moonwell is ours and it glows in our eyes.

\* *The last line is disputed. The court sings "it glows in their eyes." Nobody has settled the matter.*

## A note

I did not write these. I wrote down what I heard on the bridges, at dusk, in the voices of people who did not know I was listening. I apologise to them, and I hope they are not offended. If you cross a bridge and hear someone humming, join in. It is better manners than silence.

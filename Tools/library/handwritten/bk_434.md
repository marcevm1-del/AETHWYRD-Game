# The Hearth-Stone That Went Cold
*A story told to dwarf children in the Third Hall, with the lamps turned low*

**Shelf:** Stories for reading aloud  
**Region:** Iron Hold  
**In-world date:** 1009 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

*(Read it plainly. A dwarf's story does not need much help.)*

There was once a young smith named Thrain, who was very good with a hammer and very bad at waiting.

He had a hearth-stone, as every smith does, from his first forge. It sat on the shelf by the fire. He touched it every morning. He told it his plans. But Thrain was young, and he was proud, and one day he decided the stone was only a stone.

"It is a pebble," he said. "A pebble cannot help me. I shall make my own luck."

He put the stone in the bottom of a drawer and forgot about it.

---

That winter, nothing went right. The metal would not take the heat. The hammer slipped. The apprentices were slow, the orders were late and the ale turned sour in the barrel.

"It is bad luck," said Thrain, to no one.

"It is bad temper," said an old smith, passing the door.

"It is the stone!" said Thrain, and flung open the drawer.

The stone was cold. It was colder than the drawer, colder than the cellar, colder than any stone had a right to be. Thrain picked it up and it was like holding a handful of winter.

He sat down on the floor of the forge, with the stone in his two hands, and felt a little foolish. After a while, he felt a little warm. After a while longer, he felt that he had been talking to the stone for an hour.

"I am sorry," he said.

The stone did not answer. It was a stone. But the fire in the hearth caught, all at once, and the whole forge lit up gold.

*(Say the last line together, in the deepest voice you have:)* **"A promise kept in metal is kept by a friend."**

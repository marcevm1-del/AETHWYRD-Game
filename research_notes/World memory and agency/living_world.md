# World reactivity, information flow and bounded simulation

## What does each source explicitly claim?

### Takeaway
The strongest public developer descriptions show several distinct ways to make a world readable: locally witnessed reputation, crowd rules and settlement-specific behavior, authored delayed quest consequences, and wildlife with recognizable species behaviors. They also expose the production cost: authored branches and path testing scale quickly, while a fully individualized population can demand substantial design, art, and voice work.

### Cited Findings
- Guerrilla’s description of *Horizon Forbidden West* says every non-combat NPC belongs to a crowd system supporting rules for reactions, paths, and animations; an attitude system differentiates behavior. Tribe-specific gestures, settlement activities, and props communicate culture, and the Living World team collaborates with Narrative, Quest, and Environment teams. This is a shipped game’s developer account. [PlayStation.Blog: “Horizon Forbidden West: An authentic world”](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=356989)
- The same Guerrilla account describes purpose-built environmental detail: NPCs handle materials, drink, talk, use objects, and perform culturally specific activities. Settlements combine visual storytelling with useful services such as vendors and upgrade resources. [PlayStation.Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=356989)
- Rockstar describes roughly 200 animal, bird, and fish species in *Red Dead Redemption 2*, with distinct behaviors such as herd travel, scavenging, pack hunting, and predator bluff-charging, forming a “complex ecosystem.” This is a publisher feature description; it does not disclose the simulation architecture or its per-species cost. [Rockstar Games: “Wildlife in Red Dead Redemption 2”](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/51974aa3a8k847/Wildlife-in-Red-Dead-Redemption-2)
- Rockstar says *Red Dead Redemption 2* Honor affects interactions with the surrounding world; actions such as rescuing townsfolk raise it, while harming innocents or larceny lower it, with unique consequences and benefits. [Rockstar Games: “Honor in Red Dead Redemption 2”](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/ak14o88389271k/Honor-in-Red-Dead-Redemption-2)
- The official *Red Dead Redemption* manual documents an information path for crime: eyewitnesses can report crimes to law enforcement; wanted status persists while lawmen or civilians can see the player; hiding can end a Wanted Level; bounties can be paid or cleared with pardon letters. This is a concrete example of witness-to-authority information flow and a recovery mechanism, although from the earlier *Red Dead Redemption*, not *RDR2*. [Rockstar Games manual, “Law Enforcement”](https://media.rockstargames.com/rockstargames-newsite/img/manuals/en_us/RDR_PS3_ESSENTIALS_MANUAL_ENG.pdf)
- Ubisoft’s *The Division 2* developer account describes “Living World Activities” as sequences for NPC groups: travel, gather resources, return to safety, and despawn. NPCs receive task-specific and contextual duties; level designers place environmental opportunities such as elevated cover for overwatch. [Ubisoft News: “The Division 2 – Making Enemies Tougher and Friendlies Smarter”](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/5wu3eterDz21wNKiAcDJl/the-division-2-making-enemies-tougher-and-friendlies-smarter)
- Playground’s lead designer says the announced *Fable* Living Population has 1,000+ NPCs with personalities and lives; NPCs have homes, jobs, schedules, traits, and individualized relationships. The team initially explored procedural generation but found generated people “random and incoherent,” then chose to hand-design each NPC. The article says all are voiced and around 100 actors were cast for this population. This is an official description of a forthcoming game, not a post-release evaluation. [Xbox Wire: “Fable: How We Built the Living Population”](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/06/10/fable-living-population-details-explained-xbox-games-showcase-2026/)
- That *Fable* article says the player sees NPC name, role, and trait, and can inspect a social panel that explains why an NPC feels a certain way. Up to six reputations may be active in a settlement; NPC disposition can combine them. Its described memory examples include remembering a job provided, a life-changing gift, or the death of a spouse. [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/06/10/fable-living-population-details-explained-xbox-games-showcase-2026/)
- Playground’s game director says the announced *Fable* reputation system is based on witnessed actions; reputations vary by settlement, and people interpret the same reputation differently. The developer describes consequences for dialogue, romance, and shop prices, plus actions such as paying a town crier to overwrite a reputation. The same interview says the main story “will wait” while players explore, and that settlement population systems are not required to progress the main quest. [Xbox Wire: “Answering the Big Questions…”](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/01/22/fable-interview-overview-details-developer-direct-2026/)
- CD PROJEKT RED’s official QA transcript says quest choice combinations can number in the thousands or millions, and that QA cannot cover every path; testers follow long player paths to check that variables, consequences, and scenes work together. The transcript describes path testing as long-running and laborious. [CD PROJEKT RED: “More Than Testing: The Truth About Game QA”](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/184/more-than-testing-the-truth-about-game-qa-transcript-included)
- CD PROJEKT RED’s environment and production staff describe open-world believability as a collaboration-heavy process, with deliberate small details and even tree lines specifically created; they cite moving a mountain in *The Witcher 3* to improve a vista. This gives qualitative production-cost signals, not a budget or staffing estimate. [CD PROJEKT RED: “From Worldbuilding to Immersion”](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/137/from-worldbuilding-to-immersion-crafting-universes-in-games)
- Scope-status caveat: *Fable* is described here only as announced design. It is not evidence that these systems shipped, were technically stable at scale, or were received positively. The other examples above are descriptions of released titles, but these sources do not establish player reception.

### Inferences
- “A living world” need not mean every person is simulated at the same depth. The sources describe different layers: individual schedules and relationships (*Fable*), crowd behavior and attitude rules (*Horizon*), and group activity sequences (*The Division 2*).
- Information flow can be made legible by recording who witnessed an event, which local authority or community learned it, and what changed as a result. Rockstar’s law-enforcement manual is a clear precedent for the mechanism; applying it to town gossip and rumor boards is a design inference.
- Observable details can be authored as staged cues rather than generated from unrestricted simulation: a settlement’s gestures, props, work activity, or one rumor can signal its culture and current state.
- Production cost is not just simulation code. The sources point to hand-authored NPC data, bespoke voice work, environment and animation coordination, and extensive path QA as major cost centers.
- These sources do not show the internals of *RDR2* ecology or provide a transferable engineering cost per NPC/species. Avoid inferring a small-team cost multiplier from the published counts.

### Gaps
- No primary developer source located here provides a detailed, shipped open-world RPG design for dynamic rumor propagation across many settlements.
- No reliable source gives comparable budgets, headcounts, or person-month estimates for schedules, memory, rumor, and ecology.
- The *Fable* population, schedules, and memory details remain pre-release claims as of 2026-10-24; implementation and player outcomes cannot yet be verified.

## What player-facing design principle follows, and what is only inference?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports making causes and consequences readable at the scale where the player acts: witnessed actions can affect local opinion, environmental details can signal regional identity, and consequences can arrive later. A compact Ascend system should therefore prioritize explicit cause, knowledge path, and feedback over simulating every resident’s private life.

### Cited Findings
- *Horizon Forbidden West* developers say tribe identity should be recognizable through settlement design, behavior, animation, and material culture; their teams translate narrative frameworks into visuals and coordinate across disciplines. [PlayStation.Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=356989)
- *RDR2* developers explicitly connect Honor to world interaction and consequences. [Rockstar Games](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/ak14o88389271k/Honor-in-Red-Dead-Redemption-2)
- The announced *Fable* system exposes an NPC’s name, role, trait, and reasons for their disposition; its director describes witnessed actions and settlement-specific reputations rather than universal, omniscient morality. [Xbox Wire population article](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/06/10/fable-living-population-details-explained-xbox-games-showcase-2026/); [Xbox Wire director interview](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/01/22/fable-interview-overview-details-developer-direct-2026/)
- CD PROJEKT RED’s official QA account says teams test long action paths and consequences, while acknowledging that not all of the thousands or millions of possible combinations can be covered. [CD PROJEKT RED QA transcript](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/184/more-than-testing-the-truth-about-game-qa-transcript-included)
- Ascend canon already establishes a rumor board at every inn; named monsters’ deeds are reported there; local witness-based reputation and plausible knowledge paths are proposed; major outcomes should have a visible receipt; liberation should produce a small visible regional delta; and NPC schedules should use the locked in-game week without real-world deadlines. [Project Ascend Canon.md](E:/Ideas/Project-Ascend/Canon.md) (project source, not an external developer claim)

### Inferences
- **Design principle:** show the player a short causal receipt for high-impact outcomes: what happened, who plausibly learned it, and what changed. Use a journal, rumor board, NPC line, service change, or environment cue as appropriate. This is consistent with the sources’ legible social panels and visual signaling, but the specific “receipt” UX is Ascend’s design proposal.
- **Design principle:** rumors should travel over named, finite channels (witness to local authority, inn board, guild board, caravan, or companion) and arrive after in-game time. Do not make every NPC globally informed. The channel model follows from witness-based reputation and the existing Ascend canon; the exact propagation graph and delay are recommendations.
- **Design principle:** use schedules to support a few reliable, recognizable moments (work, rest, gathering, event) and keep essential services available or recoverable. This is an inference; source materials do not establish a universal “best” schedule model.
- **Design principle:** authored environmental change can carry a lot of meaning at relatively few, carefully chosen locations. This follows from Guerrilla’s account of deliberate settlement staging; the projected cost advantage for Ascend is an inference, not a cited measured result.
- **Design principle:** delayed consequences need a bounded horizon and a playable recovery route when the consequence blocks access or conflicts with Ascend’s protection against catastrophic loss. CDPR describes delayed consequences, and the *RDR* manual gives a concrete way to clear a bounty; the proposed recovery guarantee comes from Ascend’s canon and is not a general rule in the cited games.
- Do not describe the new *Fable* as a success case or infer player reception from its developer’s stated intentions. Its design is useful as a cost signal and a model for inspectable disposition, subject to post-release verification.

### Gaps
- Public developer materials seldom spell out how a player inspects rumor provenance, how long it takes to travel, or how stale reports are corrected.
- The sources establish examples, not a measured causal link between a specific feedback technique and player understanding or satisfaction.

## What scope risk would a tiny team face, and what compact offline model fits Ascend?

### Takeaway
For 49 regions, a very small team should author a small number of high-signal state changes per region and simulate only local, deterministic summaries. Keep unique memory for scene-critical named characters, aggregate state for ordinary populations, and wildlife behavior local to encounters. This recommendation fits Ascend’s existing boundaries: 2,500 named NPCs at several depths, a 90-minute in-game day, permanent liberation, recoverable missed defenses, and no always-online progression.

### Cited Findings
- Playground says designing every NPC by hand was chosen after procedural generation produced people the team found incoherent; every NPC gets a trait, home, job, schedule, and more, and the population alone uses around 100 voice actors. It explicitly calls the work difficult. This is a useful warning about adopting individual simulation as a baseline, but *Fable* is unreleased. [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/06/10/fable-living-population-details-explained-xbox-games-showcase-2026/)
- Guerrilla describes crowd rules and an attitude system for non-combat NPCs, with collaboration among Living World, Narrative, Quest, and Environment teams. [PlayStation.Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=356989)
- Ubisoft’s group activity example is finite and task-oriented: NPC groups gather resources and return, with duties attached to a sequence and environmental affordances placed by level design. [Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/5wu3eterDz21wNKiAcDJl/the-division-2-making-enemies-tougher-and-friendlies-smarter)
- CD PROJEKT RED’s QA team describes path combinations reaching thousands or millions and says it cannot test every possible path. [CD PROJEKT RED](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/184/more-than-testing-the-truth-about-game-qa-transcript-included)
- Canon fixes the product as offline-first, single-player, and premium; defines 40 surface regions, five Underworld layers, the Great Labyrinth, and Vesper, while also referring to 49 existing Gazetteer echoes; sets 2,500 named NPCs with 150 core, 600 major, and 1,750 townsfolk; and locks a 90-minute in-game day. It also defines permanent liberation, settlement-safe missed sieges, rumor boards, and post-story peaceful continuation. [Project Ascend Canon.md](E:/Ideas/Project-Ascend/Canon.md)
- Canon’s bounded memory proposal says nearby and scene-critical NPCs react to plausible witnessed events, rumors travel through explicit local channels, and distant populations update in cheaper tiers. Its consequence-authoring proposal calls for a compact set of environmental, social, service/board, and journal feedback. [Project Ascend Canon.md](E:/Ideas/Project-Ascend/Canon.md)

### Inferences
#### Recommended bounded model (design proposal, not a claim about the cited games)
1. **One region-state record per region.** Keep a small set of durable flags and counters: siege/liberated state, story phase, key settlement condition, ecology pressure band, active local rumor IDs, and a few authored recovery or aftermath states. A region transition fires only from explicit player or story actions; no background simulation needs to run while offline.
2. **Three information radii.**
   - **Scene memory:** nearby witnesses and scene-critical NPCs store a small event token (event, time, witness/source, disposition effect). Named core/major NPCs may have authored reactions; townsfolk use templates.
   - **Local knowledge:** each settlement has a bounded rumor queue with source, topic, arrival day, and expiry or correction. Inns and guild boards surface selected reports. Avoid a world-wide omniscient reputation value.
   - **Distant summary:** update only region-level counts/bands and board text when the player returns or when a relevant in-game time boundary is crossed. These are derived state summaries, not individually simulated inhabitants.
3. **A small authored receipt kit.** For each region, author a handful of repeatable outputs for high-value transitions: one environmental cue, one social/ambient line set, one service or quest-board change, and one journal/rumor explanation. Prioritize liberation, siege defense/miss, Tyrant defeat, major migration, and story outcomes. Treat the number of cues as a production cap to be decided in planning, not a fact established by research.
4. **Schedules as templates and exceptions.** Give shops, guards, inn staff, and a small group of scene-critical residents authored daily/weekly roles. Use a few settlement archetypes and day themes, then add bespoke schedule exceptions only for quests or festivals. Keep essential vendors, beds, and quest access available through an alternate NPC, a board, or a time-independent fallback.
5. **Ecology as local pressure, not a global food-web simulation.** Track a small pressure band by region/habitat/species group (for example stable, rising, depleted), updated by authored events, player hunting, siege/liberation, and migration triggers. Spawn and encounter tables read that band; specific hunts and Tyrants remain authored. Avoid simulating births, deaths, predation, and migration for every creature across all regions.
6. **Bound consequence recovery.** Every major negative result should state its cause and whether it is temporary or permanent. Permit recovery through a designed quest, payment, apology, time passage, alternate service, or post-story restoration when canon allows. No consequence should destroy a settlement, erase levels/skills, require real-world time, or strand the player from essential progression, consistent with Canon §§4b–5.
7. **Offline save and deterministic clocks.** Persist all event, rumor, schedule, and ecology state locally. Advance it only with the game’s 7-day calendar; do not use real-world deadlines, server checks, online-gated rewards, or decay while the game is closed. This follows the offline-first canon and keeps saves reproducible.
8. **Production guardrail: test state transitions, not every theoretical permutation.** Use a small matrix of region states × event types × information sources, then test each authored critical path and fallback. The CDPR QA source shows how quickly branch combinations become untestable; keeping independent flags and authored consequence horizons few is a scope-control inference.

#### Main tiny-team risks
- **State explosion:** many flags, schedules, rumors, quests, and ecology events can cross-trigger. Every additional persistent variable multiplies combinations that must be debugged and tested.
- **Content multiplication across 49 regions:** a “small bespoke response” repeated across each region becomes a large writing, animation, audio, QA, and localization task.
- **False omniscience:** NPC reactions without a plausible witness or rumor path make reputation feel arbitrary and undermine Ascend’s canon requirement for knowledge provenance.
- **Clock and access softlocks:** an NPC sleeping or moving can make services, quests, or romance paths inaccessible; delayed consequence branches can strand progress without fallbacks.
- **Over-simulation:** full schedules and granular ecology across 2,500 named NPCs and thousands of monster variants would spend effort on state the player cannot see and create large save/test burdens. The source evidence supports the production risk qualitatively; the exact Ascend cost is unknown.
- **Authored-only response can feel thin if repeated:** a compact kit needs regional variation in language, materials, sound, and social behavior. That risk and mitigation are design inferences based on Guerrilla’s emphasis on specific cultural detail.
- **Rumor contradiction and staleness:** reports may arrive after events change or reach the wrong settlement. Store provenance, arrival time, and status; correct reports explicitly rather than silently changing old information. This is an inferred implementation guardrail.
- **Save compatibility:** new authored event IDs and state fields need defaults and migration rules if the content schema changes. This is a software-production inference; the cited sources do not discuss Ascend save formats.

### Gaps
- Canon calls its regional set “49 existing Gazetteer echoes” while its world summary lists 40 surface regions, five Underworld layers, the Great Labyrinth, and Vesper. The precise accounting of which units make up 49 should be checked against canonical region data before setting a per-region content quota.
- The recommended tiers, data fields, event limits, and ecology bands are a proposed Ascend design, not a documented implementation from another game. The available sources do not establish numeric thresholds or a best-in-class simulation budget.
- The cited developers do not publish the internal runtime cost, memory footprint, save size, or staffing cost of these systems. Any hard technical budget requires an Ascend prototype and profiling.

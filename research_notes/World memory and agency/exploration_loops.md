# Exploration, discovery and long-session pacing

## What exploration loops and environmental guidance do developers describe?

### Takeaway
Official developer accounts describe several complementary ways to make exploration legible: environmental guidance that points without routing, clues that cause players to reinterpret familiar terrain, and recognizable landmarks/biomes that support orientation. These sources support a layered approach for Ascend, but provide no validated numerical rule for point-of-interest density or exploration duration.

### Cited Findings
- Sucker Punch’s environment art lead said Ghost of Tsushima’s world had more than 40 biomes and hundreds of points of interest. The team’s stated goal was that visible places should generally be reachable; it limited foliage variety and used stronger color choices to make areas feel fresher and more memorable. [Sucker Punch, “Crafting the World of Tsushima,” PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/07/09/crafting-the-world-of-tsushima/)
- Sucker Punch’s lead VFX artist explained that the Guiding Wind aims directly at the objective and leaves obstacle-aware pathfinding to the player. The team wanted players to engage their minds in navigation rather than follow a UI route. He also reported that cliffs and rock meshes made an early terrain-following particle version clip and disappear, so the team changed its approach. [Sucker Punch, “How stunning visual effects bring Ghost of Tsushima to life,” PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=345372)
- The same artist reported that a two-person VFX team handled much of Ghost’s development, so it relied on automated content updates. A small amount of elements were hand-placed to guide exploration; a biome-aware particle system replaced hundreds of individually grown particle systems in average locations. [Sucker Punch, “How stunning visual effects bring Ghost of Tsushima to life,” PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=345372)
- Nintendo developers said Tears of the Kingdom’s cliffside caves could make players wonder whether other cliffs also held caves. They described a valuable addition to familiar terrain as changing how players perceive the broader landscape. They also said the sequel added sky and cave spaces and more vertical play. [Nintendo, “Ask the Developer Vol. 9, Part 3: The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom”](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-part-3/)
- Nintendo developers also said sky islands in early development looked cluttered when there were too many, and they adjusted their quantity, appearance and spatial placement to make the sky readable. [Nintendo, “Ask the Developer Vol. 9, Part 3”](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-part-3/)
- In a PlayStation interview, FromSoftware president Hidetaka Miyazaki said Elden Ring aimed to let players explore at their own pace. He described balancing exploration with boss fights, progression order and event progression as a major development challenge, including pacing the player across a vast open-ended map. [Hidetaka Miyazaki interview, PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=359935)
- Bandai Namco’s official Elden Ring gameplay overview says Sites of Grace provide directional guidance toward important areas, map fragments reveal map guidance, and a player may avoid a difficult area and return later. [Bandai Namco, “Elden Ring: Gameplay Overview”](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/es_mx/news/elden-ring-introduction-part-3-gameplay-overview)

### Inferences
- **Player-facing principle:** guidance can communicate a destination while preserving the player’s route choice. Ascend’s canonical choice between System thread, natural cues, companion guidance, conventional markers and no active guidance fits this pattern; keep each mode functionally complete and provide equivalent nonvisual cues where possible.
- **Player-facing principle:** one memorable discovery can teach a reusable way of looking. Place a few authored landmark/lead/unmarked discoveries that reveal a recognizable regional pattern, rather than filling the map with identical icons or requiring every clue to be collected.
- **Player-facing principle:** nearby threats and detours should not make progress feel trapped. Make postponing a hard encounter legible, preserve a return route, and distinguish navigational direction from a compulsory route.
- **Small-team scope risk:** bespoke guidance particles, bespoke landmarks and unique routes for every point scale poorly. Reuse a small environmental-cue vocabulary and automate biome responses, reserving hand placement for high-value discoveries. Automated placement also risks visual clutter or cues that point through inaccessible geometry, as Sucker Punch’s VFX account illustrates.
- **Small-team scope risk:** vertical layers and seamless transitions multiply art, audio, navigation and testing work. Canon permits flight/teleport abilities but forbids flying mounts; any vertical exploration should be scoped around a small, tested set of route decisions rather than a full second world layer.

### Gaps
- The developer accounts do not provide validated landmark spacing, discovery frequency, guidance strength, or time-to-fatigue targets.
- Official sources explain authorial intent and production methods, not comparative player telemetry on whether these exact guidance systems improve long-session retention.

## How do RPGs reward discovery, expose quests, and support return visits and side-content choice?

### Takeaway
Ghost of Tsushima explicitly treats optional stories, exploration rewards and towns as parallel ways to deepen the main journey. Tears of the Kingdom’s developers describe new discoveries within a familiar setting as a way to make the whole map newly interesting, while Fable’s pre-release director interview describes a story that waits and progression designed around open access. For Ascend, the strongest fit is optional leads with tangible but nonessential rewards, recurring location-state changes, and main-story pacing that does not punish detours.

### Cited Findings
- Sucker Punch described Ghost of Tsushima’s side tales as optional character stories that broaden the player’s understanding of the island and companions; some are brief and some provide deeper character arcs. The studio said non-main-story content provides varied rewards, including collectibles, resources, charms and some armor sets. [Sucker Punch, “Ghost of Tsushima: Your questions answered,” PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/05/29/ghost-of-tsushima-your-questions-answered/)
- In the same Q&A, Sucker Punch said towns encountered during travel contain vendors and opportunities to find side stories. It also described the open-world game as accommodating both players who get distracted by distant sights or nearby activity and those who focus mainly on the main story. [Sucker Punch, “Ghost of Tsushima: Your questions answered”](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/05/29/ghost-of-tsushima-your-questions-answered/)
- Nintendo developers said they wanted players to explore Hyrule after the ending of Breath of the Wild, and that Tears of the Kingdom kept the familiar setting because new play could be layered onto a world the team and players already knew. They also described the difficulty of adding new “wonder” without making the established world incoherent. [Nintendo, “Ask the Developer Vol. 9, Part 1”](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-chapter-1/)
- Nintendo’s official Tears of the Kingdom Explorer’s Guide encourages players to record information that can help locate shrines, explains that entering a shrine makes it available as a travel gate, and states that players can leave a shrine and warp back when ready. It describes Spirit Orbs as a reward exchangeable for health or stamina growth. [Nintendo, “Tears of the Kingdom Explorer’s Guide,” PDF](https://zelda.nintendo.com/tears-of-the-kingdom/_pdfs/ExplorersGuide.pdf)
- Playground Games’ Fable game director said players could go almost anywhere after leaving the starting village; the team gave the story stakes but no ticking bomb so players could do other activities and the story would wait. He said progression and level balancing were shaped around access to settlements and fun activities wherever players choose to go. This is a pre-release design statement, not post-launch evidence. [Xbox Wire, “Fable: Answering the Big Questions After that Long-Awaited Developer_Direct Deep-Dive”](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/01/22/fable-interview-overview-details-developer-direct-2026/)
- CD PROJEKT RED quest designer Sarah Grummer said a quest should translate an emotional goal into something playable; she described consequences that can surface hours after the player’s action and the need to test long paths because changes can break distant scenes. [CD PROJEKT RED, “Turning Emotions, Story, and Big Moments Into Gameplay”](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/155/turning-emotions-story-and-big-moments-into-gameplay)
- FromSoftware’s Miyazaki said Elden Ring gives players options to approach challenges, including leaving an impasse and coming back later. He said the team wanted challenge to feel fair and fulfilling rather than difficult for its own sake, and noted that freedom, challenge and progression order had to be balanced. [Hidetaka Miyazaki interview, PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=359935)
- BioWare’s official Exalted Plains feature says the environment team portrayed the civil war through effects on the land and its people, and designed spaces where the Inquisition could make its mark and local people could rally around that change. [BioWare, “Exploring the Dales’ Exalted Plains”](https://blog.bioware.com/2014/04/11/exploring-the-dales-exalted-plains/)

### Inferences
- **Player-facing principle:** discovery rewards should be varied across narrative, practical resources and build expression, so players with different interests can find value. Keep signature or essential progression obtainable through reliable routes; optional discovery should accelerate, contextualize or personalize progression rather than gate it.
- **Player-facing principle:** quest discovery can come from people, environmental evidence, travel incidents and boards, not just a map icon. Let clues record as leads with enough context to return later; avoid making the player remember exact coordinates or long dialogue verbatim.
- **Player-facing principle:** support both completion styles: provide a clear main path and readable optional branches, but avoid making side content feel like an obligation for basic viability. Use one or two rewards per side story that matter to distinct player motivations.
- **Player-facing principle:** return visits feel worthwhile when a location recognizes a meaningful state change. Ascend already has strong canon hooks: besieged-to-liberated board changes, permanent safety, region recovery, inns, rumors and post-story continuation. A compact feedback bundle (route/patrol, service or board, rumor/dialogue, ambient cue) can make the state visible without requiring full settlement simulation.
- **Player-facing principle:** postpone ordinary exploration without hidden real-world deadlines. When an authored in-game urgency exists, communicate it clearly; otherwise let the main story wait, consistent with Canon’s no real-world-time deadlines and no live-service consequences.
- **Small-team scope risk:** branching side quests multiply writing, localization, voice, flags, QA paths and late-state contradictions. Use a repeatable quest spine with a few meaningful approaches, a small number of delayed receipts, and representative long-path tests. Do not promise bespoke consequences for all 2,500 named NPCs.
- **Small-team scope risk:** revisiting 40 regions can become a checklist if each one requires many bespoke state variants. Define a bounded region-memory kit and author it once per high-signal milestone; use reuse and state-driven variants for lesser updates.

### Gaps
- Fable evidence located here is a director’s pre-release description of intended design, not an evaluation of the shipped game or actual player behavior.
- The cited sources do not provide cross-game side-quest completion rates, reward utility comparisons, or quantified return-visit behavior.
- Public official sources do not establish how many meaningful branches or delayed callbacks a tiny team can safely author; that depends on Ascend’s actual tools, voice/localization policy and QA capacity.

## How should exploration fit frequent combat, long progression and accessibility, and what should Ascend avoid?

### Takeaway
The sources support pacing exploration through varied pressure, optional retreat, distinct calm and combat spaces, and adjustable information presentation. They do not support copying the scale of large RPGs; Ascend should prove a compact regional loop and let the player choose how much guidance and side content to engage, with accessibility modes treated as equivalent ways to understand the world.

### Cited Findings
- Miyazaki described Elden Ring’s challenge as a cycle of studying, adapting and trying again, with options to defer an impasse. He also said a vast world cannot remain dark and oppressive throughout, and described using color and traces of a past golden age to create contrast with darker situations. [Hidetaka Miyazaki interview, PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=359935)
- The official Horizon Forbidden West accessibility overview describes Story Mode as easing combat so players can focus on exploration and story, alongside harder settings. It also lists an in-game Notebook, tutorials, toggleable ability/enemy reminders, customizable HUD information, adjustable camera shake/motion blur, subtitle sizing/backgrounds, audio mixing and mono audio. [Guerrilla Games, “Accessibility features in Horizon Forbidden West,” PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/02/10/accessibility-features-in-horizon-forbidden-west/)
- Rebellion’s official Atomfall accessibility interview describes Exploration settings for Navigational Hints, Flagged Leads and Waypoint Markers, allowing different amounts of quest guidance. It also describes short hint text intended to reduce reading/cognitive load, plus contrast settings and large text scaling. [Rebellion, “Atomfall: How Rebellion Created Its Most Accessible Game to Date,” Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/04/10/atomfall-accessibility/)
- Sony’s official accessibility feature description for The Last of Us Part I lists adjustable HUD size/color/background, high-contrast display, subtitle size/color/background, speaker names/direction indicators and customizable difficulty. It documents concrete alternatives for visual and audio information; it is a single-player action-adventure example rather than an RPG design interview. [Naughty Dog, “The Last of Us Part I: full list of accessibility features,” PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/08/26/the-last-of-us-part-i-full-list-of-accessibility-features/)
- Nintendo developers said seamless sky-to-ground travel lets players survey information from above, while noting that making new gameplay intuitive to newcomers was a specific design concern. They also stated that same-world continuation demanded considerable effort to add novelty without breaking the setting. [Nintendo, “Ask the Developer Vol. 9, Part 1”](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-chapter-1/); [Part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-9-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-part-3/)
- Sucker Punch’s VFX account says the team moved from hundreds of separately placed particle systems toward a camera-following biome-aware system, and that a small number of hand-placed elements remained for exploration guidance. [Sucker Punch, “How stunning visual effects bring Ghost of Tsushima to life”](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=345372)
- Canon states that Ascend targets a focused main story of roughly 200–250 hours and an explorer pace of roughly 600–800 hours, with SSS and the secret ending at 1,000+ hours. It also defines the game as offline-first and single-player, with in-game days and no real-world-time deadlines or online-gated consequences. [AETHWYRD Canon.md](../../Canon.md) (project source; governs design constraints, not external research)

### Inferences
- **Player-facing principle:** frequent combat needs rhythm, not constant escalation. Alternate authored combat clusters with safe social moments, navigation, investigation and quiet traversal. Let players prepare and choose when to enter optional danger; avoid respawning/return-trip combat that turns every revisit into combat tax.
- **Player-facing principle:** support long sessions and long gaps between sessions with a journal of active leads, concise “story so far” or quest summaries, visible next-step options, and the ability to lower combat pressure or raise guidance without losing progress. These are design inferences from official examples, not a universal prescription.
- **Player-facing principle:** environmental information needs redundant forms. A landmark may be visually distinctive, but also support a map/journal clue, contrast or shape distinction, subtitle/caption or directional audio option, and direct marker setting. Do not encode essential clues only by hue, distant fine detail, or an audio-only cue.
- **Player-facing principle:** allow players to set guidance independently from difficulty and content choice: natural cues, broad search areas, direct route, or no active guidance. Keep essential progression accessible in each mode; the game’s mystery should come from authored uncertainty, not inaccessible controls or hidden UI state.
- **Small-team scope risk:** Ascend’s 40 surface regions, 5 Underworld layers, 120-floor labyrinth, thousands of NPCs, 680 monster entries and 1,000-hour target are canon ambitions, not evidence of sustainable content production. A tiny team cannot safely make every region and return visit bespoke. First validate one complete region loop—lead, clue, traversal, frequent combat, optional detour, reward, return-state change—then scale with shared content schemas and strong authored exceptions.
- **Small-team scope risk:** too many simultaneous systems (daily boards, ecology, sieges, rumors, relationship state, hidden discoveries, combat, classes) can make each return visit unreadable and multiply regression testing. Keep the player-facing receipt short and explainable; test high-risk combinations rather than generating many low-value flags.
- **Small-team scope risk:** accessibility parity can fail if marker-free play is the only “authentic” option or if direct guidance is an afterthought. Build and test guidance settings, readable text, cue redundancy, camera/motion options and scalable combat assistance in the first representative slice.
- **Online scope:** nothing in these findings requires online play. Canon’s premium offline-first promise should remain complete offline. Optional platform features such as cloud saves, achievements, photo export or community sharing are separate platform conveniences; assess engineering/support cost and privacy implications before adopting them, and never attach progression or discovery rewards to them.

### Gaps
- The sources do not establish a proven ideal ratio of combat to quiet exploration, a maximum session length, or a pacing formula for a 200–1,000-hour game.
- The accessibility examples are feature descriptions, not controlled evidence that one option set covers all players or all disability needs. Ascend would need accessibility-informed playtesting.
- No official source reviewed gives labor estimates for reproducing large-RPG world density with a tiny team. Scope guidance here is an inference from Ascend’s canonical content scale and the cited production accounts.
- Online platform features are outside the core exploration loop and not evaluated as recommendations here. Any optional addition would need a separate cost and privacy assessment.

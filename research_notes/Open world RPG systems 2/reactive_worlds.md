# Reactive worlds for a two-person RPG team

## How can 2,500 named NPCs feel alive without simulating or authoring everything?

### Takeaway

Ascend should separate *being named* from *being fully simulated*: every named person can have identity, home, role, relationships, and a persistent small memory record, while only nearby/high-priority people run rich schedules and reactions. This is not an untested fantasy: Warhorse’s 2026 GDC session describes nearly 2,400 NPCs in KCD2, with simulation LODs and early limits as core lessons; it does not mean each NPC has an individual quest arc.

### Cited Findings

- At GDC Festival of Gaming, AI programmer Petr Smrcek described KCD2 as having nearly 2,400 NPCs, about half concentrated in one city, and said Warhorse introduced AI simulation levels of detail to control frame time and memory while keeping scripting largely unaware of the LOD. The session takeaway names early limitation-setting, threading, moving standardized logic into code, and stripping detail according to use case. (March 10, 2026, [GDC session listing](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/supporting-thousands-of-npcs-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/915120); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035718/))
- Warhorse’s GDC session gives an important distinction: the nearly 2,400 figure is total NPCs on the map, with roughly half in one city; it is not a claim that every resident is fully active at once. Its stated approach is AI LOD and resource limits. ([GDC session listing](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/supporting-thousands-of-npcs-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/915120), March 10, 2026.)
- Warhorse’s earlier public explanation of KCD’s AI described NPC daily preferences, behavior supplied contextually by the world, interruptions, perception, and different reactions to a corpse in a town versus a battlefield. A Warhorse AI programmer also described a role-based dialogue system: dialogue is authored for reusable roles from generic to specific, because separate dialogue for every individual would be infeasible. These are older first-game developer materials, useful as design lineage, not proof of KCD2’s exact implementation. ([Warhorse forum interview with AI programmer Michal Bartoň](https://forum.kingdomcomerpg.com/t/warhorse-studios-weekly-torch/29593/82), May 12, 2017; [developer interview excerpt on the AI system](https://forum.kingdomcomerpg.com/t/new-interview-with-many-technic-details-ai-cryengine/28624), 2016.)
- Bethesda’s Skyrim Radiant Story experience included explicit limits: job quests were restricted to roles that fit them; custom lines by voice type helped avoid mechanical repetition; the team reduced how often some reactions occurred; and it shifted some effort back to handcrafted content after finding too much procedural story felt flat. This account is a developer lecture recap/interview, not a current Bethesda technical document. ([GamesBeat, “Bethesda’s Nesmith reflects on the difficult birth of Skyrim’s Radiant Story system,” January 27, 2012](https://gamesbeat.com/bethesdas-nesmith-reflects-on-the-difficult-birth-of-skyrims-radiant-story-system/).)
- BioWare’s GDC 2015 account says Dragon Age: Inquisition’s team found authored discrete narrative content scaled poorly when used alone, while standalone systems had disconnected impact; the practical answer was an intentional middle ground. ([GDC Vault, “Worlds Collide: Combining Story and Systems in Dragon Age: Inquisition,” GDC 2015](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022377/Worlds-Collide-Combining-Story-and).)
- Skyrim design lead Bruce Nesmith later described Radiant Story as a tool rather than an end state: overuse made the game feel flat, and teams must choose where limited time and staff go. This is a developer interview, rather than a Bethesda publication. ([GamesBeat, July 4, 2018](https://gamesbeat.com/skyrim-director-todd-howard-why-triple-a-games-are-better-when-you-dont-play-it-safe/).)

### Inferences

- **Use four simulation tiers (proposal, tune through profiling):**
  1. **Tier A — nearby/scene-critical (roughly 20–60 at a time):** full navigation, schedule transitions, perception, combat/crime reactions, conversation state, and short-lived memory. Includes the player’s current scene and quest-critical actors.
  2. **Tier B — same settlement/region (roughly 100–400):** coarse schedule blocks and destination/role state; resolve relevant events through simple rules and summaries, not per-frame perception.
  3. **Tier C — distant named residents:** clock-driven state transitions (home/work/public place), event-triggered state updates, and persistent identity/relationships; no continuous pathfinding or observation.
  4. **Tier D — background population:** aggregated counts and settlement conditions; no individual named entity unless promoted into the player’s local experience.
  These counts are starting budgets for a small team, not sourced industry limits. Profile on target hardware and adjust.
- Let every one of the 2,500 names share data structures and authored role templates. Invest bespoke writing in a much smaller cast (perhaps 30–60 anchors), with another 150–300 memorable residents receiving a short personal detail, relationship or local concern. The remainder can still be recognizable through name, occupation, home, faction, visual motif, and a town-specific line pool. Those ranges are proposed content budgets, not published benchmarks.
- Treat simulation LOD as a gameplay contract: lower tiers must preserve outcomes players could later check (at work, injured, moved, fearful), while omitting intermediate movement and moment-to-moment thought. A person should not teleport into a contradiction because their detailed AI was asleep.
- Reuse one schedule grammar (home, work, travel, social/rest, emergency) with authored role profiles and location calendars. Put deviations into temporary patches such as “sheltering after the flood” or “avoids the market after the threat,” then expire them or resolve them to a durable state.
- Use an Ascend-appropriate emotional register: routines should communicate care, craft, kinship, recovery, and small joys as often as danger. Simulation is an opportunity to make a hopeful world feel inhabited, not just to amplify surveillance and punishment.

### Gaps

- The public GDC session description provides the headline count and categories of optimization, but full talk details may require GDC access; the exact LOD cadence, update frequencies, memory budgets, and thread model are not established by the public listing.
- Public developer materials located here do not establish a directly comparable runtime NPC count for Skyrim, Fallout 4, Avowed, or the Dragon Age titles. Do not infer such counts from how busy a town appears.
- The proposed tier counts depend on Ascend’s engine, target platforms, map density, and whether 2,500 means unique resident records, active AI agents, or visible named NPCs. Treat the numbers as hypotheses until these are specified and profiled.

## What scope of witness, memory, dialogue callback, quest reactivity, and town response creates meaningful consequence cheaply?

### Takeaway

Make reactivity *local, legible, and layered*: a small set of witnesses should drive immediate consequences; short-term memory should expire while important facts persist as compact tags; and dialogue should acknowledge the player at role, settlement, and character levels. Pair systemic consequences with a few hand-authored moments so a choice feels personal without multiplying every quest into a full branching tree.

### Cited Findings

- Obsidian’s Avowed developers describe choices across short-term, medium-term, and long-term horizons: dialogue can reveal information or change a quest approach; quest outcomes can carry through the quest; larger choices can affect multiple quests or the whole game. Game Director Carrie Patel said consequences should feel earned and be foreshadowed for major choices, with iteration and internal testing. ([Xbox Wire, “How Avowed Lets You Choose Your Own Adventure with Incredible Freedom,” January 24, 2025](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/01/24/avowed-choose-your-own-adventure-incredible-freedom/).)
- The same Obsidian interview says consequence magnitude varies: not every action needs to affect the entire world. Developers spoke of believable effects on individuals, groups, settlements, and longer arcs, while preserving room for discovery. ([Xbox Wire, January 24, 2025](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/01/24/avowed-choose-your-own-adventure-incredible-freedom/).)
- Obsidian set Avowed in large defined zones rather than a single seamless open world, and the interview explicitly connects bounded player location to its ability to offer detailed choices and consequences. ([Xbox Wire, January 24, 2025](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/01/24/avowed-choose-your-own-adventure-incredible-freedom/).)
- Larian’s official pre-release Baldur’s Gate 3 Q&A says the world responds to choices and character identity, but also frames systemic rules and multiple creative solutions as a core route to reactivity. ([Larian Studios Q&A, 2019, reproduced with the original Q&A context by GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/news/123520-baldur-s-gate-iii-larian-studios-ama.html); [Larian’s Reddit Q&A archive](https://www.reddit.com/r/baldursgate/comments/c4q87g/larian_studios_ama/).)
- PlayStation’s official BG3 article reports that character background, class, and ancestry affect situational dialogue and reactions, and gives a concrete example: the Duergar subclass had over 200 tailored dialogue responses. That is a single subclass in a large, well-resourced production; it is evidence of a focused reactivity budget, not a small-team baseline. ([PlayStation Blog, September 14, 2023](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/09/14/how-your-choices-in-baldurs-gate-3s-character-creator-carry-through-your-entire-playthrough/).)
- The GDC 2025 Dragon Age: Inquisition talk describes the design tradeoff between bespoke narrative and disconnected standalone systems, supporting a hybrid approach rather than maximal simulation or maximal branching. ([GDC Vault, GDC 2015](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022377/Worlds-Collide-Combining-Story-and).)
- A GDC 2026 Owlcat talk description notes that large branching RPGs can accumulate hundreds or thousands of narrative variables, and presents hierarchical “Etude” states that activate/deactivate while managing conflicts as an alternative to ad hoc global flags. This is a useful contemporary primary-source signal about state organization, though Owlcat’s scale differs from Ascend’s. ([GDC Vault, “Etudes and Actors: Owlcat’s Tooling for Highly Branched Narrative,” GDC 2026](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035684/).)
- Warhorse’s older AI explanation says NPC perception can react to corpses and crime, and notes that an NPC may retain an interrupted task for later. It illustrates event perception plus persistent follow-up, not an unlimited world-wide witness network. ([Warhorse developer interview excerpt, 2016](https://forum.kingdomcomerpg.com/t/new-interview-with-many-technic-details-ai-cryengine/28624).)

### Inferences

- **Witness scope:** On a crime or public act, query only entities in the immediate perceptual area and relevant state (line of sight, audibility, allegiance, attention, schedule). Create a small event record with actor, event type, place, time, and confidence. Nearby witnesses may remember/report it; distant residents learn it only through a defined social route (guard report, household, faction bulletin, rumor carrier). Avoid omniscient global “town knows” updates.
- **Memory scope:** Give each NPC a compact ring buffer for a few recent local events (proposal: 3–5 entries, expiring by time or relevance) plus durable relationship/faction facts (for example, “helped my sister,” “seen stealing,” “trusted healer”). Let rumors be lossy, delayed, and sometimes uncertain. Keep quest facts in quest state, not duplicated in each NPC’s permanent memory.
- **Callbacks:** Organize authored callback lines as layered pools: (a) role/occupation, (b) settlement condition or shared event, and (c) a character-specific line for major relationships/quests. Proposed budget: 1–3 callbacks for a major public event per relevant role, 1–2 highly specific callbacks per anchor NPC per major quest, and a short generic pool for other residents. Gate them with cooldowns and priority so repeated conversations do not recite every flag. The numbers are an Ascend proposal, not developer-published counts.
- **Quest affordances:** Make each quest recognize a small set of reusable affordance categories—combat, stealth, persuasion, knowledge/skill, item/tool, ally/faction, and “leave / return later.” One authored quest can expose several routes, each with a few outcome tags, then feed those tags into systemic role or settlement reactions. Do not promise every possible action has a bespoke branch; let consistent world rules provide many plausible approaches, and reserve unique writing for surprising or emotionally central solutions.
- **Low-cost narrative consequence:** Use a three-horizon ledger inspired by Avowed: immediate (NPC reaction, dialogue, access), local durable (quest/household/faction/settlement state), and campaign-scale (rare, heavily foreshadowed arc changes). Every consequential choice should land at least one observable receipt: changed routine, changed service/access, a short line, a changed location prop, a public notice, or a changed relationship. A tiny visible response is more valuable than a buried global variable.
- **Town response:** Model response as settlement state, not 2,500 independent conversations. A few variables can drive different affordances: safety, trust, scarcity, celebration/grief, faction pressure. Recompute at milestones/events; then affect patrol density, public availability, prices or services, ambient barks, gathering locations, and routines. Keep town reactions proportionate and recoverable so the world can answer harm while retaining the user’s warm and hopeful baseline.
- **Production practice:** Build quest conditions/outcomes from named, documented state tags; use hierarchical scopes (quest → settlement → campaign), explicit ownership, and expiration rules. Add a small editor/debug view that reveals which condition selected a line or outcome. Avowed’s GDC QA case and the Owlcat state-organization talk both suggest that tool-aware validation and coherent state structure become essential as branches accumulate. ([GDC 2025 Avowed QA session](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/avowed-branching-narratives-shifting-from-qa-testing-to-analyzing/907172); [GDC 2026 Owlcat session](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035684/).)
- A good two-person production rule: every new unique callback, witness exception, or branch must either reuse a documented pattern or replace another planned bespoke beat. This turns “reactivity” into a curated promise the team can actually honor.

### Gaps

- Public sources do not specify a safe universal witness radius, memory capacity, callback cap, or authored-branch count. All proposed numeric budgets above are design starting points, to be validated with Ascend’s content and engine.
- Larian and Bethesda examples disclose design philosophy more often than implementation detail. They do not provide a full data schema or staffing cost suitable for direct transplantation.
- The public Avowed source explains principles and examples but does not disclose the total number of reactive flags, dialogue variants, or authored outcomes.

## What should Ascend borrow from BG3, KCD2, Skyrim/Fallout, Avowed, and Dragon Age while honoring canon and a small-team scope?

### Takeaway

Borrow the *shape* of the craft, not the production scale: KCD2’s simulation LOD; Skyrim’s role-limited procedural selection and restraint; BG3’s concentrated identity-aware dialogue and systemic affordances; Avowed’s consequence horizons; and Dragon Age’s midpoint between bespoke narrative and detached simulation. Ascend’s canon and emotional tone should determine which facts persist and which responses matter.

### Cited Findings

- **KCD2 / Warhorse:** GDC 2026 documents nearly 2,400 NPCs and the use of AI LOD, early constraints, threading, standardized logic, and stripping unneeded detail. This directly supports scaling simulation with fidelity tiers. ([GDC session, March 10, 2026](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/supporting-thousands-of-npcs-in-kingdom-come-deliverance-kingdom-come-deliverance-ii/915120).)
- **KCD2 / branching dialogue:** At the 2025 Game Developers Session in Prague, Warhorse narrative designer Martina Daněk presented a talk on the hidden complexity of KCD2 branched dialogue, describing player influence over what NPCs tell them and the unexpected complications in branching dialogue. The event listing verifies talk title, speaker, and scope; detailed recommendations should wait for the talk itself. ([GDS Prague 2025 schedule](https://2025.gdsprague.cz/index.php?action=schedule), November 2025.)
- **Skyrim / Radiant Story:** Bruce Nesmith’s developer account describes constraining radiant jobs to appropriate roles, using voice-type-specific dialogue, dampening overly frequent reactions, and increasing custom authored content when procedural repetition felt flat. ([GamesBeat, January 27, 2012](https://gamesbeat.com/bethesdas-nesmith-reflects-on-the-difficult-birth-of-skyrims-radiant-story-system/).)
- **Fallout:** The available developer interview reports that Bethesda refined Radiant Story through later Fallout games but used it less broadly there than in Skyrim, and Nesmith emphasizes the finite resource tradeoff and the flatness that can come from overusing procedural stories. This is a developer retrospective, not a detailed Fallout quest postmortem; keep claims about Fallout-specific mechanics modest. ([GamesBeat interview with Skyrim design lead Bruce Nesmith](https://press-box-pr-v3.webflow.io/news/bruce-nesmith-exclusive-skyrims-design-lead-on-where-he-would-set-the-elder-scrolls-6-and-what-to-expect-from-the-future-of-fallout), 2026; current page’s publication date should be checked against the publisher if formal bibliography precision is required.)
- **BG3 / Larian:** The official PlayStation Blog documents identity-aware dialogue callbacks, including over 200 Duergar-subclass responses, while Larian’s own Q&A describes player action as the heart of reactivity and systemic rules as a way to support creative solutions. ([PlayStation Blog, September 14, 2023](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/09/14/how-your-choices-in-baldurs-gate-3s-character-creator-carry-through-your-entire-playthrough/); [Larian Q&A archive, 2019](https://www.reddit.com/r/baldursgate/comments/c4q87g/larian_studios_ama/).)
- **Avowed / Obsidian:** Obsidian’s team gives a clear short-, medium-, and long-term consequence framework, stresses foreshadowing and earned outcomes, and ties bounded zones to the ability to author detailed choice. ([Xbox Wire, January 24, 2025](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/01/24/avowed-choose-your-own-adventure-incredible-freedom/).)
- **Dragon Age / BioWare:** BioWare’s GDC 2015 talk describes the failure modes of only hand-authored content or only systemic content and argues for combining them. ([GDC Vault, 2015](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022377/Worlds-Collide-Combining-Story-and).)
- **Dragon Age / authored world response:** Mike Laidlaw said that choices should not be offered if the team will trivialize them in the end; his interview describes the Inquisition’s central role as a narrative “wrapper” connecting many open-world actions to reputation and purpose. ([Stevivor interview with BioWare Creative Director Mike Laidlaw](https://stevivor.com/features/interviews/biowares-mike-laidlaw-dragon-age-inquisition/), 2014.)
- **Avowed / content QA:** GDC 2025’s session description says Obsidian’s QA analysts worked in the narrative/design tools and held joint quest-analysis sessions to identify defects before conventional in-game reports. This is a practical small-team lesson in reviewing branches as data, though the studio’s staffing context differs substantially. ([GDC 2025 session listing](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/avowed-branching-narratives-shifting-from-qa-testing-to-analyzing/907172).)

### Inferences

- **Original Ascend pattern — “the Hearth Ledger”:** Keep a compact authored ledger of meaningful public events and personal promises. Each entry records who was involved, where, its scale, its emotional valence, and whether it is known, rumored, resolved, or remembered. This is a design proposal, not a known feature of the cited games.
- **Canon-aware knowledge:** Separate *what happened* from *who knows it*. A canon event can be globally true while only participants, witnesses, and connected communities know it. For major canon beats, author a small whitelist of permitted interpretations and callback lines; avoid dynamically rewriting established history through generic systemic rumor.
- **Hopeful consequence arcs:** Let a town move from strain to repair through visible acts: restored routine, renewed gathering, repaired shared space, repaired trust, and a remembered kindness. This yields emotionally resonant downstream callbacks while using one settlement-state transition to serve many NPCs.
- **Use narrative “seams”:** Tie systemic outcomes to a few authored scenes at high-value transitions (the first day after an event, a settlement milestone, a reunion). The simulation supplies the ongoing texture; authored scenes make the consequence legible and emotionally particular.
- **Original low-cost promise pattern — “one promise, three echoes”:** For each major quest, write one specific acknowledgement from a directly involved character, one role/settlement-level echo that can reach many residents, and one environmental or routine change. This gives the player three scales of recognition without three new branches per NPC.
- **Build a curated reactivity map before content production:** List the 8–12 state families the game truly cares about (examples: trust, safety, scarcity, faction standing, public witness, personal promise, repaired/harmed, grief/celebration). For each family, define one immediate reaction, one persistent consequence, and one eventual recovery or closure rule. Freeze this vocabulary early and avoid uncontrolled one-off flags.
- **Canon and warmth guardrail:** When a state transition affects established lore, review it against a canon ledger. When it affects ordinary people, include a humane route forward where consistent with canon—repair, apology, restitution, mutual aid, or a quiet acceptance of consequences. Hope should not erase harm; it should make care and recovery observable.

### Gaps

- There is no public source establishing Ascend’s canon rules, target engine, intended map count, platform budget, or staffing beyond the user’s two-person constraint. Recommendations should be reconciled with those project materials before implementation.
- Official/public sources found for BG3, KCD2, Skyrim/Fallout, Avowed, and Dragon Age are uneven. KCD2 and Avowed have current, specific developer sources; Fallout-specific reactivity detail is sparse in the material found here. No claim is made that all requested titles expose comparable technical information.
- Several cited developer talks disclose design lessons rather than code, per-feature costs, or full production budgets. The proposed patterns should be treated as implementable hypotheses, then validated in a small vertical slice.

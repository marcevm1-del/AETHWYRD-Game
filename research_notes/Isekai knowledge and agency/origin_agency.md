# Character Origins That Add Role-Play Without Locking Builds

## What is directly documented by official sources?

### Takeaway
Shipped RPGs show a range of origin investment: *Dragon Age: Origins* gave each origin a substantial distinct prelude and later story reactivity; *Cyberpunk 2077* pairs a short opening with recurring lifepath dialogue recognition; *Starfield* treats background mainly as a modest mechanical start and occasional world recognition. They establish possible patterns, not a universally superior formula.

### Cited Findings
- BioWare/EA described six *Dragon Age: Origins* origin stories as unique locations, characters, and events, each with several hours of gameplay; the publisher said those choices influence story elements, dialogue, and plot branches. This was an announcement before release, so the described design is a pre-release claim, not by itself proof of the final shipped amount of content. [EA/BioWare announcement](https://news.ea.com/press-releases/press-releases-details/2009/BioWare-Reveals-All-Six-Dragon-Age-Origin-Stories/default.aspx)
- EA’s current *Dragon Age: Origins* product page describes the released game and its Grey Warden premise, but does not independently document detailed origin reactivity or origin-specific starting gear. [EA product page](https://www.ea.com/games/dragon-age/dragon-age-origins)
- A 2019 PlayStation Blog hands-on account described *Cyberpunk 2077*’s three lifepaths (Nomad, Street Kid, Corpo) as shaping V’s starting journey and perspective with Night City characters. The article is a pre-release hands-on preview, so treat its experiential claims as preview evidence. [PlayStation Blog preview](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=332566)
- CD Projekt RED’s post-release Patch 2.01 notes refer to a Corpo-lifepath dialogue option in the shipped *Phantom Liberty* quest “Balls to the Wall”; an official Patch 1.63 note says Padre recognizes Corpo and Nomad V in the intro holocall. These establish that at least some lifepath recognition and dialogue persisted in released content. [Patch 2.01](https://www.cyberpunk.net/en/news/49231/patch-2-01); [Patch 1.63](https://www.cyberpunk.net/en/news/48364/patch-1-63-list-of-changes)
- Bethesda’s support page documents *Starfield* character creation: backgrounds grant Rank 1 starting skills, traits are optional, and background can be changed before confirming character creation. The separate official background list enumerates starting skill bundles. [Bethesda character creation](https://help.bethesda.net/app/answers/detail/a_id/61027/~/character-creation---starfield); [Bethesda background list](https://help.bethesda.net/app/answers/detail/a_id/61069/~/character-backgrounds---starfield)
- Bethesda’s post-release guide says *Starfield* has 21 backgrounds with three initial skills each; backgrounds can affect NPC perception, dialogue options, and quest/encounter solutions, and it frames starting choices as a direction players can later change through skill investment. [Bethesda, “Build Your Ideal Starship Captain”](https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/build-your-ideal-starship-captain-in-starfield)
- *Baldur’s Gate 3*’s official site documents seven hand-crafted Origin heroes with distinct traits, agendas, and outlooks, and says companions react to player decisions. This applies to authored Origin characters and companion relationships; the page does not establish that custom-player backgrounds receive equivalent bespoke relationship arcs. [Official BG3 site](https://baldursgate3.game/about/)
- Larian’s 2021 Early Access community update introduced background goals that award capped Inspiration points, usable to reroll checks. Because this is explicitly an Early Access update, it documents the announced/then-current feature and should not be taken alone as a complete account of the final release implementation. [Larian Community Update 13](https://forums.larian.com/ubbthreads.php?Number=779663&ubb=showthreaded)

### Inferences
- Origins can affect dialogue, opening context, practical starting capabilities, and replay in different proportions; none of the examples proves that every origin needs a unique main quest or exclusive ending.
- The *Cyberpunk* evidence supports a relatively narrow content strategy: establish a distinctive start, then let past identity resurface selectively in dialogue/world recognition while the main adventure converges.
- *Starfield* illustrates how a background can be mechanically meaningful at the start while remaining a starting advantage: if the same skill progression remains open to all players, the background need not define a permanent build.
- Large multi-hour preludes, as announced for *Dragon Age: Origins*, create a strong replay promise but imply substantially more authored locations, characters, quest logic, and QA than occasional reactive lines.

### Gaps
- Official sources reviewed here do not give a verified count of shipped lifepath/background-specific dialogue lines, quests, or relationship scenes for these games.
- Official sources do not provide comparative replay rates, player sentiment, or production cost per origin.
- Starting inventory differences are not sufficiently documented by these primary sources for a reliable item-by-item comparison.

## What is an inference for Ascend?

### Takeaway
Ascend should let the chosen old-world job color what the protagonist notices, knows, or says, while leaving combat builds and essential quest access available to everyone. Origins can carry the larger differences in the new-life situation; old-world jobs should stay as a lightweight role-play layer on top of Canon’s remembered Earth life.

### Cited Findings
- Canon states that the protagonist remembers their former life and that they died, does not know the cause, cannot return to Earth, and that old-world jobs provide small practical perspectives and dialogue while the new life in Aethwyrd carries the story. [AETHWYRD Canon.md](../../Canon.md)
- Canon also sets a full creator, multiple origin premise, premium single-player format, offline-first availability, long 1,000+ hour progression, and respec protection: learned skills and character level are never lost on class change. [AETHWYRD Canon.md](../../Canon.md)
- *Cyberpunk 2077*’s post-release patch notes demonstrate occasional recognition tied to lifepath in shipped content, while Bethesda documents background-linked dialogue and quest solutions alongside later player skill development. [CDPR Patch 1.63](https://www.cyberpunk.net/en/news/48364/patch-1-63-list-of-changes); [Bethesda Starfield guide](https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/build-your-ideal-starship-captain-in-starfield)
- BioWare/EA’s *Dragon Age: Origins* announcement is evidence for a high-replay, high-content approach in which each origin begins differently and later changes dialogue or plot branches; its “several hours” claim was pre-release. [EA/BioWare announcement](https://news.ea.com/press-releases/press-releases-details/2009/BioWare-Reveals-All-Six-Dragon-Age-Origin-Stories/default.aspx)

### Inferences
- Treat two axes separately in the creator: **Aethwyrd origin** answers “what immediate social situation and opening brought me here?”; **Earth job/memory** answers “what practical lens do I bring?” This preserves room for multiple Earth jobs across origins without multiplying every origin-job combination into separate branches.
- For old-world jobs, favor small, optional affordances: a selectable observation, a contextual knowledge prompt, a little alternate phrasing, or a journal note. Keep the main response/quest continuation available through ordinary dialogue, exploration, companions, or another known clue route.
- Knowledge should improve interpretation or convenience rather than provide exclusive solutions: an engineer can recognize a principle or suggest a safe experiment, but anyone can solve the same problem by finding local evidence or asking a knowledgeable NPC. A nurse can notice triage priorities without being the only route to save someone.
- The effect should remain legible in a very long save: revisit a small authored set of high-value situations (guild paperwork, illness, crops, machines, festivals, lore) rather than attempting a global simulation of every job in all regions.
- Use player-selected options rather than automatic personality assumptions. “You recognize…” is usually safer than forcing “you believe/choose…”; the player can acknowledge the memory, ignore it, or frame it differently.
- Make job advantages modest and either non-combat or recoverable. A lasting combat/stat advantage risks turning role-play into a build tax; job-specific knowledge should not be the only source for essential progression, rare class access, major relationships, or endings.
- Distinguish a unique line from a unique branch: one short line that returns to a shared response can still validate identity, while branching state, bespoke scenes, and enduring relationship changes multiply implementation and testing burden.
- Replay value can come from recognition and interpretation even when the critical path converges. Give players a reason to notice a different opening, social treatment, contextual clue, or side-story beat, but do not imply that a player must replay for an essential class, companion, or ending.
- A simple authoring rule for Ascend: every background-gated option must be tagged as (a) flavor/knowledge, (b) alternate approach to the same outcome, or (c) genuinely exclusive content. Keep (c) rare and never make it the sole route to a mandatory milestone.

### Gaps
- Canon does not yet define the final list of selectable origins, whether each gets a prologue of equal length, or how often old-world jobs should recur. The origin table in `06_Races_Origins/races_and_origins.md` is draft material and should not override Canon.
- Canon does not settle whether old-world jobs grant mechanical perks. The current draft origin document proposes several perks, but the research recommendation to keep these small/recoverable is an inference, not a settled rule.
- No estimate can be made from public official sources for Ascend’s dialogue authoring, localization, voice recording, save-state, or QA cost.

## What tradeoffs or risks matter for a tiny team?

### Takeaway
The main production risk is combinatorial content: origins × jobs × race × class × relationship state × quest choices. For a tiny team and a thousand-hour game, use a small number of high-signal authored reactions, shared quest outcomes, and data-driven tags; avoid promising hours of origin-exclusive content across every combination.

### Cited Findings
- *Dragon Age: Origins*’ publisher described six origins with several hours of unique gameplay each and additional story/dialogue/plot effects. This is a clear example of a high-content bar, but its published statement is pre-release and does not quantify team cost. [EA/BioWare announcement](https://news.ea.com/press-releases/press-releases-details/2009/BioWare-Reveals-All-Six-Dragon-Age-Origin-Stories/default.aspx)
- CD Projekt RED’s released patch notes show lifepath-specific dialogue checks still require quest-specific correctness and bug fixes: Patch 2.01 fixed a repeatable Corpo dialogue option that could block progress in a *Phantom Liberty* quest. [CDPR Patch 2.01](https://www.cyberpunk.net/en/news/49231/patch-2-01)
- Bethesda documents 21 *Starfield* backgrounds and bundles each with three starting skills; its guide further describes background recognition and alternate dialogue/quest solutions. [Bethesda background list](https://help.bethesda.net/app/answers/detail/a_id/61069/~/character-backgrounds---starfield); [Bethesda guide](https://bethesda.net/en-US/news/build-your-ideal-starship-captain-in-starfield)
- Larian’s official BG3 page advertises seven hand-crafted Origin heroes and extensive reactivity, demonstrating the authored-character approach. This is not a direct cost statement and should not be interpreted as a feasible benchmark for a two-person team. [Official BG3 site](https://baldursgate3.game/about/)

### Inferences
- For Ascend, each extra origin-job combination creates more than text: conditions, save flags, testing paths, localization, possible voice work, and continuity checks against the canon secret order. Prefer reusable response fragments with shared follow-through.
- Aim for a “small but noticeable” job footprint: a few authored prologue beats per major origin; several recurring job-aware dialogue/knowledge moments over the main story; occasional optional side content; minimal or no job-only combat stats. This is a proposed scope target, not a fact established by the cited games.
- Use tags such as `earth_job.engineer`, `origin.transported`, and `origin.summoned` to gate reactions independently. Compose them only where both are narratively relevant; do not author the full Cartesian product.
- Prioritize reactions by narrative value and replay visibility: opening, first guild/academy/medical/craft encounter, companion bond moments, and interpretation of historical/technical evidence. A generic line repeated everywhere can make the system feel cosmetic; a few memorable moments are more credible.
- Keep relationship outcomes based on what the player actually does and says, with job background offering context or an optional opening. If a job automatically grants trust or romance progress, it can read as a hidden relationship advantage and make other choices feel suboptimal.
- Avoid exclusive skill unlocks or permanent best-in-slot items for Earth jobs. If a modest starting tool is appropriate, make it replaceable early and ensure comparable value across jobs; otherwise starting inventory becomes a balance comparison players may feel compelled to optimize.
- For offline-first play, all origin/job conditions and content should be local, deterministic, and included with the premium game. No online checks, rotating content, timed events, or remote services are needed or appropriate under Canon.
- Define acceptance criteria for each job reaction: it acknowledges relevant experience; it never assumes the character’s personality or exact Earth biography beyond what the player selected; it does not block essential content; and it works with all supported creator combinations.

### Gaps
- Public primary sources do not disclose the number of writers, QA resources, localization budget, or internal branch-maintenance practices for the comparison titles.
- The exact Ascend creator matrix and whether appearance/race/origin/job choices can be changed after starting are unresolved; these choices determine how much combination testing is needed.
- Voice acting scope is undecided in Canon. Voice acting every background-specific line would materially increase cost; no figure is available from public sources.

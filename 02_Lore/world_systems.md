## 1. The Laws of Mana (hard magic)
Sanderson's second law guides this section: the limits matter more than the powers. Players should be able to predict what magic can do, so magic that solves problems in the story follows these rules.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Source** | **Aether**, the substance of the Weave, pools along **ley lines**. It is plentiful but **locally finite**: heavy drawing *thins* a place (lamps dim, crops slow, spells weaken) for days ("ley-thinning"). This is why mage towers are spaced apart and why battles between mages ruin farmland. |
| **Who can use it** | Every soul filters a little Aether into **mana**. Status measures the filter (MP) and lets it grow with training. Monsters have no filter and absorb raw Aether, which is why they mutate and grow by eating cores. |
| **How** | Mana is shaped by **chanting** in Old Weave-tongue. Mastery shortens it: full chant → short chant → skill name only → chantless (`the_big_upgrade.md`). A chant needs focus: **being staggered breaks it** (this matches combat poise). |
| **Everyday cost** | Spent mana comes back with rest, food and proximity to ley lines (inn rest bonuses; towns on ley lines recover faster ⚙). |
| **Overcasting** ⚙ | Casting with an empty pool burns HP and leaves a **Weave-scar**: faintly glowing veins that fade over days. Veterans carry permanent ones, and a mage's scars are read like a soldier's medals. |
| **Raw Aether** | Eating cores raw or sleeping inside an Aether well brings on **Aether sickness**: fever, then changes to the body. This is the real danger behind the Predation heresy. The Predator path survives it because Status filters what is devoured. |

**What magic cannot do** (tracked rules; any exception must be foreshadowed):
1. **Create Aether.** Magic only moves and shapes it. There is no free energy anywhere in Aethwyrd.
2. **Make lasting gold or cores.** Transmuted metal reverts within a day (*"wizard's gold"*), which is why coins and cores keep their value.
3. **Undo death once the soul's lantern goes out.** Revival works only within minutes, through Morrowyn's mercy (this matches the existing revive chance). The Lantern Seal is the single exception, and it cost a Hero's soul.
4. **Rewrite a Status.** No spell changes a rank, a level or a Unique Skill; only growth does. *(⚠️ only the System, and its harvest, can.)*
5. **Open a road to Earth.** Summoning is one-way (Canon §4).
6. **Command a Status-bearer's will** for more than moments. Charm and fear break on any hit. Lady Nocturne's disguise is illusion and imitation, not control.

**The soft edges:** these stay mysterious and must never conveniently solve the main plot.
- **Divine magic** depends on a god's favour, built through Devotion. Gods can refuse, and sometimes do.
- **Spirit magic** depends on Bond and on a spirit's mood. Spirits keep their own counsel.
- **Forbidden magic** (Space, Time, Void) touches the Weave directly. The Church says this is blasphemy. ⚠️ The truth: each use *frays* the Weave, and the System must stitch the damage. That is the Church's real reason for hunting it.

**Ley lines in the story.** Towns grow on ley lines, and the old roads follow them. Since the **1019 flicker**, the lines pulse unevenly: core-lamps stutter, monsters migrate toward strong nodes, and the swollen monsters there become **Tyrants** ⚙ (this explains why Tyrants appeared across the world at once). ⚠️ The flicker is Vesper pulling on the Weave as it waxes.

## 2. Why Goods Move (the trade web)
Each region's own trade is in the gazetteer (`powerAndTrade`). This section shows **how they depend on each other**, which tells us why the world fights.

**Three currencies.**
- **Coin:** minted by Aldmere's banks, from gold carried north along Zaruma's gold road.
- **Cores:** the fuel of every lamp, forge and rune engine. In practice they are a second currency, weighed in *carats*.
- **Favours:** in fey and spirit lands, people pay with names, memories and years (Everdusk, the Kitsune Isles, the True-Name Isles).

**The core loop.** Dangerous wild regions produce cores, and safe heartlands burn them. The Guild buys cores at the **Charter rate** that Kaede set ⚙, which makes it the world's real energy supplier as well as a hiring hall. The **Merchants' Consortium** wants that rate abolished; cheap cores in the south and dear ones in the north would make the Consortium rich.

| Who | Needs | Gets it from | Chokepoint / threat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aldmere (banks, wheat, wool) | Gold, cores, steel | Zaruma's gold road; wild-region cores; Iron Hold | **Ashgrave** shuts the roads at night |
| The cold north (Frostveil, Whitewater Reach, Bastion) | Grain, iron, timber | The south, by longship and caravan | **Ysgrath** breaks the sea lanes; Bastion's iron convoys |
| Iron Hold (Ashen-seam ore, mithril) | Food, timber, buyers | Aldmere, Sylvaran (under the strained Treaty of Ash and Leaf) | **Skarn** wants the forges themselves |
| Desert and gold road (Sunscar, Zaruma, Zarvan) | Water, timber | Oases, aqueducts, Dunewhale Drift | **Mansacor** eats the gold; the **Colossus** shrinks Qadira |
| The East (Hoshizora steel, Shen-Lu manuals and tea) | Metals, cores, open ports | Goldmere and the Consortium | Kurogane, Wuxiang |
| Port Goldmere (salvage, the Consortium) | Wrecks, which is to say other people's losses | The wreck-tide | **Krakenmother Ys** is both the threat and the supply |
| The Underworld (Umbravel, Hollow Sea) | Surface food, light | Mana crystals and silk traded upward | Vel'thara collects her "rent" |
| Fanghold steppe | Water | The Deep Well, the only one in a week's ride | **Varrick Kade** trades water for people |

**Design hook ⚙:** liberating a region lifts a price penalty on its exports everywhere (a cheaper Iron Hold means cheaper steel in Brindlewick). Players then *see* the web change.

## 3. Three Generations (650 → 1024 SC)
The deep ages are already written. This section covers the history that living people remember. ⚙ The names and dates are new; the anchor years 922, 1003 and 1019 are existing canon.

**Grandparents' generation (~940–975 SC): legends and old wounds**
- **The Grey Harvest (951–953)** ⚙: a wheat blight empties Aldmere's granaries. Border baronies "lease" beastkin labour to save their fields, and the slavery Hero #2 abolished creeps back under another name. This is the root of the Choir of Chains and of Varrick Kade's trade. Granaries still keep one sealed "Grey Sack" that is never opened.
- **The Cult takes root:** founded in 922, it spreads in the blight's aftermath with one message: *the gods let you starve.*
- **Old Hollis's war stories**, Lantern Night songs about the hungry years, and the beastkin distrust of barons all date from this era.

**Parents' generation (~975–1000 SC): what shaped today**
- **The Marches War (978–981)** ⚙: Aldmere and the Imperium of Vharlen fight over the Strife Marches. It ends in a truce, not a peace, so the baronies still change flags every season and mercenary companies never went home.
- **The Floor-Forty Breach (986)** ⚙: monsters climb out of the Labyrinth into Aldmere City for one night. Since then, Guild Emergency Calls are law, and the Labyrinth gate has a garrison.
- **Gearholm's engine boom (990s)** ⚙: cheap cores drive the clockwork city upward, and the Undercity fills with the people the engines replaced.
- The parents of today's companions fought, fled or profited in these years, which gives each companion's personal arc somewhere to start.

**The present (1000–1024 SC)**
- **1003:** Archbishop Valerian Crowe takes the Church's highest seat (canon).
- **1019:** the ley flicker begins (canon). Core prices double, smugglers thrive and Tyrants rise in region after region ⚙.
- **1024:** Vesper waxes early, the Lantern Seal cracks, and you arrive (canon).

**Iceberg plan.** Act I shows only the Grey Sack and Hollis's stories. Act II brings the flicker's prices and the Floor-Forty garrison. Act III ties the Marches War to the Choir of Chains. ⚠️ Act V reveals that the flicker is Vesper.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Holy Church of Lumina** (Crowe; the Inquisition; the Veilborn Registry) | Order; summonings that go to plan | Forbidden magic, "System questioning", rogue Veilborn | Law, licences, the Inquisition | Crowe knows *half* the harvest truth |
| **Adventurers' Guild** (Grand Guildmaster Celestine) | Neutrality, the Charter, a supply of rising adventurers | Being made a tool of the crown or the Church | Ranks, contracts, the Charter rate | Celestine knows Adrian's story and carries the guilt |
| **Crown of Aldmere** | Its heartland and its banks | Vharlen, a hungry nobility, a Hero it can't control | Gold, marriages, the Cathedral | Little; it trusts the Church |
| **Imperium of Vharlen** | Security on the demon frontier | Being abandoned at the wall | A drilled citizenry, its Bastion-style walls | Real Mina is held beneath it, unknown to most |
| **Merchants' Consortium** (Port Goldmere) | Free core prices, the sea lanes | Guild price control, the wreck-tide failing | Credit, salvage rights, bribes | Nothing; it profits from chaos either way |
| **Shadow Syndicate** ("Nobody") | The space between laws | Order of any kind | Smuggling, blackmail, people-trade (in places) | Rumours, and it sells them |
| **Mages' Academy** (est. 512) | Knowledge, safe magic | Inquisition raids, ley-thinning | Licences, research, duels | Suspects the Weave is fraying |
| **Red Moon Cult** (the Seven Choirs) | "Freedom from the gods' leash" | Exposure | Charity masks, infiltration, despair | ⚠️ its Prophet wants Vesper for himself |

**Friction that drives quests:** Guild vs Church (who licenses Veilborn) · Consortium vs Guild (the Charter rate) · Aldmere vs Vharlen (the unfinished Marches truce) · Iron Hold vs Sylvaran (the Treaty of Ash and Leaf) · Cult vs everyone, from inside each power.

## 5. Class and Mobility (three ladders)
Aethwyrd ranks people on three ladders at once, and the tension comes from where they disagree.
1. **Birth:** nobility, knights, freemen, serfs (in Aldmere's feudal law). It changes slowly, through marriage, war or the crown.
2. **Status:** **Appraisal Day at 12** sets a child's expectations for life. A serf's child who shows a Unique Skill gets scouted by the Academy or the Church (or quietly by the Inquisition), and a noble heir with a weak Status is a family's private shame.
3. **Guild rank:** under the Wanderer's Charter, anyone can climb, and an S-Rank outranks most nobles socially. Rank is the fastest ladder in the world, and the deadliest: the Hall of Cards is full.

**Veilborn break all three ladders.** They have no birth here, their growth has no ceiling and their rank climbs fast. This explains the mixed welcome described in `life_in_aetheria.md`: heroes in legend, threats to the order in person. ⚠️ It is also exactly why they are summoned.

**Hooks ⚙:** a baron's daughter hides her low appraisal behind hired adventurers · a beastkin family saves for a child's Guild fee as its only way up · nobles buy "card-padding" (bought ranks) from corrupt examiners, a story for Garrick's line.

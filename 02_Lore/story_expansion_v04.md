# 02e — Story Expansion v0.4

**Core theme:** *Save the world, and decide what it's worth saving it from.* The threat is real: the Weave is unraveling. Every faction has its own answer to how to save it, and each answer costs something.

**Tone:** hopeful, warm, sincere. Joy comes from found family, festivals, small kindnesses and earned victories, not slapstick. **No fanservice.** The dark moments hit harder because the happy ones feel real.

## 4. The System's 8 Personalities (Q11–12)
The System **adapts to how you live**. Its personality shifts over time as you play (with a hidden score per type). Voice: **male or female**, chosen at creation.
| Personality | Grows when you… | Sample line |
|---|---|---|
| **Administrator** (default) | Play neutrally | *"Level up confirmed. Proceed."* |
| **Mentor** | Help people, do quests carefully | *"Well done. Rest now, you've earned it."* |
| **Companion** | Build bonds, camp often | *"Level up! They'd be proud of you, you know."* |
| **Archivist** | Read books, find lore, explore | *"New record! Did you know this ruin is 3,000 years old?"* |
| **Zealot** | High Devotion to any god | *"Praise be. The heavens witnessed your strength."* |
| **Warden** | Dark Karma, cruelty | *"Threat level of user: rising. Noted."* |
| **Duelist** | Seek strong enemies, hunt bosses | *"Finally, a worthy opponent. Don't disappoint me."* |
| **Fractured** | Forbidden magic, truth-seeking, Ezrath | *"Lev—el up. W̷h̷y̷ do you keep asking what I am?"* |
In Act V, the System's personality **decides how it reacts to the truth**: it may beg, help, resist, or ask you to free it.

- Each god has **unique requirements** too: Thorgrim needs masterwork crafts, Vex needs you to win a rigged game, Morrowyn needs you to guide lost spirits, Kaelthas needs you to never retreat from a Worthy fight.

- **Fourth-wall rule:** gods and the System speak to your **character**. Only two endings (*Unwoven Sky* and *Thirteenth Star*) briefly address **the player** directly.

## 6. Dragons: The Wyrmconclave (Q70)
- **The Wyrmconclave:** three **Ancient Dragons**, older than the Elder Dragons. They were born from the first threads before the Weave was finished and they judge the world. They sit in a hall outside time, reached only through the Sky Archipelago's highest island.
| Ancient Dragon | Aspect | Personality |
|---|---|---|
| **Solvoryn, the Dawnwyrm** | Aurelion's thread: order, memory | Stern judge; remembers every oath ever broken |
| **Nythraxa, the Duskwyrm** | Nyxara's thread: dream, change | Kind, strange, speaks in riddles |
| **Kaelumbra, the Twilight Arbiter** | The space between | Neutral; casts the deciding vote |
- The **Five Elder Dragons** (Ignivar, Glacirra, Zephyros, Terragon, Umbrael) answer to the Conclave. All five can be befriended or fought.
- The Conclave **judges your choices** at three points in the story; its verdict changes which endings are possible. You can even **fight the Conclave** (an optional super-boss trial; it's nearly impossible).

## 15. Monster Towns & Intelligence (Q66)
- Most monsters are animals or instinct-driven. **Intelligent, talking monsters** live mainly in **monster settlements**: goblin villages, lizardfolk towns, orc holds, the Arachne court, and the **Free Monster Commonwealth of Moraveil** (a monster nation you can help build, see World).
- Named monsters can become intelligent through evolution or Name-Giving.

## 16. Mystery Design Principles (Q61, research)
Based on how lore-driven games build mysteries:
- **Environment first:** ruins, murals, item descriptions and ghost memories tell the story; a ruined library tells you scholars lived there and studied what destroyed them. ([FromSoftware storytelling](https://amrsalehduat.substack.com/p/how-fromsoftware-tells-stories-without))
- **Players as detectives:** discovery gives players ownership of the story. ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/reclaiming-narrative-reviving-environmental-storytelling))
- **Two layers:** clear enough for casual players, deep enough for dedicated ones to keep finding meaning. ([Pixune](https://pixune.com/blog/environmental-storytelling-in-games/))
- Environmental storytelling as "arranging objects so they suggest a story." ([IntechOpen, quoting Henry Jenkins](https://www.intechopen.com/online-first/1225186))
- **Applied:** each Great Mystery has 10–30 scattered clues, a **Mystery Board** in the Journal (pin clues, connect them), and the truth can be solved **before** the story reveals it, for a unique reward and a special dialogue.

## 17. NPCs: ~2,500 Named, Each with a Backstory (Q65)
| Tier | Count | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** (companions, villains, trainers, rulers) | ~150 | Fully written arcs, voiced lines |
| **Major** (quest givers, shopkeepers, recurring) | ~600 | Hand-written backstory, schedule, 1–3 quests, relationships |
| **Townsfolk** | ~1,750 | Name, family, job, schedule, 3–5 sentence backstory, 1 secret, likes/dislikes, gossip lines |
- Townsfolk are authored as **data** (JSON); Claude writes them in batches per town, checked for consistency (family trees, town history).
- NPCs **react** to prosperity, war, your rank, and deaths in their families; some can die, move, marry, or become adventurers.

## 18. Presentation (Q76–80)
- Big moments: **cinematic cutscenes**. Normal talk: in-world dialogue with choices. Camp: VN-style portrait scenes.
- **Memory Reel:** the game silently captures key moments (first rank-up, companion recruitments, boss kills, weddings, losses) and plays them during the ending credits.
- **Anime Opening per Act:** a 90-second OP with a song, changing visuals (it updates to show the companions *you* have and the ones you lost).
- **Playable flashbacks** for companions and previous Heroes.
- **System Voice is the only narrator.**

## The story told to children

I set this down at Stiller spring-towns because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Stiller spring-towns the work of the day sits inside low houses on flexible frames. People eat corn cakes and bean paste and they talk about a shake that the locals do not apologize for the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Stiller spring-towns says one thing. A speaker at Quaking Lands says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Solenne: a golden scale, kept for oaths and the morning light. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim in the joints of the house. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## How this copy is used

I set this down at Stiller spring-towns because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Tamsin Ibarra of Quaking Lands keeps this book in the kitchen at Stiller spring-towns, where the houses are low houses on flexible frames. The everyday pot already knows corn cakes and bean paste. What follows is the version you can cook when a shake that the locals do not apologize for has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Thorgrim in the joints of the house. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Hedge Tea

*As cooked when Stiller spring-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a legal hedge herb
- honey
- water

If you cannot name the leaf, do not pour it for a guest. Steep it short. Honey after, so you can taste whether the leaf was kind.

## Millbrook Rabbit Pot

*As cooked when Stiller spring-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- one horned rabbit, jointed
- an onion
- a heel of bread
- thyme if the hedge allows
- water and salt

Brown the joints in a little fat. Add onion. Cover with water and the bread so the broth goes thick. Keep it under a boil. Eat with the spoons you have.

## The story told to children

I set this down at Fanghold because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Fanghold the work of the day sits inside felt yurts and horse-lines. People eat smoked meat and salted milk and they talk about raid dust on the steppe edge the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Fanghold says one thing. A speaker at nomad yurt camps says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Kaelthas named before a ride. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

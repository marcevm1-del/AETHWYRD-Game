## The charter, in a human voice

I set this down at Fox Shrine Isle because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Fox Shrine Isle the work of the day sits inside wooded stairs and a crater cove. People eat seaweed, sweet potato, and broth and they talk about a story that arrives by boat and leaves changed the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the Wanderer's Charter: no birth, race, or crown between a soul and its rising. A speaker at Fox Shrine Isle says one thing. A speaker at spirit inns says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. fox-lights that the shrine keeper will not explain. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## Words, learned in a room

At spirit inns you will hear 'card-burner' for someone who died at F, 'goblin-chewed' for a bad beating, 'silver-sick' for a C-rank who has stalled, and 'a brightlamp' for someone whose growth frightens the table. The nickname is not always a compliment. Do not use the death-words to be colorful. The Hall of Cards is a wall of people.

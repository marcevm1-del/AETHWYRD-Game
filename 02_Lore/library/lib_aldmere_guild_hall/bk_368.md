# The Law of Small Things
*Sorrel Fennimore, a magistrate of Thimblefolk Mushroom Town, in the form of questions people have asked her*

**Shelf:** Law notes  
**Region:** Mossdeep  
**In-world date:** 1012 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

*Q. If a big folk steps on my house, who pays?*

The big folk pays, and pays well, in honey and in apology. It does not matter whether the step was an accident. It does not matter whether the house was in the way. A house is a house. We have a schedule of fees, set by the council, and it is longer than you would like.

*Q. If a beetle eats my garden?*

The beetle's rider pays, and the beetle apologises, which it does by lying very still and looking ashamed. It is not an effective apology, but it is a sincere one.

*Q. If I borrow a needle and lose it?*

You owe the lender a needle. You owe the smith a needle. You owe the weaver a day of your time. It is the custom that a lost needle costs three, and I have never known a person to argue the arithmetic.

*Q. If I find something of the big folk's in the ferns?*

You leave it where it lies, and you tell the council. A big folk's dropped coin is a danger to the whole town. It is a wheel that will crush a house. Do not roll it. Do not touch it. Let the council decide.

*Q. Who judges the judges?*

I do not know. I have asked. The answer is generally tea.

## What I have learned

Our law is about scale. A thing that is a nuisance for the big folk is a calamity for us. A thing that is a small courtesy for us is a great kindness for them. Every dispute I have heard has come down to the same question: who is bigger, and who is kinder?

I try to give the same answer each time: it does not matter who is bigger, and the kinder person pays.

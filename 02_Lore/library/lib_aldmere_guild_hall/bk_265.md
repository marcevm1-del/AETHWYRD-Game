## The charter, in a human voice

I set this down at Ashira because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Ashira the work of the day sits inside orchards and doors left on the latch. People eat apple butter, cheese, and biscuits and they talk about a guest who arrived and did not speak of leaving the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the Wanderer's Charter: no birth, race, or crown between a soul and its rising. A speaker at Ashira says one thing. A speaker at Ashira Vale says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

## Words, learned in a room

At Ashira you will hear 'card-burner' for someone who died at F, 'goblin-chewed' for a bad beating, 'silver-sick' for a C-rank who has stalled, and 'a brightlamp' for someone whose growth frightens the table. The nickname is not always a compliment. Do not use the death-words to be colorful. The Hall of Cards is a wall of people.

# Potsherds from the Proving Halls
*Ysmay Dunlow, a copyist, writing at the Mages' Academy*

**Shelf:** Valdrisian fragment  
**Region:** Great Labyrinth (Floors 1-40)  
**In-world date:** 1005 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I am a copyist. I copy what other people have already found, and I add a note when I am not sure. That is all the Academy asks of me, and it is a good deal more than most scholars give.

These are three sherds that came up from the Great Labyrinth, one from each of the upper floors, and the lines I have copied from them. I do not read Old Weave-tongue well. I read it the way I read a friend's bad handwriting.

## The first sherd

A cup fragment, glazed green. The line along the rim reads, as near as I can make it, *"let the guest drink first."* The Academy's reader says it is a courtesy. I think it is a precaution, and I think the people who wrote it knew that was the same thing.

## The second sherd

A tile, baked hard. The surface has a chart that looks like a map of a road. There are marks beside it that may be a count of days. Beside those, a small drawing of a tower. The tower is thin and very tall.

The old empire of Valdrisia built towers, and the histories say they touched the clouds. I will not say this tile shows one. I will say it is the sort of tile a person would make who had seen one.

## The third sherd

A broken lamp, with a line burned into the base. It reads *"carry it down."* My reader says it is a line for a procession. I say it is a line for anyone who walks into the dark with a light in their hand.

## A caution

The Labyrinth did not make these. The Labyrinth swallowed them: it is a place that takes things in. The sherds came up because somebody carried them up. I do not know whose hands.

I note this because every scholar who has been to the Labyrinth tells me that it is an old thing with a long memory. I do not think that is true. I think it is an old thing with a good deal of other people's belongings.

Maelis, who keeps the dreamer's hours, may know more than I do. I write what I can see.

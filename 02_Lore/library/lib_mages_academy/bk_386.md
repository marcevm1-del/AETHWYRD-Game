## What a core is for

I set this down at longhouse steads because the Guild card was right about the danger and wrong about the cause. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At longhouse steads the work of the day sits inside turf longhouses and fjord wind. People eat smoked fish, sour cream, and rye and they talk about ice that sounds like a door the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a demonstration. A speaker at longhouse steads says one thing. A speaker at Skjaldheim says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Kaelthas in the weather, Morrowyn for the lost. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## Limits, said early

Street lamps burn core-light. Pumps run on small rune engines. This makes a town look effortless. It is not effortless. A core is a spent life of a monster, bought, sold, and argued over. A rune repeats what it was taught. It does not become wise, and it does not forgive a sloppy circle.

Divine magic is borrowed from the Eight through devotion. The Church licenses healers. An unlicensed healer is illegal even when the patient stands up afterward. That is a law about power, not a proof that the patient should have stayed down. Know which argument you are in.

The official teaching says Ilvaris gave Status so mortals could grow enough to face calamities. This lecture repeats the official teaching and then returns to the work: what the mark on your sheet allows you to practice today. Wonder belongs in the room. Convenience does not. If a working solves your trouble by ignoring its own cost, you skipped a page.

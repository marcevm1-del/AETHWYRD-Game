## A potsherd, not a dynasty

I set this down at Thimblefolk Mushroom Town because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The blue lamp-glass on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Thimblefolk Mushroom Town the work of the day sits inside toadstool eaves and moss steps. People eat mushroom stew and last year's honey and they talk about spores that sweeten a closed room the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a walker. A speaker at Thimblefolk Mushroom Town says one thing. A speaker at Mossdeep says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara under a green dark. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## What this fragment refuses

Valdrisia is a name for an old empire of towers and ley-line farms, dated by scholars near six thousand years before the Summoning Calendar, and gone, on the public record, in a single night about three thousand years before that calendar’s year zero. Sunscar keeps the ruins people can walk to. Mossdeep keeps whatever this copy actually found, which may be less romantic.

I will not write the night as if I were there. I will not name a single cause to please a patron. A vanished city is not a riddle you are owed. It is a wound in the record, and the people who live on that ground now have suppers to cook. If your theory needs them to be props, leave the theory in your pocket.

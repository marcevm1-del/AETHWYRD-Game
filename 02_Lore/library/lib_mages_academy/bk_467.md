## A potsherd, not a dynasty

I set this down at Moraveil goblin village because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Moraveil goblin village the work of the day sits inside market awnings and goblin-mended stalls. People eat pepper stew and fried bread and they talk about a bargain that changes if you blink the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a walker. A speaker at Moraveil goblin village says one thing. A speaker at Moraveil market town says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Vex's dice on the counter. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## What this fragment refuses

Valdrisia is a name for an old empire of towers and ley-line farms, dated by scholars near six thousand years before the Summoning Calendar, and gone, on the public record, in a single night about three thousand years before that calendar’s year zero. Sunscar keeps the ruins people can walk to. Moraveil keeps whatever this copy actually found, which may be less romantic.

I will not write the night as if I were there. I will not name a single cause to please a patron. A vanished city is not a riddle you are owed. It is a wound in the record, and the people who live on that ground now have suppers to cook. If your theory needs them to be props, leave the theory in your pocket.

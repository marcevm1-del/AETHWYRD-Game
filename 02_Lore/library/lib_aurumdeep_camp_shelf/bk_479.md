## The week as people actually live it

I set this down at Root of the World because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The tea-stained map on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Root of the World the work of the day sits inside root-pillars and dripping dark. People eat waybread saved for the last day and they talk about a quiet that feels planted, not empty the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a household. A speaker at Root of the World says one thing. A speaker at Root of the World says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. ## Days, without a whip

The week runs Solenday, Maelsday, Thorsday, Sylvasday, Kaelsday, Morrowsday, Vexday. Ilvaris has no day. People notice, and they do not all explain it the same way. You can miss a festival and still be a neighbor. You cannot miss a promised watch and call it piety.

Status Appraisal Day comes at twelve. It is a festival and it is a sorting, and adults who joke through it are remembering their own. Bring the honey cakes before the reading. Afterward, walk the child home whether the sheet was kind or not.

The Lamp Vigil is for the dead, with offerings and a light that is not a dare. Eat the biscuits before the lighting. Speak the names you have a right to speak. A red moon in the sky is a thing sailors mention without raising their voice. This book does not explain it. The night is already full.

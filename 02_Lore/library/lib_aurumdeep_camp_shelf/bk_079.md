## How this copy is used

I set this down at Hollow Sea boat-towns because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The tea-stained map on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Edda Park of Hollow Sea keeps this book in the kitchen at Hollow Sea boat-towns, where the houses are black water under a stone sky. The everyday pot already knows kelp-cake and lamp-oil tea. What follows is the version you can cook when a tide with no moon to explain it has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Morrowyn's lantern, hooded. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Waybread for a Real Pocket

*As cooked when Hollow Sea boat-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- flour
- a little honey
- salt
- water

A stiff dough, rolled thin, baked dry. It should bend and not powder. If it powders, you baked a tile.

## Millbrook Rabbit Pot

*As cooked when Hollow Sea boat-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- one horned rabbit, jointed
- an onion
- a heel of bread
- thyme if the hedge allows
- water and salt

Brown the joints in a little fat. Add onion. Cover with water and the bread so the broth goes thick. Keep it under a boil. Eat with the spoons you have.

## Yurt Smoke Meat

*As cooked when Hollow Sea boat-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a strip of dried meat
- salted milk
- flat stone bread

Soften the meat in the milk over a low fire. Do not boil the milk. Fold it into the bread. This is a riding meal, not a feast.

## The story told to children

I set this down at Xochitl river-cities because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The guild chalk stub on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Xochitl river-cities the work of the day sits inside flowering water and raised paths. People eat chili, cacao, and grilled plantain and they talk about a beautiful pool with a bad footing the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Xochitl river-cities says one thing. A speaker at Xochitl Mire says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Solenne: a golden scale, kept for oaths and the morning light. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara in the flowers, Morrowyn in the still water. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

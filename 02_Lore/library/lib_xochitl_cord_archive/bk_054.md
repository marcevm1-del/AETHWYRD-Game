## How this copy is used

I set this down at Xochitl river-cities because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Idris Ibarra of Xochitl Mire keeps this book in the kitchen at Xochitl river-cities, where the houses are flowering water and raised paths. The everyday pot already knows chili, cacao, and grilled plantain. What follows is the version you can cook when a beautiful pool with a bad footing has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Sylvara in the flowers, Morrowyn in the still water. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Appraisal-Day Honey Cakes

*As cooked when Xochitl river-cities is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- flour
- honey
- an egg
- a little milk

Small cakes, one each, given before the reading and not after. The day is already going to sort people. The cakes should not.

**You need**

- a river fish
- citrus peel
- oil

## Cedar Rain Rice

*As cooked when Xochitl river-cities is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- rice
- grilled fish
- pickles
- tea

Rinse the rice until the water is almost clear. Steam it. Lay the fish beside, not on top, so the rice stays its own thing. Pickles at the edge.

*As cooked when Xochitl river-cities is being ordinary.*

**You need**

Steam the millet. Stew the rest until it gives. Serve them touching, not stirred, so a guest can refuse the meat and still be fed.

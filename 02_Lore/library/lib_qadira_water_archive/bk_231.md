## The story told to children

I set this down at Oasis of Qadira because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The reed pen on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Oasis of Qadira the work of the day sits inside adobe shade and hot dust. People eat flatbread, dates, and goat cheese and they talk about a ruin that was a city, and a night nobody can date cleanly the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Oasis of Qadira says one thing. A speaker at Sunscar says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Solenne at noon, and a silence about the old empire. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

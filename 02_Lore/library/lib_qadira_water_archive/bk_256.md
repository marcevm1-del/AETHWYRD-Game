## The story told to children

I set this down at the town on Thalassa's back because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The blue lamp-glass on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At the town on Thalassa's back the work of the day sits inside dune grass and a track of posts. People eat dried fish, dates, and grit in the tea and they talk about something large that travels under the sand the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at the town on Thalassa's back says one thing. A speaker at Dunewhale Drift says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Solenne: a golden scale, kept for oaths and the morning light. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Vex, because the posts are a gamble. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

# On Keeping Bees the Size of Dogs
*Marjoram Clover, honey-keeper, with a note on the stings*

**Shelf:** Craft notes  
**Region:** Mossdeep  
**In-world date:** 1014 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I will say at the outset that the bees are not the size of dogs. They are the size of a large cat. The dogs are, in my experience, rather smaller.

## What they are

To a Thimblefolk keeper, a bee is a handsome, heavy, humming creature the length of your arm. To a big folk, it is a bee. This is the whole difficulty of our craft: we have to keep creatures that are large for us and ordinary for everyone else, in a place where everything else is also very large.

## The hive

A hive is a hollow in the Old Shade, the great oak at the valley head, whose roots hold the town's honey stores. It is warm, dry and dark. The bees come and go at dusk, with the drone of a mill. It is the best sound I know, and I have never been able to explain it to a person who has not heard it.

## Working

1. **Approach slowly.** A bee that is startled is a bee that is a problem.
2. **Smoke gently.** We burn dried moss in a little pot. It calms them. It also calms the keeper, and that is half the reason we do it.
3. **Lift the comb with both hands.** A comb is heavy. If it slips, it will not be the honey that is lost.
4. **Leave them a third.** Always. A hive that has been taken from too hard is a hive that leaves.

## Stings

They hurt. They do not kill. A keeper's skin grows used to it, and after twenty years, I feel a sting as a kind of punctuation. If you are stung, do not swat. Walk away calmly and put cold mud on the place.

## The honey

Mossdeep honey is sold to the big folk at the valley mouth, and it fetches a fortune in Aldmere. I do not know why. It tastes of moss, clover and a little of the oak. It tastes, I think, of being looked after.

I give a thimble of it to the Old Shade every spring. The Old Shade has never been known to refuse.

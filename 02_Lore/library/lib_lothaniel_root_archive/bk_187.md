# The Lodging-House Book of Widow Pellam
*Ottoline Pellam, human, keeper of a boarding-house in the trade quarters of Canopy City Lothaniel*

**Shelf:** Household book  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 1011 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I keep rooms for people the court charges twice. I charge them once, and I post the sign where the court can see it. We have not spoken since.

## Rule one: the price

A bed costs what a bed costs. If the court wishes to quarrel about it, they know where I live. It is the second-highest branch on the left, past the gnome with the clock.

## Rule two: the stairs

The stairs are not mine. They belong to the tree and are lent to me. Do not carve your name in them. Do not tie a rope to the rail. If you drop a bottle, apologise to the stair. I know how it sounds, and I have never lost a bottle since I began.

## Rule three: noise

The Canopy carries sound a long way. If you sing at night, the whole row will hear it, and by morning so will the court. We have a rule against singing after the second bell. We also have a rule that on Vexday it does not apply, because on Vexday, people who are not allowed to sing will sing anyway, and I would sooner they did it under a roof of my choosing.

## Rule four: food

I serve soup. It is a thick soup, made from whatever the market throws away, and it is better than it has any right to be. A bowl is a copper. A second bowl is free, and a third is a favour.

## Rule five: leaving

When you go, strip your bed and open the shutters. Do not leave anything behind that you would be sorry to see in a stranger's hands. I will keep it for a month; after that, it goes in the box by the stair.

## A note on the court

It is not a bad place. It is a place that has not decided whether it likes us. I keep my sign up, my rooms clean and my rates steady, and I wait. In forty years, I have been surprised only once, and it was by a tall grave elf who came to my door at midnight with a sick child and no coin. I did not charge her.

She remembered. That is what the court is, in the end: a very large memory with a very slow smile.

## The short version on the board

I set this down at Moonwell hamlets because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The guild chalk stub on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Moonwell hamlets the work of the day sits inside living timber and root-stairs. People eat waybread, honey, and leaf-tea and they talk about the old argument over felled heartwood the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at Moonwell hamlets says one thing. A speaker at Canopy City Lothaniel says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Sylvara: a leaf of the World Tree, kept for growth, beasts, and shade. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara, spoken to as a neighbor. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

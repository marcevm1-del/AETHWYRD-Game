# Who Owns a Branch
*Ilthiel Vaun, clerk of the Council of Elder Houses, writing for a human apprentice who asked*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 994 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

You asked me a simple question: whose is the bridge you stand on? I will answer it the way the court answers every simple question, which is at length.

## The first answer

The bridge is the court's. It was grown by the court, over eleven human lifetimes, from living wood that the court tends. It is the court's in the same way that a child belongs to its parents: the parents do not own it, they are responsible for it, and the difference is the whole of our law.

## The second answer

The bridge is the tree's. The court asks the tree before it asks the bough to bear weight, and the tree answers in a language the court has learned to read, a little, and the humans have not.

## The third answer, which you will hear in the hamlets

The bridge is whoever crosses it. This is the answer of the wood elves on the forest floor, who have never been invited to the court and have never asked to be. They say a bridge that is not walked is only a dead branch with ideas.

## How a house comes to hold a branch

A house is granted the use of a branch for as long as it tends it. It prunes, it feeds, it listens. If it neglects the branch, the branch is given to another house, with an apology to the tree. If it tends it well for a very long time, the branch becomes the house, in the sense that the two cannot be separated. That is not ownership. That is marriage, and the law of marriage in the Canopy is older than the law of property.

## Why I am telling you

You will be charged twice for everything in the trade quarters, and you will want to know why. It is not because the court dislikes humans. It is because the court dislikes strangers, and it has found that a price is the gentlest way to say so. You may earn your way out of it. Be patient. We have long memories, and they are not always unkind.

Sylvara, whom we call the Gardener, sees all of it. I would not argue with her about a branch.

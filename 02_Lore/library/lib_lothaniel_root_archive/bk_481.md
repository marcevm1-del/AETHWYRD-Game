# The Pressed-Flower Calendar
*Sage Lindenbloom, who keeps the calendar of a Thimblefolk household, and has done for sixty years without a mistake*

**Shelf:** Festival notes  
**Region:** Mossdeep  
**In-world date:** 1010 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Every house in the town keeps a pressed-flower calendar, and a family's luck depends on keeping it accurate. I have kept ours since I was twelve, and the family has been unusually lucky ever since. I make no claim that the one thing is the cause of the other. I make a modest claim that it has not hurt.

## How it works

There is a long board, hung by the hearth. It is divided into days, by weeks, by seasons. Every morning, at sunrise, a flower is picked from the garden and pressed into the space for that day. At the end of the year, the board is taken down, wrapped in a cloth and put in a chest. Another is hung in its place.

A house with fifty boards is a house with a history. A house with a hundred is a house that has been lucky.

## The festivals

**The first warm day.** We put a thimble of milk at the foot of the Old Shade. It is gone by dawn. We say it is the oak. Others say it is the Giant who once shared our bread. I do not say anything. I put out the milk.

**Honey-Week.** The honey-keepers take the comb. The whole town smells of it. People walk about with sticky hands and a look of dazed contentment. No work is done on the last day, for good reason.

**The Beetle Race.** A beetle-rider's holiday. The racers set out from the Warm Springs and run the length of the ferns. It is not clear who wins. It is very clear who is proudest.

**The Long Night.** In deep winter. A candle in every window and a song from every door. We sing the same song in every house, and we sing it at different speeds, and the result is a sort of tide.

## The day-gods

We keep the festivals of the day-gods, each in its turn, with some enthusiasm and a good deal of confusion about which comes next. The calendar settles it. A calendar is a way of being right.

## For anyone who keeps one

Pick the flower in the morning, when it is wet. Press it between two clean leaves under a flat stone. Do not look at it for three days. When you do, it will have become a day.

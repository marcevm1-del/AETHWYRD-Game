## How this copy is used

I set this down at Moonwell hamlets because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Satsuki Ellison of Sylvaran keeps this book in the kitchen at Moonwell hamlets, where the houses are living timber and root-stairs. The everyday pot already knows waybread, honey, and leaf-tea. What follows is the version you can cook when the old argument over felled heartwood has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Sylvara, spoken to as a neighbor. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Named-Cove Stew

*As cooked when Moonwell hamlets is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- fish
- coconut or milk
- onion
- salt

Simmer, do not boil hard. Name the cove you caught it in if you are thanking someone. Do not name the person who fell in.

## Millbrook Rabbit Pot

*As cooked when Moonwell hamlets is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- one horned rabbit, jointed
- an onion
- a heel of bread
- thyme if the hedge allows
- water and salt

Brown the joints in a little fat. Add onion. Cover with water and the bread so the broth goes thick. Keep it under a boil. Eat with the spoons you have.

## Yurt Smoke Meat

*As cooked when Moonwell hamlets is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a strip of dried meat
- salted milk
- flat stone bread

Soften the meat in the milk over a low fire. Do not boil the milk. Fold it into the bread. This is a riding meal, not a feast.

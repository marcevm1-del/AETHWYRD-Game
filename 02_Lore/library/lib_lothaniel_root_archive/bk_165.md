## The short version on the board

I set this down at Moonwell hamlets because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Moonwell hamlets the work of the day sits inside living timber and root-stairs. People eat waybread, honey, and leaf-tea and they talk about the old argument over felled heartwood the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at Moonwell hamlets says one thing. A speaker at Canopy City Lothaniel says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara, spoken to as a neighbor. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

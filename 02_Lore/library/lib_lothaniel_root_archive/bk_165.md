# What We Say About the Court
*Brae Mossfoot, a wood elf of the moonwell hamlets, who has been to the court twice and does not intend a third*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 1010 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Someone asked me to write down what the hamlets think of Lothaniel. I said I would, if they promised not to print it. They have printed it. So I will be careful.

## First, what we do

We farm. We hunt. We tend the moonwell, which is not ours, though we have tended it for four hundred years. We plant a seed for every child at the naming and we water the sapling until it is old enough to be rude about it. It is a good life. It is a life that does not require a bridge.

## What the court thinks of us

It thinks we are charming. That is the word. It says it the way you say "charming" of a particularly well-behaved dog.

## What we think of the court

We think it is lovely, and very high, and has forgotten where the water comes from.

We say that the court speaks of a hundred-year plan as though a hundred years were a short time, and of a single week as though a week were an insult. We say that a council which meets for a decade to decide a footbridge has lost its sense of the foot.

## The blight

I have watched the blight come up from the hollow for three summers now. It is a grey softness at the root of things. It makes the bark spongy and the mind slow, and it has reached the first hamlet. I am told the court is "considering the matter." I am told it is a very thorough consideration.

We do not wait. We cut, we burn, we ask the trees, and we keep a watch at night. It is not elegant. It works for a little while, which is as long as most things work.

## What I would say to the court

Come down. Drink from the moonwell with permission, and you will have it. Sit at a hamlet table and eat what we grow. You will find we are not as foolish as you have been told. We are, I think, only more recent.

I do not hate the court. I wish it would remember that a tree has two ends.

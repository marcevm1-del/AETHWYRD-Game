## The short version on the board

I set this down at Canopy City Lothaniel because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Canopy City Lothaniel the work of the day sits inside living timber and root-stairs. People eat waybread, honey, and leaf-tea and they talk about the old argument over felled heartwood the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at Canopy City Lothaniel says one thing. A speaker at Moonwell hamlets says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara, spoken to as a neighbor. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

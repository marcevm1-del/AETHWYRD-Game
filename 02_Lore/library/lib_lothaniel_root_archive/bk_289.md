# Sylvasday at the Root
*Thessaly Greenmantle, a keeper of the rootward shrine, who has not missed a Sylvasday in sixty-one years*

**Shelf:** Chapel notes  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 1006 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

In Sylvaran, the day-gods are honoured, but the World Tree is revered, and Sylvasday is the one day on which the two agree.

## The morning

We rise before the court. The court rises late on Sylvasday, as a courtesy to the tree, and we rise early, as a courtesy to ourselves. By the time the light comes down through the leaves, we are already at the root.

## The rootward shrine

A small hollow among the great roots, no larger than a kitchen. A bench. A bowl of water. A candle in a jar. There is nothing else, and that is the point. A shrine that has too much in it is a shrine that is talking over the tree.

## What we do

1. We lay a palm on the root. We do not pray. We listen.
2. We sit. The bench is warm, because the root is warm, because the tree is alive.
3. We say what we are grateful for, aloud, to nobody in particular. It has been said that the tree prefers it to be specific. I say "the soup" and "my left knee, on a good day."
4. We water the bowl. It is a small act and it takes no time.

## What the tree does

Nothing. It is a tree. But there are mornings when I have sat on the bench and felt a hum, not in the ear but in the bone, as though a very large thing were thinking of me for a moment and then thinking of something else. I have never mentioned it to anyone. I mention it now because I have grown old, and honesty is cheap at my age.

## For the visitor

You are welcome. Take your shoes off. Do not carve the bench. If you cannot think of anything to be grateful for, say so, and sit for a while. The tree can wait.

Sylvara is a Gardener. The first thing a gardener does with a stranger is give them a seed.

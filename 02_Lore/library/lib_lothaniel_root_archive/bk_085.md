## How this copy is used

I set this down at Moonwell hamlets because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Safiya Novak of Sylvaran keeps this book in the kitchen at Moonwell hamlets, where the houses are living timber and root-stairs. The everyday pot already knows waybread, honey, and leaf-tea. What follows is the version you can cook when the old argument over felled heartwood has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Sylvara, spoken to as a neighbor. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

- stout bread
- dripping

## Peat Eel with Berries

*As cooked when Moonwell hamlets is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- two small eels
- bog-berries
- rye crust
- a pinch of pepper

Coil the eel in a low pan. Crush the berries over it. Set the crust on top and bake at the edge of the fire until the crust sounds hollow.

## Anvil Stout Bread

*As cooked when Moonwell hamlets is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- barley flour
- a spoon of stout
- salt
- a roast root

Mix the flour, stout, and salt into a stiff dough. Bake it on the oven floor. Split it and put the hot root inside.

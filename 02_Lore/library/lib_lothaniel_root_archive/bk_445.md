# Why the Bridges Creak
*A fireside tale of the moonwell hamlets, told with the hands as well as the voice*

**Shelf:** Stories for reading aloud  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 1017 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

*(Read this one with a creak. Make the creak with your chair. The children will help.)*

A long time ago, before the court was built, there was a bridge that did not creak.

It was a very quiet bridge. It carried a very quiet people over a very quiet stream, and nothing about it was ever remarked upon. It was the sort of bridge you cross without noticing and forget as soon as you have crossed.

One day a small wood elf named Pip sat down on the middle of the bridge and said, "I am bored."

"Bored?" said the bridge, which had never spoken before, and was a little startled.

"Nobody ever talks to you," said Pip. "You carry us all day and you never say anything."

"I did not think anyone wished to hear."

"I do."

The bridge thought for a while. It was a very old bridge, and it had never thought about talking, and it did not know where to begin.

"I could creak," it said.

"Creak?"

"It's what I do when I am glad."

So the bridge creaked, a long low sound, like a very old door opening on a very good morning. Pip laughed. The next elf to cross laughed too. The creak spread from bridge to bridge, along the whole of the forest.

---

That is why the bridges creak. They are not broken, and they are not complaining. They are glad that you came.

*(Say it together, and creak:)* **"Thank you for crossing."**

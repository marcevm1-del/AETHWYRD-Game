# The Naming and the Seed
*Perrin Elmgrove, keeper of the Twin Groves, who has planted four thousand seeds and watered most of them*

**Shelf:** Chapel notes  
**Region:** Sylvaran  
**In-world date:** 1016 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Every elven child plants a seed when they are named. The tree that grows is their twin for life. This is the custom of the Twin Groves, and I am its keeper.

## The ceremony

It is short. It has to be: the child is usually asleep.

The parents bring the child to the grove at dawn. The name is spoken once. A seed is chosen: an oak, a birch, a beech, a yew, anything the family has loved. It is placed in the soil by the child's own hand, or, if the hand is too small, by the parent's hand around the child's.

The keeper, that is, I, says three words. I will not write them. They are not secret, but they are meant to be heard and not read. After that, the child is handed back, the seed is covered and the family goes home to breakfast.

## The tree

It grows. That is the whole of the matter. A tree that grows well is a child that grows well, and a tree that grows crooked is a child who will grow into the crookedness, which is no worse than anyone else.

When the child is ill, the tree droops. When the child is happy, the tree puts out a new shoot. When the child dies, which is the thing no one speaks of, the tree is left to stand until it falls. We do not cut it. We say it is resting.

## A visit

You will see a grove of a few thousand trees, of every age, in rows that are not quite straight. The older ones are very tall. The youngest are the height of a spoon. I know them all by name, because I know the names of their children.

## The blight

It has not come to the groves. I watch for it every morning. If it comes, I will not move the children. I will move the keeper, and the keeper will stand between the blight and the trees until it is stopped, or I am.

Sylvara keeps the beasts and the green things. I keep the names.

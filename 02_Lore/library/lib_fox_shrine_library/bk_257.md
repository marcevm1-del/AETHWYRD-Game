## The story told to children

I set this down at Naming Voyage harbor because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The chipped scale-weight on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Naming Voyage harbor the work of the day sits inside salt cedar and named coves. People eat fish stew and coconut and they talk about a name given carefully, or not at all the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Naming Voyage harbor says one thing. A speaker at True-Name Isles says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Sylvara: a leaf of the World Tree, kept for growth, beasts, and shade. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Maelis, who is asked before a child is named. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

# The Mender at the Foot of the Stair
*Ilse Brannigan, mender of salvaged gear, Labyrinth gate*

**Shelf:** Craft notes  
**Region:** Great Labyrinth (Floors 1-40)  
**In-world date:** 1013 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

My bench is a plank on two barrels at the foot of the stair. People come to me with a bent greave, a cracked cap or a boot that has lost its heel to the dungeon, and I put it back together for what they can pay. This is how I do it, in case you ever have to do it yourself with a candle and a bad mood.

## What comes out of the Warren

The kobolds stack what adventurers drop, and some of it comes back through the gate in better shape than it went in. Their stacks are sorted the way a magpie sorts: by shine. So the first job is always the same. Put out what you found on the plank and sort it by what it is made of, not by how it looks.

- **Scrap Metal** goes to the pile for the smith.
- **Crude Iron** goes for a mace or a rivet.
- **Labyrinth Bronze** is the good one; it takes a polish and holds a shape.
- **Carapace Shard** from an armour beetle makes a decent cap if you drill it first.

## A cap, step by step

For the Carapace Cap, the recipe is a smith's recipe and I do not pretend otherwise. I do the part before the forge. Soak the shards in warm water until the grit lets go. Dry them on a flat stone, not a hot one; heat cracks them. Mark the line you will cut with a sliver of chalk, and cut a hair outside it, because a cap that is cut tight is a cap that kills a headache.

## Bronze Greaves

Bronze goes soft when it is hot and hard when it is cold, and the trick is to know which you are holding. I tap it with the back of a nail. A dull sound is soft; a clear ring is hard. Do not hammer cold bronze. It does not forgive.

## Honest advice

Do not mend what you cannot lift. A greave that has lost a third of itself is not a greave; it is a story. Take it to the smith and let him decide.

Thorgrim's promise is that the metal remembers. Mine is that I will tell you when it does not.

# The Year at the Gate: One Household's Calendar
*Greta Holloway, who keeps a boarding-house at the foot of the Labyrinth stair*

**Shelf:** Festival notes  
**Region:** Great Labyrinth (Floors 1-40)  
**In-world date:** 1015 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I keep a boarding-house. The year, for me, is measured in beds. I know the time of year by who is asking for a room.

## First Frost

The first frost is the first rush: every party that wanted to finish the halls before winter arrives on the same night. I put two to a bed and charge for one. Nobody has ever complained, because nobody can sleep through the noise of a house full of nervous people.

## The Week of Closed Doors

Once a year, in the dead of winter, the Guild desk closes the gate for seven days. They call it a week of closed doors. The kobolds are too cold to be useful and the slimes are too slow, and the clerk's fingers are too stiff to write. The house is full of people with nothing to do. I make soup. I make a great deal of soup.

## The Lamp Vigil

On the night of the Lamp Vigil, we hang a blue lantern at the foot of the stair for everyone who did not come back. It is Morrowyn's night. Nobody says the names aloud, because there are too many. We say the numbers instead: the year, and the floor. I say them in my sleep.

The next morning the lanterns are taken down and the stairs are swept, and everyone behaves as though nothing had been said. It is the kindest silence I know.

## The Spring Count

When the thaw comes the Guild counts who went in and who came out, and the two numbers are read at the desk. I do not go. I know both numbers already.

## Midsummer

The best week. The halls are quiet, the roads are dry and a few parties come down smelling of sunshine rather than damp. I put flowers on the table. Somebody always dances. It is never me.

## The whole year, in one line

A boarding-house at the foot of a dungeon is a place where people come to be afraid and then to be fed. I like to think of it as the second half of the job.

Solenne for the morning, Morrowyn for the night, and Vex for whoever is knocking at my door at an hour I will not describe.

# Reading the Old Tally Chalk
*Wenna Ashby, chalk-maker, with a note on how I learned to read it*

**Shelf:** Craft notes  
**Region:** Great Labyrinth (Floors 1-40)  
**In-world date:** 1001 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I make chalk. People laugh until they need it. Then they ask whether mine will mark wet stone, and I say yes, and I charge a copper more.

This is not a chalk-maker's book. It is the other thing I know: how to read the marks the old tally-keepers left on the walls under the third floor, near the kobolds' stacks. I learned it the way you learn to read your grandmother's handwriting: by getting it wrong for a long time.

## The marks

The tally-keepers counted things that went through a door, and they counted them in fives. Four strokes and a slash through them is a five. A circle round a stroke is a day. A small hook at the foot of a column means the count was checked by a second person, which is the habit I admire most.

At the Chalk Landing the marks are plain, and anyone can read them. Further down, near the rusted tally door, they change. The columns turn sideways, and a mark I had learned to read as a "five" begins to mean "a way". I did not understand that for six winters.

## What I will tell you

I will not say where the way is. I can tell you how to look.

1. Read the marks with a lamp held low. Chalk casts a tiny shadow, and the shadow shows you which stroke was drawn last.
2. A column that was rubbed out and redrawn is a column somebody cared about.
3. Do not touch the old chalk. It lasts only because nobody has leaned on it.

## Making your own

Take lime that has dried from a slurry, grind it fine and press it into a mould with a little tallow. Let it set in the shade. It must be dry before it is used, or it will crumble and smear. Mark everything you pass, in your own hand, so that if you come back you will know which way is back. I teach every young adventurer the same habit: leave a trail you would trust.

Maelis watches the sleeper and the dreamer; Vex keeps the road. I have no god for chalk. I make do with both.

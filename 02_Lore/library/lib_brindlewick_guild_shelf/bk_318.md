# Goods Through Brindlewick
*Perrin Dace, factor for the mill on the Brindle river*

**Shelf:** Trade notes  
**Region:** Whisperwood  
**In-world date:** 1003 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Notebook of a flour factor, kept for whoever takes the counting-room after me. Read it with the sack-scales in reach, because half of it is arithmetic.

## Mornings

The mill wakes before the town does. By the time the bell on Guild Square rings, the millrace has already turned two hundredweight of grain into flour and put a white film on every windowsill between the river and the Sleeping Griffon. I count that film as goodwill. People who complain about it are people who have not yet bought their first loaf from us.

Brindlewick eats flour. It eats it as bread at the inn, as dumplings in the stew, as the dust in a rookie's boots after the first week. If you want to know how the town is doing, do not ask the reeve; ask the baker on Market Row how many sacks she turned away on Solenday.

## What the new arrivals buy

Every season the Guild sends a fresh row of F-rank hopefuls up the north road, and every season they all want the same four things, in the same order:

1. A hearth loaf, because it keeps.
2. Something sweet, because the first night in the wood is longer than they thought.
3. Salt, which they will forget they own.
4. A second pair of laces. Nobody believes me until the first pair snaps.

I sell all four, and I quietly pass the loaf price to the baker so she can match it. A rookie who is fed on the first morning is a rookie who comes back to the counter in a month with a Goblin Ear in one hand and an apology in the other.

## What the wood sends back

Whisperwood pays for flour in odd currency. Hunters bring Pelt and Prime Meat; foragers bring Sap and Seed; the lucky ones come in with Slime Jelly in a jar and the unlucky ones come in with nothing but a story. I take jelly only from people who rinsed the jar, because the alchemist next door has a nose like a hound and a temper to match.

Do not buy a Goblin Ear from anyone you do not know. It proves nothing about who killed the goblin, and the Guild clerk will ask you where you got it.

## Roads and weather

The grain comes down the Reeve's Road from the Aldmere side and the flour goes back the same way, with a cart of whatever the mill's neighbours do not want to be carrying north. When the Brindle floods, the cart takes a day longer and I charge a copper more per sack, and I explain this to every customer so that nobody hears about it from a rival. Honesty is cheaper than the damage a rumour does.

## A rule I keep

In the days I have kept this book, I have noticed that the people who thank the mill are the ones who have been hungry. When a rookie counts out coppers on the counter and comes up two short, I look at the loaf, then at the face, and I weigh the loaf a little heavier than the scale says. It costs me a handful of coppers a year. Sylvara watches over the wood; somebody ought to watch over the first week.

Margin, in a different hand: *He weighed mine heavy too. - T.*

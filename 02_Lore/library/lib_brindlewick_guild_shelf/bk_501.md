# Neighbors Near Brindlewick
*Tamsin Orrel, who trained rookies at Hollis's Yard for nineteen years*

**Shelf:** Field guide  
**Region:** Whisperwood  
**In-world date:** 1017 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I stopped training rookies the winter my knees stopped believing in stairs. This is the lecture I gave on the first morning, written down so that someone else can read it aloud badly and still be useful.

The wood north of Brindlewick is the gentlest country the Guild owns, and that is the danger. It teaches you that things forgive. They do not. They are merely small.

## Blue Slime

Rank F-, a Water creature, and weak to Lightning. It keeps a patch of damp and defends it. Everyone laughs at slimes until the first one sits on a boot and does not leave. Do not stand in its puddle. Strike from the dry side and move back, and it will lose interest in you faster than you lose interest in your sword.

## Green Slime

Rank F, Nature, weak to Fire, and it comes in swarms. That is the lesson: one is a nuisance, six is an afternoon. Fire does the work. If you have none, retreat to open ground so that you are fighting one at a time, not six at once. I have watched a bright young woman take a Green Slime too lightly and come out of the grass looking like a salad.

## Goblin

F-, aggressive, weak to Fire. It comes straight at you with a bad blade and worse manners. The mistake is arrogance: a goblin alone is easy, and a goblin is rarely alone. Count heads before you charge. If you hear more than one voice, there is more than one goblin.

## Goblin Archer

F+, an ambusher. It waits above you and behind a branch. If something whistles past your ear and you cannot see where it came from, the archer is above the line of your eyes. Do not stand in the open and look around. Move to cover and make *it* come down.

## Kobold Scavenger

Rank F, skittish, and it hates to fight. It takes things. Keep your pack closed and your coin pouch inside your coat. It will bolt before you finish drawing, and that is the point; do not chase it into the trees, because that is where its friends live.

## Horned Rabbit

F-, skittish, weak to Fire, and as harmless as it looks until it hits you at the knee. Take it from the front, in the open. It is a good first hunt and a good first meal, and I have never taught a rookie who did not remember the first one fondly.

## Forest Wolf

F+, skittish. It tests you before it commits. If it circles and does not close, it is deciding. Face it, keep your back to a tree, and give it a reason to decide against you.

## One last thing

Every entry here lists a weakness, and every year some rookie reads "weak to Fire" and decides that means they are safe. It does not. It means the creature is weak, and you are only slightly less weak than the creature. Bring a friend. Eat first. Tell the desk where you are going and when you will be back; they will not follow you, but they will remember.

Solenne bless the early morning. Kaelthas keep the late one.

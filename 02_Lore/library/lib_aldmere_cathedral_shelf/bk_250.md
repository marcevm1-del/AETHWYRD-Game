## The story told to children

I set this down at Brindlewick because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Brindlewick the work of the day sits inside half-timber and hornbeam shade. People eat mill bread, tea, and a rabbit stew and they talk about goblin tracks on the mill path the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Brindlewick says one thing. A speaker at Millbrook says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Kaelthas: a lightning spear, kept for courage in a storm. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara's groves and a small Solenne window. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

Evidence you can touch beats a speech you can repeat. A mended wall, a card that nobody will take down, a field that will not take the old crop, a song that changes when the singer notices who just walked in. Copy the object. Then copy who benefits if you only believe the short version.

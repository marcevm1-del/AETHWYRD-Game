## The story told to children

I set this down at Millbrook because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Millbrook the work of the day sits inside half-timber and hornbeam shade. People eat mill bread, tea, and a rabbit stew and they talk about goblin tracks on the mill path the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Millbrook says one thing. A speaker at Brindlewick says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Sylvara's groves and a small Solenne window. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

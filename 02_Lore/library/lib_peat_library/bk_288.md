## The story told to children

I set this down at the Walking Hut market because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The dry orange peel on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At the Walking Hut market the work of the day sits inside reed thatch on stilts above peat. People eat eel, bog-berries, and black bread and they talk about lights that are not lanterns out on the peat the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at the Walking Hut market says one thing. A speaker at Greymoor stilt-village says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Ilvaris: a spindle and stars, kept for fate, and the Status the Guild teaches. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Morrowyn's blue lamps along the causeway. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

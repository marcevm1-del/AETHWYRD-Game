# Hand-Bells at Dusk
*Osric Penn, keeper of the dusk-bells for the stilt-village shrine of Morrowyn*

**Shelf:** Chapel notes  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1004 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I keep the small shrine at the end of the longest walkway. It has a blue lamp, a shelf of bells and a bench that has been sat on by every person I have ever disliked and several I loved.

## Why we ring

We ring at dusk to tell the dead which way is home. That is all. It is not a spell. It is a courtesy. In a fen where the paths shift, the dead lose their way as readily as the living, and they are not always as quick to ask directions.

## The three beats

One for the one who left. One for the one who stays. One for the one who has not yet decided. I have never been asked what the third is for, and I have never had to explain it. It does the work without an explanation.

## The bells

There are nine. The big one is the abbey's, or was. It came out of the bog in my grandmother's time with the clapper missing. We hang it, and ring it with a stick, and it makes a sound like a held breath.

The small ones are the hand-bells, which each house keeps. They are made from whatever metal was to hand: a pot lid, a bit of bog-iron, a nail. A house with no bell is a house in mourning. We lend one.

## The blue lamp

It is lit before the first bell and put out after the last. It is a plain lantern with a blue glass, and Morrowyn's mark is on its door. People come to the shrine to sit under it. They do not pray, as a rule. They sit. After a while, they stand up and go home, and the sitting has done what the praying would have.

## A thing I have seen

On a very still evening, I have rung the third beat and heard a small answer, from the reeds. It might have been a bird. I have rung the third beat every dusk for thirty years, and I have never been sure it was not.

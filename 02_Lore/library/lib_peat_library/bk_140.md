# Why the Fen Pays No Tax
*A surveyor's note from Aldmere, and the reply written beneath it in the witches' hand*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1009 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

## The surveyor's note

"The Crown claims the moor of Greymoor by charter, from the Greymoor River to the Witchwood. I was sent to value it for the roll. I record that I could not. The ground does not hold a measuring chain. The paths move. The only men who know where a stake may be driven decline to say, and the persons I take to be the magistrates refuse the title and say they are *a moot*.

"I record that I was fed very well. I record that the eel was excellent.

"I recommend that no tax be set until a road can be built. I note that nobody has built a road."

## The reply

*(in a narrow, upright hand, beneath the clerk's signature)*

We have read your note. We are not offended by it. We are pleased that you were fed.

We do not pay tax because we have nothing that can be counted. We have eels, which are eaten before the clerk arrives. We have peat, which burns. We have reeds, which rot in a season. We have medicine, which we give to anyone who asks, and which you will find costs less than the cart it travels in.

We have a word for what the Crown offers: *kindly*. We use it of a stranger who offers to carry your water and then asks where you keep your well.

We will keep the moot. We will keep the witches. We will keep the hand-bells at dusk, and when the Crown wishes to ring one with us, it is welcome. It need only come on foot and stay for supper.

## What the Keeper adds

I have the note and I have the reply. I do not have a decision. In the years since, no tax has been set and no road built. I think that is a settled matter, and I think it is the best kind of settlement, which is the kind that nobody has had to say aloud.

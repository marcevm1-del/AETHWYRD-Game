# The Moth Who Would Not Be Jarred
*A story for reading aloud by lamplight, as told in the stilt-houses of Greymoor*

**Shelf:** Stories for reading aloud  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1008 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

*(Read slowly. Pause where you see a dash. Let the smallest child say the last line.)*

Once there was a lantern-moth who lived in a jar. She did not mind. The jar was warm, and the sugar-water was sweet, and her light made a small gold circle in the window where a family sat and ate their supper.

But she had heard of the fen.

*A moth in a jar hears things.* She heard the reeds sing without singing. She heard the bell, and the answer to the bell. She heard that there was a place, out in the mist, where the moths flew free.

"I should like to see that," she said.

"You will be cold," said the other moths.

"I will be quick."

"You will be lost."

"I will be bright."

So the next time the jar was opened — she went.

---

The fen was bigger than the jar. It was greyer, and wetter, and very much quieter. She flew in a long line over the reeds and saw a punt. She saw a bell. She saw a small blue lamp at the end of a walkway.

"Whose is that?" she asked a passing heron.

"It is the dead's," said the heron, "and the way home."

She flew down to look. A tired old man sat under it, ringing a hand-bell, three times, and then a fourth for no reason at all.

"You are out late," he said.

"I wanted to see the fen."

"And?"

"It is large."

"Yes," said the old man. "It is. Would you like to go home?"

She thought about it for the length of a breath.

"I think," she said, "I should like to go home *and* have seen the fen."

"That," said the old man, "is the only kind of going home worth the name."

He held out the jar, and she went in.

*(And the smallest child says:)* **"And the light was brighter after."**

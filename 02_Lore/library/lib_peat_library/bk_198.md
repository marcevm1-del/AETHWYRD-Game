## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Greymoor stilt-village because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Greymoor stilt-village the work of the day sits inside reed thatch on stilts above peat. People eat eel, bog-berries, and black bread and they talk about lights that are not lanterns out on the peat the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Greymoor stilt-village says one thing. A speaker at the Walking Hut market says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Solenne: a golden scale, kept for oaths and the morning light. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Morrowyn's blue lamps along the causeway. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

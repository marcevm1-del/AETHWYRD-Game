# When the Bells Stopped
*Aldith Marsh, bell-ringer, retold from the village and the reeds*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1019 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I ring the hand-bell at dusk. It is my work and I have never been paid for it. This is the account of how the bells stopped, as I have been given it, and where I have seen it for myself.

## What the old people say

They say there was a time when a bell could be heard from one village to the next. That is not true any more. The fog thickens, and sound dies in it; you can ring a bell at the foot of your own ladder and not be heard on the walkway. They say it was not always so.

They say that something walks the reeds. They say it is slow, and it is patient, and it is a procession. They say it sings, but nobody hears it, and in the reeds where it has passed, the birds fall quiet and the frogs stop and the water makes no sound against the punt. They call this the Choir of Silence. It follows an abbot.

I have never seen the abbot. I have seen the silence.

## What I have seen

I have stood on the walkway at dusk with a bell in my hand and felt the sound go out of it. It is not as though you have been struck deaf. It is as though the air has been taken away. The bell moves. Your arm moves. Nothing answers.

I have learned to ring in a rhythm, three beats and a rest, so that the people who are listening can tell by the rest that the bell has not stopped, only that it has not been heard.

## What we do

We keep the feast days of the sunken abbey. We do it because the dead need to know which way is home. We do it because, if we stopped, the silence would have won.

## A caution

Do not whistle on the water. Do not sing. Do not try to out-shout it. I have seen a man shout himself hoarse at the fog, and the fog did not mind. Ring your bell, count the beats and go home.

Morrowyn keeps the blue lamp and the long memory. I keep the rhythm.

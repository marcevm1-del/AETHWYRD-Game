## How this copy is used

I set this down at the Walking Hut market because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Anwen Reed of Greymoor keeps this book in the kitchen at the Walking Hut market, where the houses are reed thatch on stilts above peat. The everyday pot already knows eel, bog-berries, and black bread. What follows is the version you can cook when lights that are not lanterns out on the peat has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Morrowyn's blue lamps along the causeway. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Oath-Anvil Crumbs

*As cooked when the Walking Hut market is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- stout bread
- dripping
- salt

Not a recipe so much as a right. The first slice from a new bake is touched to the anvil-side of the board and then eaten by the youngest person in the room.

## Peat Eel with Berries

*As cooked when the Walking Hut market is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- two small eels
- bog-berries
- rye crust
- a pinch of pepper

Coil the eel in a low pan. Crush the berries over it. Set the crust on top and bake at the edge of the fire until the crust sounds hollow.

## Anvil Stout Bread

*As cooked when the Walking Hut market is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- barley flour
- a spoon of stout
- salt
- a roast root

Mix the flour, stout, and salt into a stiff dough. Bake it on the oven floor. Split it and put the hot root inside.

## Spray Trout

*As cooked when the Walking Hut market is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a trout
- flatbread
- pine honey
- salt

Gut the trout. Salt it. Cook it until the eye goes white. Honey on the bread, not on the fish, unless you are alone and will not be judged.

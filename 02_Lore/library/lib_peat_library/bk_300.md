## The story told to children

I set this down at Greymoor stilt-village because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The tea-stained map on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Greymoor stilt-village the work of the day sits inside reed thatch on stilts above peat. People eat eel, bog-berries, and black bread and they talk about lights that are not lanterns out on the peat the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Greymoor stilt-village says one thing. A speaker at the Walking Hut market says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Maelis: a crescent and an eye, kept for dreams and the silver moon. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Morrowyn's blue lamps along the causeway. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

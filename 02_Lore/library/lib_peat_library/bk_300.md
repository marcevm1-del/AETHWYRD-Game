# What the Leaning Tower Keeps
*Jessamy Holt, who guides pilgrims to the tower on the feast days of the sunken abbey*

**Shelf:** Chapel notes  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1017 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Every year, on the feast days, I take whoever asks across the fen to the Leaning Tower. It is the bell tower of the abbey that sank, and it sinks a hand's width a year. By my reckoning it has three hands' widths to go before it is a reed bed. By the village's, it has never been taller than it is now.

## The walk

We go at dawn, by punt, in a line, with the lead punt carrying a bell. Nobody speaks. The water is black and the sky is grey, and the only sound is the pole. By the time we land, most pilgrims have forgotten why they came, and that is the first mercy of the journey.

## What the tower is

A stone shaft, as thick as a house, leaning towards the south-west like a tired man against a wall. The top is gone, or the top is under the water. The door is a window now. Inside, the floors have fallen into one another and there is a smell of damp stone and old wood.

## What we do there

1. We ring the bell. Any bell. The witches ask for it, and we bring it.
2. We say the names of the feast days. There are four. I will not write them here; a name written is a name that can be mislaid.
3. We leave a small thing: a loaf, a ribbon, a pebble. The tower takes it.
4. We sit for the count of a hundred. It is the longest hundred I know.

## What the tower keeps

I do not know. I have a notion it keeps whatever is given to it, which would explain why there is so little in the fen that has not been given. I have a notion that the village does not own the tower and the tower does not own the village; they simply stand in each other's company, as old neighbours do.

I say the names, and I leave my pebble, and I come home.

Morrowyn has the hour. I have the punt.

# The Abbey, Told Twice
*Two accounts of the sunken abbey of Greymoor, set side by side by the Peat Library's keeper*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Greymoor  
**In-world date:** 1000 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

I have two accounts of the same event and I will not choose between them. The keeper's duty is to set both down and let the reader decide who benefits.

## The village account

The abbey was built on the fen by monks who wished to be left alone. It stood a long time. Then the bog took it, a hand's width at a time, as a patient creditor takes a house. The monks were still inside. The villagers heard the bells under the water for a year and then the bells stopped, and that is why we keep the feast days: to tell them which way is home.

The village says the abbot was a good man who stayed with his brothers. The village says it with a lowered voice, because there is more to say and they do not say it.

## The Aldmere account

A letter filed in the royal records says the abbey was abandoned. It says the monks were relocated to a drier house, and that the buildings, being unsound, were left to the bog. The letter is dated, signed and sealed, and it is wrong in one particular: it says nobody was lost.

Whoever wrote it was not there. They wrote what a clerk would write to make a file look tidy.

## Where they agree

Both say the tower leans. Both say it sinks a hand's width each year. Both say there were bells. Neither says who cut the cords.

## Where they differ

The village says the monks are still there. The letter says they are not. I have walked to the Leaning Tower on a clear day and found nothing in the water but reeds and my own reflection. I have also walked there on a foggy dusk, and I will not say what I found, because I am a keeper and not a storyteller.

## What the evidence says

The peat preserves. It kept a monastery's books so well that I can read them if I dry them carefully, a page at a time, over a month. In those books there are no dates after a certain autumn, and no signature of the abbot's. That is all. It is not an answer.

## A note for the reader

The next time somebody tells you what happened at the abbey, ask who benefits from the version. A clerk benefits from a tidy file. A village benefits from a story it can keep. I benefit from neither, which is why they let me keep the shelf.

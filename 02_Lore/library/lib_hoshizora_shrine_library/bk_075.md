## How this copy is used

I set this down at Bamboo Sea villages because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The blue lamp-glass on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Safiya Costa of Hoshizora keeps this book in the kitchen at Bamboo Sea villages, where the houses are curved tile and cedar rain. The everyday pot already knows rice, grilled fish, and pickles. What follows is the version you can cook when a river mouth that fogs the streets has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Maelis and a household shrine. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Ash Onion Soup

*As cooked when Bamboo Sea villages is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- onions
- water or a bone broth
- grit-bread
- salt

Cook the onions until they go sweet and then a shade darker. Add water. The bread dips. Do not serve it grey.

## Terrace Potatoes

*As cooked when Bamboo Sea villages is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- potatoes
- chili
- herb salt
- oil

Boil, then crush with oil. Chili and herb salt at the end. If the terrace lost a row this year, use fewer potatoes and do not apologize with a speech.

## Mayonnaise, and the Argument

*As cooked when Bamboo Sea villages is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- an egg yolk
- oil
- a little vinegar
- salt

## Peat Eel with Berries

*As cooked when Bamboo Sea villages is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- two small eels
- bog-berries
- rye crust
- a pinch of pepper

Coil the eel in a low pan. Crush the berries over it. Set the crust on top and bake at the edge of the fire until the crust sounds hollow.

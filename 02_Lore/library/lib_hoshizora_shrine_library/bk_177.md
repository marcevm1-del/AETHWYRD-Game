## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Hoshizora City because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The chipped scale-weight on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Hoshizora City the work of the day sits inside curved tile and cedar rain. People eat rice, grilled fish, and pickles and they talk about a river mouth that fogs the streets the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Hoshizora City says one thing. A speaker at Bamboo Sea villages says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Maelis and a household shrine. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

Evidence you can touch beats a speech you can repeat. A mended wall, a card that nobody will take down, a field that will not take the old crop, a song that changes when the singer notices who just walked in. Copy the object. Then copy who benefits if you only believe the short version.

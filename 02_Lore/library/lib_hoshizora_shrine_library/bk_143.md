## The short version on the board

I set this down at Hoshizora City because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The chipped scale-weight on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Hoshizora City the work of the day sits inside curved tile and cedar rain. People eat rice, grilled fish, and pickles and they talk about a river mouth that fogs the streets the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at Hoshizora City says one thing. A speaker at Bamboo Sea villages says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Sylvara: a leaf of the World Tree, kept for growth, beasts, and shade. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Maelis and a household shrine. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## The story told to children

I set this down at Bamboo Sea villages because the Guild card was right about the danger and wrong about the cause. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Bamboo Sea villages the work of the day sits inside curved tile and cedar rain. People eat rice, grilled fish, and pickles and they talk about a river mouth that fogs the streets the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Bamboo Sea villages says one thing. A speaker at Hoshizora City says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Maelis and a household shrine. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

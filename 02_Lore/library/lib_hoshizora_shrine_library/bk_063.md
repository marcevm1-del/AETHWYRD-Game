## How this copy is used

I set this down at Bamboo Sea villages because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The chipped scale-weight on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Bruni Lind of Hoshizora keeps this book in the kitchen at Bamboo Sea villages, where the houses are curved tile and cedar rain. The everyday pot already knows rice, grilled fish, and pickles. What follows is the version you can cook when a river mouth that fogs the streets has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Maelis and a household shrine. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Barrack Beans

*As cooked when Bamboo Sea villages is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- beans
- oil
- salt
- bread

Oil in first. Beans until soft. More salt than a manor cook would allow. Bread is a tool here.

## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Hoshizora City because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The reed pen on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Hoshizora City the work of the day sits inside curved tile and cedar rain. People eat rice, grilled fish, and pickles and they talk about a river mouth that fogs the streets the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Hoshizora City says one thing. A speaker at Bamboo Sea villages says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Maelis and a household shrine. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

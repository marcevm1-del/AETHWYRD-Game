## The story told to children

I set this down at Cinderhole because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Cinderhole the work of the day sits inside ash camps and mine timbers. People eat grit-bread and onion soup and they talk about a fire in the rock that is older than the camp the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Cinderhole says one thing. A speaker at Cinderhole says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim for the shift, Morrowyn for the names on the beam. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

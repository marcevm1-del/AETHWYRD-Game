## How this copy is used

I set this down at Ashen Mines camps because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Kwame Brooks of Iron Hold keeps this book in the kitchen at Ashen Mines camps, where the houses are basalt halls and anvil-heat. The everyday pot already knows stout, stone-bread, and roast roots. What follows is the version you can cook when a vow hammered where a stranger can hear it has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Thorgrim, whose mark is on every new tool. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Clockwork Sausage

*As cooked when Ashen Mines camps is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a sausage
- mustard
- beer bread

Fry the sausage whole. Mustard on the side so the bread can be plain for someone who needs plain.

## Millbrook Rabbit Pot

*As cooked when Ashen Mines camps is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- one horned rabbit, jointed
- an onion
- a heel of bread
- thyme if the hedge allows
- water and salt

Brown the joints in a little fat. Add onion. Cover with water and the bread so the broth goes thick. Keep it under a boil. Eat with the spoons you have.

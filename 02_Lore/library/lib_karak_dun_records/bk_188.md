## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Karak Dûn because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The knot-cord on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Karak Dûn the work of the day sits inside basalt halls and anvil-heat. People eat stout, stone-bread, and roast roots and they talk about a vow hammered where a stranger can hear it the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Karak Dûn says one thing. A speaker at Ashen Mines camps says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim, whose mark is on every new tool. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

Evidence you can touch beats a speech you can repeat. A mended wall, a card that nobody will take down, a field that will not take the old crop, a song that changes when the singer notices who just walked in. Copy the object. Then copy who benefits if you only believe the short version.

# Steel for Both Sides
*Garrow Thrum, a steel factor of Karak Dûn, in a private letter he asked to have kept on the shelf*

**Shelf:** Local history  
**Region:** Iron Hold  
**In-world date:** 1013 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

My dear niece,

You asked whether it troubles me, selling iron and steel to every kingdom in the world, including those who are at war with one another. I said I would think about it. I have thought for three weeks.

## What I sell

Plate, blades, nails, rivets, horseshoes, picks. Mostly picks. For every sword that leaves this hold, there are fifty ploughshares, and a hundred nails, and a thousand horseshoes. The steel that armed a legion also shod its horses.

## What I tell myself

That a hold that does not sell to everyone sells to no one. That the day Iron Hold picks a side is the day it stops being a place and starts being a weapon. That the steel is not the quarrel; it is only the metal.

I tell myself that, and some mornings I believe it.

## What I do not tell myself

That nobody dies of what I forge. I have seen the wounds. I have seen the greaves, bent by something I would have been proud to have made.

## What I do

I keep a ledger. In it I record every sale, the buyer, the quantity and the date. Every year, I add up what has gone to the battlefield and what has gone to the farm. The farm is always greater. This is a comfort. It is also, I understand, a little like counting the loaves to avoid counting the bruises.

## What I would say to you

Do not follow me into the trade unless you can sit across from a customer and say, plainly, "this is for a field, and this is for a fight, and I will not pretend I cannot tell." That is the only honesty the work allows. It is a thin one. I have lived on it for thirty years and have not yet starved.

Thorgrim is the god of promises kept in metal. I find that I make a great many small ones.

Your uncle, who loves you, and is not sure he has answered.

## The short version on the board

I set this down at Karak Dûn because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The guild chalk stub on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Karak Dûn the work of the day sits inside basalt halls and anvil-heat. People eat stout, stone-bread, and roast roots and they talk about a vow hammered where a stranger can hear it the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at Karak Dûn says one thing. A speaker at Ashen Mines camps says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim, whose mark is on every new tool. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

Evidence you can touch beats a speech you can repeat. A mended wall, a card that nobody will take down, a field that will not take the old crop, a song that changes when the singer notices who just walked in. Copy the object. Then copy who benefits if you only believe the short version.

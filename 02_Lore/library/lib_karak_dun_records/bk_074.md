## How this copy is used

I set this down at Karak Dûn because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Anwen Ndlovu of Iron Hold keeps this book in the kitchen at Karak Dûn, where the houses are basalt halls and anvil-heat. The everyday pot already knows stout, stone-bread, and roast roots. What follows is the version you can cook when a vow hammered where a stranger can hear it has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Thorgrim, whose mark is on every new tool. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Shake-House Cakes

*As cooked when Karak Dûn is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- corn meal
- bean paste
- salt
- a hot plate

Cook the cakes dry. Spread the paste. Eat them before the next shake, which is not a joke the locals need you to make.

## Millbrook Rabbit Pot

*As cooked when Karak Dûn is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- one horned rabbit, jointed
- an onion
- a heel of bread
- thyme if the hedge allows
- water and salt

Brown the joints in a little fat. Add onion. Cover with water and the bread so the broth goes thick. Keep it under a boil. Eat with the spoons you have.

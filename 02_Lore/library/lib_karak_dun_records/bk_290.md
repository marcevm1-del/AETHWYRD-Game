## The story told to children

I set this down at Karak Dûn because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Karak Dûn the work of the day sits inside basalt halls and anvil-heat. People eat stout, stone-bread, and roast roots and they talk about a vow hammered where a stranger can hear it the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Karak Dûn says one thing. A speaker at Ashen Mines camps says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim, whose mark is on every new tool. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

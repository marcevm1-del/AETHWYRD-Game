# Thorsday at the Great Forge
*Ulf Hammerhall, a priest-smith of the anvil-shrine, who wishes it noted that he is also a very ordinary smith*

**Shelf:** Chapel notes  
**Region:** Iron Hold  
**In-world date:** 1004 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

Thorsday is the day of the great forging. It is the busiest day in Karak Dûn, and the holiest, and nobody has ever found a way to tell the two apart.

## Before dawn

The hold wakes in the dark. The Furnace-Heart, the living fire at the centre of the hold, has never gone out, and on Thorsday it is stoked a little higher. The glow comes up the halls like the sun, without the weather.

## The procession

Every smith in the hold walks to the great forge, carrying their hearth-stone. It is a long walk and a slow one. Nobody speaks. We carry our stones against our chests, like children, and each of us has a quiet face.

At the forge, the stones are set on a long bench, side by side, and a bell is rung. It is a deep bell, and the stones seem to hum.

## The forging

Every smith makes one thing on Thorsday, and one thing only. It need not be fine. It need not be useful. It must be honest. A nail is a good Thorsday offering. So is a ring. So is a hammer-head, if you can stand the weight of the commitment.

The offerings are laid on the anvil, one by one, and the priest-smith, who is I, says the words. They are not many. They are: *"Made well. Made true. Made to be used."*

## What happens after

The offerings are given away. Not sold: given. A nail goes to a child. A ring goes to a widow. A hammer-head goes to the next smith who needs one. In my experience, the nail is the one that gets kept for forty years.

## A note

The day is not solemn. By noon, the forge is loud with laughing, and by evening it is loud with singing. I think this is what Thorgrim wished. A promise kept in metal is a promise that has been made with pleasure.

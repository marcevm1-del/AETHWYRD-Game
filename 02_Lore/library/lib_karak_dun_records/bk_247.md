## The story told to children

I set this down at Cinderhole because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The reed pen on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Cinderhole the work of the day sits inside ash camps and mine timbers. People eat grit-bread and onion soup and they talk about a fire in the rock that is older than the camp the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at Cinderhole says one thing. A speaker at Cinderhole says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Kaelthas: a lightning spear, kept for courage in a storm. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim for the shift, Morrowyn for the names on the beam. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Ashen Mines camps because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Ashen Mines camps the work of the day sits inside basalt halls and anvil-heat. People eat stout, stone-bread, and roast roots and they talk about a vow hammered where a stranger can hear it the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Ashen Mines camps says one thing. A speaker at Karak Dûn says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Ilvaris: a spindle and stars, kept for fate, and the Status the Guild teaches. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Thorgrim, whose mark is on every new tool. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

Evidence you can touch beats a speech you can repeat. A mended wall, a card that nobody will take down, a field that will not take the old crop, a song that changes when the singer notices who just walked in. Copy the object. Then copy who benefits if you only believe the short version.

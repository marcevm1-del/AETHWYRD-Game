## The short version on the board

I set this down at pirate stilt-towns because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At pirate stilt-towns the work of the day sits inside beacon posts, salt, and tide tables. People eat fried fish, citrus, and tavern gravy and they talk about a harbour tax and a story about a retired cook the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at pirate stilt-towns says one thing. A speaker at Port Goldmere says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

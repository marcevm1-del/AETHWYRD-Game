## The story told to children

I set this down at Port Goldmere because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Port Goldmere the work of the day sits inside beacon posts, salt, and tide tables. People eat fried fish, citrus, and tavern gravy and they talk about a harbour tax and a story about a retired cook the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Port Goldmere says one thing. A speaker at pirate stilt-towns says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Solenne: a golden scale, kept for oaths and the morning light. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Vex before a sailing, Solenne on the customs house. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

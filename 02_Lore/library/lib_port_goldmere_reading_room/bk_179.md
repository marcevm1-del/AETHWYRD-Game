## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at pirate stilt-towns because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The dry orange peel on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At pirate stilt-towns the work of the day sits inside beacon posts, salt, and tide tables. People eat fried fish, citrus, and tavern gravy and they talk about a harbour tax and a story about a retired cook the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at pirate stilt-towns says one thing. A speaker at Port Goldmere says the part that was left on the road between them. The official line is shorter. The household line feeds more people.

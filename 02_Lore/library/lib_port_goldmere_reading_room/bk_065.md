## How this copy is used

I set this down at pirate stilt-towns because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The blue lamp-glass on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Ilyra Lind of Goldmere Coast keeps this book in the kitchen at pirate stilt-towns, where the houses are beacon posts, salt, and tide tables. The everyday pot already knows fried fish, citrus, and tavern gravy. What follows is the version you can cook when a harbour tax and a story about a retired cook has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Vex before a sailing, Solenne on the customs house. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Mayonnaise, and the Argument

*As cooked when pirate stilt-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- an egg yolk
- oil
- a little vinegar
- salt

Whisk the yolk. Add oil by drops until it looks like a mistake, then in a thread. Vinegar and salt. Serve it with fried fish or be prepared to defend your character.

In Goldmere Coast, people argue about this one. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse. Write your correction in the margin. A clean cookbook is a cookbook nobody cooks from.

## Peat Eel with Berries

*As cooked when pirate stilt-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- two small eels
- bog-berries
- rye crust
- a pinch of pepper

Coil the eel in a low pan. Crush the berries over it. Set the crust on top and bake at the edge of the fire until the crust sounds hollow.

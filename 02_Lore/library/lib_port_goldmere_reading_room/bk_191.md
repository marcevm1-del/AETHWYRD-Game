## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Port Goldmere because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Port Goldmere the work of the day sits inside beacon posts, salt, and tide tables. People eat fried fish, citrus, and tavern gravy and they talk about a harbour tax and a story about a retired cook the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Port Goldmere says one thing. A speaker at pirate stilt-towns says the part that was left on the road between them. One of them is remembering a person. The other is remembering a rule.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street.

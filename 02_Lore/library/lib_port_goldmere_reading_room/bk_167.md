## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at pirate stilt-towns because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The spare button on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At pirate stilt-towns the work of the day sits inside beacon posts, salt, and tide tables. People eat fried fish, citrus, and tavern gravy and they talk about a harbour tax and a story about a retired cook the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at pirate stilt-towns says one thing. A speaker at Port Goldmere says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Vex: a coin with two heads, kept for luck, roads, and the laugh you did not plan. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Vex before a sailing, Solenne on the customs house. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

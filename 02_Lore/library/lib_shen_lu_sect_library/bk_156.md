## The short version on the board

I set this down at Haneul gate-towns because the shelf copy had been edited by someone with a tidy mind and a bad memory. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Haneul gate-towns the work of the day sits inside gate-towns under a high pass. People eat buckwheat, chili, and broth and they talk about the wind at the Red Gate the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a reeve's paper. A speaker at Haneul gate-towns says one thing. A speaker at Haneul says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Kaelthas on the wall, Solenne in the oath. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

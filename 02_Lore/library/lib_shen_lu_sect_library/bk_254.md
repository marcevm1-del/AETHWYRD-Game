## The story told to children

I set this down at sect foothill towns because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At sect foothill towns the work of the day sits inside tiled courtyards and ink-stone. People eat dumplings, tea, and winter greens and they talk about a sect rule and a family rule that disagree the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of children. A speaker at sect foothill towns says one thing. A speaker at Shen-Lu says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Kaelthas: a lightning spear, kept for courage in a storm. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. a quiet chapel with no single patron. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

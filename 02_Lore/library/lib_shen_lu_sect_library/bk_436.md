# Read This at Haneul gate-towns

*Dugan Cho of Haneul*

**Shelf:** Stories for reading aloud  
**Region:** Haneul  
**In-world date:** 976 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.
## For reading aloud

These are told at Haneul gate-towns. They are not sermons. If a child corrects the ending, write the correction in. That is how a story stays a household thing.

## The Guest Who Stayed

In a vale of orchards, a guest took a room and did not speak of leaving. The host set a third plate every night and did not ask the question that would turn a person into a reason. Years are not the same as a trap. Sometimes they are a kitchen that learned another appetite.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## The Reset Stair

Students in the upper Labyrinth marked a door and found it gone the next week. They were furious, and then they were careful. A place that forgets your chalk still remembers your habit, if you have one. They learned the habit.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## Bread on the Scale

Solenne’s scale in a market was used as a weight for bread, which offended a visiting priest. The baker said justice that cannot see a loaf is too tall for this street. The priest bought a loaf, which did not settle the theology and did settle lunch.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## The Unsent Letter

A runner carried a letter for three regions and then was told it had been unsent on purpose. The words were true. They were not owed to the person named on the front. The runner was paid anyway, because the walking was real.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## Mayonnaise

Two taverns on the same road served fried fish. One had the strange sauce the Veilborn brought, and one did not. A feud lasted a summer. In autumn both kitchens were busy, and the feud had become a menu. Nobody won. Everybody ate.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## The Lamp and the Window

A town lit its streets with cores and then scolded the dark for staying in the fields. A child carried one lamp to the gate and left it there, not to own the night, but so the person coming home could find the latch. In the morning the lamp was empty and the child was not praised. The road, however, had been used.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

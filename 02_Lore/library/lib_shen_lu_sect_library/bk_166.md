## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Haneul gate-towns because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Haneul gate-towns the work of the day sits inside gate-towns under a high pass. People eat buckwheat, chili, and broth and they talk about the wind at the Red Gate the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Haneul gate-towns says one thing. A speaker at Haneul says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Solenne: a golden scale, kept for oaths and the morning light. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Kaelthas on the wall, Solenne in the oath. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

Evidence you can touch beats a speech you can repeat. A mended wall, a card that nobody will take down, a field that will not take the old crop, a song that changes when the singer notices who just walked in. Copy the object. Then copy who benefits if you only believe the short version.

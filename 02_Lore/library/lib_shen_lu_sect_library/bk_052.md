## How this copy is used

I set this down at Haneul gate-towns because the Guild card was right about the danger and wrong about the cause. The chipped scale-weight on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Maren Sato of Haneul keeps this book in the kitchen at Haneul gate-towns, where the houses are gate-towns under a high pass. The everyday pot already knows buckwheat, chili, and broth. What follows is the version you can cook when the wind at the Red Gate has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Kaelthas on the wall, Solenne in the oath. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Canal Fish with Peel

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a river fish
- citrus peel
- oil
- salt
- a green herb

Score the fish. Rub it with oil, salt, and peel. Grill it over charcoal, not core-flame, or the skin tastes of lamp.

## Cedar Rain Rice

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- rice
- grilled fish
- pickles
- tea

Rinse the rice until the water is almost clear. Steam it. Lay the fish beside, not on top, so the rice stays its own thing. Pickles at the edge.

## Ink-Stone Dumplings

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- dumpling wrappers
- greens
- a little meat or mushroom
- tea

Fill the wrappers less than you want to. Boil them until they float and then a little longer. Tea after, not with, if you care about the broth.

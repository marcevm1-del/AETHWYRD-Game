## How this copy is used

I set this down at Haneul gate-towns because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The wooden spoon on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Safiya Okeke of Haneul keeps this book in the kitchen at Haneul gate-towns, where the houses are gate-towns under a high pass. The everyday pot already knows buckwheat, chili, and broth. What follows is the version you can cook when the wind at the Red Gate has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Kaelthas on the wall, Solenne in the oath. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Yurt Smoke Meat

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- a strip of dried meat
- salted milk
- flat stone bread

Soften the meat in the milk over a low fire. Do not boil the milk. Fold it into the bread. This is a riding meal, not a feast.

## March Barley Soup

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- barley
- a bone if you have one
- hard cheese
- water

Boil the barley longer than you think. Grate the cheese in at the end so it becomes a line, not a lump.

## How this copy is used

I set this down at Haneul gate-towns because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The hammer-mark stamp on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Imani Ibarra of Haneul keeps this book in the kitchen at Haneul gate-towns, where the houses are gate-towns under a high pass. The everyday pot already knows buckwheat, chili, and broth. What follows is the version you can cook when the wind at the Red Gate has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Kaelthas on the wall, Solenne in the oath. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Giant-Step Beans

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- beans
- smoked chili
- plantain
- water

Soak the beans. Cook them with the chili until the broth is red-brown. Fry the plantain to the side.

## Peat Eel with Berries

*As cooked when Haneul gate-towns is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- two small eels
- bog-berries
- rye crust
- a pinch of pepper

Coil the eel in a low pan. Crush the berries over it. Set the crust on top and bake at the edge of the fire until the crust sounds hollow.

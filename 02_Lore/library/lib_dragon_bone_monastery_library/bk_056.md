## How this copy is used

I set this down at Heavenfolk eyries because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The reed pen on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Haru Brooks of Heavenreach keeps this book in the kitchen at Heavenfolk eyries, where the houses are plateau grass and thin air. The everyday pot already knows yogurt, herbs, and roast grain. What follows is the version you can cook when a sky that feels closer than the nearest town has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Solenne at dawn, because dawn is most of the day. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Adobe Noon Bread

*As cooked when Heavenfolk eyries is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- flour
- a little oil
- dates
- salt

Make a soft dough. Bake it on a hot plate. Split it and put the dates in while it is too hot for your fingers. That is the point.

In Heavenreach, people argue about this one. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse. Write your correction in the margin. A clean cookbook is a cookbook nobody cooks from.

## Gold-Road Millet

*As cooked when Heavenfolk eyries is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- millet
- a spice you can name
- stew meat or squash
- salt

Steam the millet. Stew the rest until it gives. Serve them touching, not stirred, so a guest can refuse the meat and still be fed.

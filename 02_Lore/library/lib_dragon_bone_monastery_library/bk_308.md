## The story told to children

I set this down at Heavenfolk eyries because the shrine keeper and the miller had stopped speaking, and the road still needed both. The tea-stained map on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Heavenfolk eyries the work of the day sits inside plateau grass and thin air. People eat yogurt, herbs, and roast grain and they talk about a sky that feels closer than the nearest town the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of swept stone. A speaker at Heavenfolk eyries says one thing. A speaker at Heavenreach says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Kaelthas: a lightning spear, kept for courage in a storm. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Solenne at dawn, because dawn is most of the day. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

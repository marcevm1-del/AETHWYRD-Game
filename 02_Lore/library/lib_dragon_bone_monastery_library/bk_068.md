## How this copy is used

I set this down at Heavenfolk eyries because I had walked the route twice and the second time the first notes looked vain. The chipped scale-weight on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

Bramble Diallo of Heavenreach keeps this book in the kitchen at Heavenfolk eyries, where the houses are plateau grass and thin air. The everyday pot already knows yogurt, herbs, and roast grain. What follows is the version you can cook when a sky that feels closer than the nearest town has made the market short, and when you still mean to sit down.

A note on faith, because someone always asks: Solenne at dawn, because dawn is most of the day. None of these dishes require a blessing to be edible. A blessing is for the table, after the salt is in.

Prices move. A copper is still the coin of a small bite, and a silver is still a bed. If a stall quotes you a mithril piece for supper, you are in the wrong story or the wrong street.

## Terrace Potatoes

*As cooked when Heavenfolk eyries is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- potatoes
- chili
- herb salt
- oil

Boil, then crush with oil. Chili and herb salt at the end. If the terrace lost a row this year, use fewer potatoes and do not apologize with a speech.

## Mayonnaise, and the Argument

*As cooked when Heavenfolk eyries is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- an egg yolk
- oil
- a little vinegar
- salt

Whisk the yolk. Add oil by drops until it looks like a mistake, then in a thread. Vinegar and salt. Serve it with fried fish or be prepared to defend your character.

## Peat Eel with Berries

*As cooked when Heavenfolk eyries is being ordinary.*

**You need**

- two small eels
- bog-berries
- rye crust
- a pinch of pepper

Coil the eel in a low pan. Crush the berries over it. Set the crust on top and bake at the edge of the fire until the crust sounds hollow.

## Morning, before the door is public

I set this down at Heavenfolk eyries because three neighbors told the same story with three different weather reports. The blue lamp-glass on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Heavenfolk eyries the work of the day sits inside plateau grass and thin air. People eat yogurt, herbs, and roast grain and they talk about a sky that feels closer than the nearest town the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of the person who lights the lamp. A speaker at Heavenfolk eyries says one thing. A speaker at Heavenreach says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Maelis: a crescent and an eye, kept for dreams and the silver moon. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Solenne at dawn, because dawn is most of the day. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

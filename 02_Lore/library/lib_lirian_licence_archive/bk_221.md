# Notes on Mending a Bridge
*Odalys Brickwright, mason of the Truce Bridge, to the masons who come after*

**Shelf:** Craft notes  
**Region:** Moraveil  
**In-world date:** 1014 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.

A bridge is a promise made of stone. I know that sounds grand. It is also a plain fact, and it is the first thing to learn.

## What the bridge is

A single arch of grey stone over the river, wide enough for two carts and a goat. On one end, the goblin village, a cliff of caves and ladders. On the other, the market town, a clutter of roofs. In the middle, the council table, where the joint council meets once a month, and where, on Truce Day, we hold the feast.

## What goes wrong

Frost. Always frost. Water gets into the joints and freezes, and a frozen joint is a joint that opens. Every spring, I walk the bridge from end to end and tap every stone with the back of my trowel. A sound stone rings. A cracked one gives a dull little *tock*, like a tooth being tapped.

## What I do

I rake out the old mortar to the depth of a thumb. I wet the joint, so that the new mortar does not drown. I press it in with the heel of my hand and leave it to set for a week, covered, in the shade. It is not exciting. It is the most important work in Moraveil.

## What I do not do

I do not replace a stone unless I must. An old stone has seen a good deal. It has been walked on by both peoples. It has the shape of both. I would sooner patch it than put in a new one that has never carried anything.

## The tradition

On the first day of work every spring, a goblin and a human stand together at the middle of the bridge. They each lay a hand on the same stone. They say nothing. Then they get on with it.

I do not know who began that. I know that it has never been missed.

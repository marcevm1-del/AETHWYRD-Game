## The week as people actually live it

I set this down at Moraveil market town because a child asked a straight question and the adults answered with a proverb. The folded tide-table on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Moraveil market town the work of the day sits inside market awnings and goblin-mended stalls. People eat pepper stew and fried bread and they talk about a bargain that changes if you blink the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a household. A speaker at Moraveil market town says one thing. A speaker at Moraveil goblin village says the part that was left on the road between them. Both saw the same hour. They were standing on different banks.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Thorgrim: a hammer on a mountain, kept for the forge and a promise kept in metal. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. Vex's dice on the counter. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## Days, without a whip

The week runs Solenday, Maelsday, Thorsday, Sylvasday, Kaelsday, Morrowsday, Vexday. Ilvaris has no day. People notice, and they do not all explain it the same way. You can miss a festival and still be a neighbor. You cannot miss a promised watch and call it piety.

Status Appraisal Day comes at twelve. It is a festival and it is a sorting, and adults who joke through it are remembering their own. Bring the honey cakes before the reading. Afterward, walk the child home whether the sheet was kind or not.

The Lamp Vigil is for the dead, with offerings and a light that is not a dare. Eat the biscuits before the lighting. Speak the names you have a right to speak. A red moon in the sky is a thing sailors mention without raising their voice. This book does not explain it. The night is already full.

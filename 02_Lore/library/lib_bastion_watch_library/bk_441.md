# Read This at Bastion outer ring

*Safiya Lind of Bastion*

**Shelf:** Stories for reading aloud  
**Region:** Bastion  
**In-world date:** 981 SC  
**Status:** Draft. Player-facing. Not Canon until reviewed. Written as what a person in Aethwyrd could know.
## For reading aloud

These are told at Bastion outer ring. They are not sermons. If a child corrects the ending, write the correction in. That is how a story stays a household thing.

## The Wyvern’s Bowl

A wyvern came to a ridge kitchen and was offered a chain with the meal. It left the meal. The next night the chain was gone from the offer and the bowl remained. The wyvern ate, and the cook kept both hands on the table where the guest could see them. Trust, the story says, is a kind of posture.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## The Pass That Was a Gap

Travelers demanded a mountain. The people who lived there showed them a fortified gap and a kettle. The travelers were angry to have carried ropes for a hill. They were alive, which was the point of the ropes, and also of the kettle.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## Honey Before the Reading

On the day the children’s sheets were read, a baker handed out cakes first. A clerk said the cakes would spoil the solemnity. The baker said the solemnity would still be there in an hour, and the children would still be children if someone had fed them.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## The Coin with Two Heads

Vex’s shrine at the crossroad has a coin that seems to cheat. A merchant flipped it to avoid a choice. It landed, and the merchant still had to choose, because a coin is not a porter. The laugh from the shrine was not cruel. It was the sound of a lesson sitting down.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## Stones for the Anvil

An apprentice wanted to make a sword on the first day. The smith set out nails. The apprentice made forty nails and understood, by the thirty-first, that a promise can be small and still be a promise. The sword came three years later. It was better for the nails.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

## The Guest Who Stayed

In a vale of orchards, a guest took a room and did not speak of leaving. The host set a third plate every night and did not ask the question that would turn a person into a reason. Years are not the same as a trap. Sometimes they are a kitchen that learned another appetite.

Question you can ask afterward, and then drop: who in the story had to do something small on purpose?

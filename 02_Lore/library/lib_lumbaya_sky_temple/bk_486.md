## The week as people actually live it

I set this down at Lumbaya temple villages because the Guild card was right about the danger and wrong about the cause. The tea-stained map on the table is not a relic. It is how this copy stayed honest.

At Lumbaya temple villages the work of the day sits inside vine over old giant-stone. People eat plantain, beans, and smoked chili and they talk about a stair that was not built for human legs the way coastal towns talk about weather: often, and with corrections.

This chapter listens by way of a household. A speaker at Lumbaya temple villages says one thing. A speaker at Lumbaya says the part that was left on the road between them. Neither is lying. Each left out the part that made their own choice look worse.

The shrine mark nearby belongs to Morrowyn: a lantern with a blue flame, kept for the dead, and the living who remember them. That is public devotion, the kind you can see from the street. stories of builders, told as stories. I am not writing what a god intends. I am writing what the keeper swept this morning.

## Days, without a whip

The week runs Solenday, Maelsday, Thorsday, Sylvasday, Kaelsday, Morrowsday, Vexday. Ilvaris has no day. People notice, and they do not all explain it the same way. You can miss a festival and still be a neighbor. You cannot miss a promised watch and call it piety.

Status Appraisal Day comes at twelve. It is a festival and it is a sorting, and adults who joke through it are remembering their own. Bring the honey cakes before the reading. Afterward, walk the child home whether the sheet was kind or not.

The Lamp Vigil is for the dead, with offerings and a light that is not a dare. Eat the biscuits before the lighting. Speak the names you have a right to speak. A red moon in the sky is a thing sailors mention without raising their voice. This book does not explain it. The night is already full.

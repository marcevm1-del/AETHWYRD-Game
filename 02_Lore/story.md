# 02c — Story: Main Plot, Villains, Endings

**Core theme:** *Save the world, and decide what it's worth saving it from.* The Weave is really unraveling. Every faction has its own answer to how to save it, and every answer costs something.

**Tone:** hopeful, warm, sincere. Joy comes from found family, festivals, small kindnesses and earned victories, not slapstick. **No fanservice.** The dark moments hit harder because the happy ones feel real.

Tone curve: **warm, funny and adventurous (Acts I–II) → political and tragic (III–IV) → cosmic and personal (V–VI).** Choices are tracked as **Story Flags** and change scenes, companions and endings.

**Theme:** war, sacrifice, understanding your enemy. **Structure:** fully **open**, tackle the Generals in any order. Each one you defeat changes the others' tactics.

| General | Region | What you learn | Big choice |
|---|---|---|---|

| Example | Why it works | What we take |
|---|---|---|

## The Red Prophet
### The face you know: **Brother Caspian**
- A gentle, **blindfolded monk** who runs the **Brindlewick Orphanage of the Small Lantern**.
- **He gives you your first meal in this world** (Transported Origin: after Lyra brings you to town, Caspian feeds you stew and gives you a blanket; other Origins meet him in Act I).
- He teaches the orphans songs, tends Morrowyn's shrine, and asks you to bring the children sweets. Everyone loves him.

### The truth: **Malgareth Vey**, the First Demon Lord
- The Valdrisian archmage who caused the **Night of Unweaving** 3,000 years ago **never died**. He survives by **moving from body to body**. His hosts' eyes turn crimson, which is why Caspian wears a blindfold.
- "**Malchior Vane, the Red Prophet**" is the masked name he preaches under. Caspian is just his current host.

### The cosmic twist: **Vesper, the red moon, IS Valdrisia**
- The Night of Unweaving didn't just destroy Valdrisia. It **tore the capital city into the sky**. The red moon Vesper is the lost empire: a city of trapped souls, glowing red with their Aether.
- When Vesper is full, the souls press closest to the world. That's why monsters grow stronger.
- **Malgareth's sermon:** *"The gods harvest us! Follow me and the red moon will set us free!"*
- **Malgareth's real goal:** keep Vesper **full forever** so he can **devour the entire moon**, three thousand years of souls, and ascend as the **only** harvester. He isn't fighting the gods' harvest. He wants it for himself.
- **The orphans** are being raised as his **next host bodies**.

### Why he can't be redeemed
He burns villages to "feed the moon," he grooms children as vessels, and he enjoys every lie. His final monologue: *"Your gods eat souls with apologies. I simply skip the apology."*

### How it connects
| Thread | Link |
|---|---|
| **Morthis** (General of Rot) | Her lost people are on Vesper. She works with Azrael because she believes he'll bring them back. When she learns Malgareth plans to eat them, she can turn on the Cult (redemption path) |
| **Azrael** | Azrael *uses* the Red Moon Cult as allies of convenience and despises Malgareth |
| **Q63: Malgareth alive** | Defeating the Red Prophet in Act V kills Caspian's body. Malgareth flees **into Vesper** |
| **Post-game region** | **Vesper, the Drowned Sky-City**: travel to the red moon (via the Titan Sky-Gate or SSS Waystride; never by mount) and fight Malgareth in the ruined capital of Valdrisia among three thousand years of ghosts |

### Foreshadowing (visible on a second playthrough)
1. Caspian's orphan songs are in **Old Weave-tongue**. Translate them later: they're hymns to the red moon.
2. The orphanage is "closed for prayer" every **Blood Vesper** night.
3. Whiskers the cat (Vex) **refuses to enter** the orphanage.
4. Caspian always knows where you are, even though he's "blind."
5. Orphans who "were adopted" are never seen again. Their names appear on a cult ledger in Act II.
6. In Act I, the red-eyed hobgoblin's runes match the stitching on Caspian's blindfold.
7. Caspian is the only NPC who **never comments on your rank**: *"Rank is a cage, child."*
8. The System Voice, in its *Fractured* personality, glitches when he's nearby.

**Reveal moment (Act V):** the Lantern Night festival in Brindlewick. The orphans sing, Vesper rises full, and Caspian removes his blindfold for the first time. Crimson eyes. *"Thank you for bringing them sweets. They'll need their strength."*
Players who solve it early (Mystery Board) can **save the orphans before the reveal**, which gives a unique ending variation and a title.

## Lady Nocturne's Stolen Face
### **Mina Feathersby**, the guild receptionist
- From **early Act III**, the Mina who congratulates you at rank-ups is **Lady Nocturne**, the Veiled Mirror, the fifth Demon General.
- The **real Mina** (ex-A-Rank) was captured on a "vacation to Port Goldmere" between Acts II and III and is imprisoned beneath the Imperium of Vharlen.

### Clues (fair, discoverable)
| Clue | Where |
|---|---|
| "Mina" forgets the inside joke from your first registration | Act III rank-up |
| She now writes with her **left hand** | Guild counter |
| **Whiskers hisses** at her | Guild hall |
| She stops fainting at your absurd feats ("too composed") | Any feat event |
| Her tea order changes | The Sleeping Griffon |
| Her rank-up speech for B-Rank uses a word only Vharlen nobles use | B ceremony |
| **"Nobody"** (Mina's sister, the Syndicate leader) contacts you: *"That isn't my sister."* | Rogue questline |

### The twist inside the twist
- Nocturne has worn a hundred faces and has **no face of her own**. As Mina, she was loved for the first time: by you, the guild, the rookies.
- At her reveal she's furious that she's begun to *feel* like Mina. Her boss fight takes place in a **hall of mirrors** where she cycles through every face she's stolen (including people you've met).
- Rescue the real Mina, and the reunion is one of the warmest scenes in the game. Whether Nocturne is destroyed or simply loses every face she ever wore is your choice. She stays **irredeemable**, but her end can be tragic.
- **Rank-up ceremonies during the fake-Mina period** get subtle alternate dialogue. On a replay they're chilling.

### Peaceful afterstory
After an ending that leaves the world able to continue, the player can return to free-roam after the epilogue. The central crisis is resolved; the ongoing life is exploration, hunting, companions, crafting, farming, shopkeeping, festivals and regional recovery. Optional contracts and discoveries remain, and the player can revisit important people and places. New adventures can be added as optional story arcs, without making another world-ending threat mandatory.

Endings that transform or end the playable world receive their authored epilogue state instead of an incompatible free-roam continuation. The afterstory does not add an ending or change the existing 11 + 1 ending count.

## Mystery Design Principles
Based on how lore-driven games build mysteries:
- **Environment first:** ruins, murals, item descriptions and ghost memories tell the story; a ruined library tells you scholars lived there and studied what destroyed them. ([FromSoftware storytelling](https://amrsalehduat.substack.com/p/how-fromsoftware-tells-stories-without))
- **Players as detectives:** discovery gives players ownership of the story. ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/reclaiming-narrative-reviving-environmental-storytelling))
- **Two layers:** clear enough for casual players, deep enough for dedicated ones to keep finding meaning. ([Pixune](https://pixune.com/blog/environmental-storytelling-in-games/))
- Environmental storytelling as "arranging objects so they suggest a story." ([IntechOpen, quoting Henry Jenkins](https://www.intechopen.com/online-first/1225186))
- **Applied:** each Great Mystery has 10–30 scattered clues, a **Mystery Board** in the Journal (pin clues, connect them), and the truth can be solved **before** the story reveals it, for a unique reward and a special dialogue.
